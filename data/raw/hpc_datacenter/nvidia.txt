										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI agents do more than generate answers. They search for information, query databases, write and test code and take action. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Each of those steps relies on a CPU, with agents moving between GPU reasoning and CPU execution throughout a task. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As businesses put more agents to work, AI factories need CPUs that can execute those steps quickly and support many agents at once, all within the limits of available power.</span></p>
<p><b>What this means:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> AI infrastructure providers need to support more agents without making each one wait longer to get work done. And critically, AI factories are power limited. This means operators need to choose infrastructure that maximizes the work done for every watt. </span></p>
<p><b>Why it matters:</b> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-cpu/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> delivers both fast agent performance and high capacity per watt within a single CPU. That gives providers one CPU for a range of agent workloads, rather than having to mix and match fast and dense x86 parts to balance speed and capacity.</span></p>
<p><b>The data that proves it:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> In a 100-megawatt data center comparison, NVIDIA found that a single Vera CPU performs better than the fastest x86 CPU and the most energy-efficient x86 CPU.</span></p>
<ul>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vera delivers industry-leading agent speed while enabling </span><b>1.6x more agents per watt </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">versus the fastest x86 CPU.</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vera delivers </span><b>2x faster agent speed</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with leading energy efficiency compared with the most energy-efficient x86 CPU.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Partners are already putting Vera to work. Early adopters see the same exceptional performance in production-style tests:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://www.perplexity.ai/en-GB/hub/blog/secure-sandboxes-for-agents"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Perplexity</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Ran agentic coding tasks 1.5x faster with Vera than on traditional CPUs. Perplexity plans to deploy Vera in an upcoming production system.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://deepinfra.com/blog/nvidia-vera-cpu-benchmark"><span style="font-weight: 400;">DeepInfra</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Delivered up to 2.2x speed of the best x86 processor on agentic orchestration for its production agent workload, achieving the fastest of the four architectures tested.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://clickhouse.com/blog/clickhouse-performance-on-nvidia-vera"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ClickHouse</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Its standard binary, with no Vera-specific tuning, scaled near-linearly across 176 cores and topped the ClickBench hardware leaderboard.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://duckdb.org/2026/10/16/nvidia-vera-benchmark"><span style="font-weight: 400;">DuckLabs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Achieved about 1.5x the median TPC-H SF1000 performance of an Intel Xeon 6 baseline.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://www.daytona.io/dotfiles/when-2000-agents-share-one-cpu"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Daytona</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Brought 1,000 agent sandboxes online in 10.8 seconds, and achieved 12.7 completed jobs per second at 2,000 concurrent sandboxes, using Vera versus 88.4 seconds with x86.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://modal.com/blog/nvidia-vera-sandboxes"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Modal</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Achieved up to 2x sandbox throughput per core versus comparable x86 hardware, with gains that grow under heavy load.</span></li>
</ul>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Turning a simulation idea into a working application means assembling assets, connecting physics and rendering, and checking that the scene behaves as intended. Developers are combining frontier AI models with</span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to help carry out that work — building applications for exploring scenarios, investigating failures and improving designs.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers direct AI agents through natural-language instructions, review results and guide changes. Omniverse libraries provide GPU-accelerated physics, rendering and sensor simulation capabilities.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore the projects below to see frontier AI models such as GPT-6 Astra at work, and check back for new examples from NVIDIA teams and developers across the ecosystem.</span></p>
<hr />
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="editable-assets" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Create Editable 3D Assets With Houdini and AI Agents </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#editable-assets"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Creating animated 3D content requires connecting modeling, simulation, materials and rendering. Nick Scarcella, a senior technical artist on NVIDIA’s BondLab team, directed AI agents — primarily using Claude — to work with CUDA libraries and Isaac Sim to create animations. The BondLab team uses</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.sidefx.com/products/houdini/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">SideFX Houdini</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and the</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA ovrtx library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to develop materials, lighting and the final rendered appearance.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In one experiment, Scarcella guided Astra to recreate an OP-1 portable synthesizer using product images, manuals and technical direction. He exported the model into the</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/openusd/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenUSD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> framework, tested it in Houdini and Blender, then used Astra and Opus to refine details before adjusting materials and textures for ovrtx rendering.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Scarcella also used a publicly shared robot-hand checkpoint in Isaac Sim, directing the motion and developing materials and lighting in Houdini with Claude’s assistance. Working with team member Steven Dvornik, the team created an “Echos” visualization: translucent versions of the hand overlap and merge into a single hand animation, offering a visual interpretation of robot training.</span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-1" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Robot_Hand_Video_Omniverse.mp4?_=1" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Robot_Hand_Video_Omniverse.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Robot_Hand_Video_Omniverse.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Figure: An artistic visualization of simulated robot-hand motion, developed with AI assistance and Houdini’s materials and lighting tools.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These workflows bring agent-assisted creation into existing artist tools, giving teams editable content they can inspect, refine and render across applications.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore Scarcella’s</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nscrdev/nscr-houdini-mcp"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Houdini MCP tools and agent skill</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>

<h2 id="robotic-disassembly" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Test Robotic Disassembly With Computer-Aided Design and Simulation </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#robotic-disassembly"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before a robot can disassemble a product, developers need to know whether its tools can reach and remove the parts. Jens Jebens, a senior product manager for OpenUSD at NVIDIA, directed Astra to model a car suspension in PTC Onshape and configure it in <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/sim/">NVIDIA</a></span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Isaac Sim</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Jebens used simulation feedback to revise the tooling, including a wrench designed to reach the suspension’s bolts, and reported successful removal of a suspension component in simulation.</span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-2" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Robotic_Dissasembly_Omniverse.mp4?_=2" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Robotic_Dissasembly_Omniverse.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Robotic_Dissasembly_Omniverse.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">PTC also </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/computer-aided-engineering/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">connects computer-aided design</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (CAD) with simulation through Onshape’s SimReady Publisher, built with NVIDIA Omniverse libraries. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://sensorirobotics.com/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Robotics startup Sensori</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> uses it to transfer its Yuri robot into Isaac Sim, carrying geometry, joints and configuration data so the team can evaluate design changes without repeatedly rebuilding the simulation model.</span></p>
<p><em><a target="_blank" href="https://www.onshape.com/en/blog/robotics-startup-sensori-cloud-native-cad-nvidia-isaac-sim-physical-ai"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Read Sensori’s story</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>
<p><em><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/extensions/latest/ext_onshape.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore the Onshape importer guide.</span></a></em></p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="building-model" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Create an Editable Building Model From Site Scans </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#building-model"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-3" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/relay-video-v14-1080p.mp4?_=3" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/relay-video-v14-1080p.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/relay-video-v14-1080p.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Planning warehouse automation requires a model of the building itself, but shelving and machinery can obscure its structure in site scans. Omniverse brings together real-to-sim tools and agent skills to help developers turn real-world captures into editable starting environments for physical AI simulation.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">David Wehr, from NVIDIA’s simulation technology team, built on that work using Astra to develop a workflow for digitally reconstructing a building without its contents. The goal is an editable, simulation-ready building model where developers can add or reposition shelving and machinery to evaluate warehouse layouts and automation plans before making physical changes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Wehr guided Astra to interpret panoramic photos, create building geometry and align it with point-cloud measurements. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/openusd/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenUSD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> stores the models, while </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse’s ovrtx library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> renders results for inspection.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse agent skills</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to help agents build with Omniverse libraries.</span></em></p>
<hr />
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="gas-turbine" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Test Gas-Turbine Operating Scenarios With a Digital Twin </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#gas-turbine"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-4" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4?_=4" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before changing how industrial equipment operates, engineers need a way to explore the consequences. Pradeep Kaushik, a member of the NVIDIA Omniverse developer relations team, directed Astra to build a what-if analysis application around a simulated gas turbine and generator.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The application lets users adjust conditions such as load, ambient temperature and fuel composition, then compare power, efficiency, fuel consumption and equipment response. Six test procedures explore scenarios including load changes, a stuck fuel valve and an exhaust-sensor error.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The prototype gives engineers an interactive way to investigate how operating changes and faults could affect equipment behavior, supporting decisions about which scenarios warrant further testing.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Astra used tools like Cantera for thermochemistry and SciPy for equipment dynamics. The application maps solver results to rotor animation and color-coded temperature, pressure and flow-velocity indicators in a 3D turbine. NVIDIA Omniverse libraries update the scene (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovstage"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovstage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), render it with RTX (ovrtx) and stream the view to the browser (ovstream). Engineers can rotate and zoom the cutaway, switch between indicators, and pause or scrub through a simulated response alongside charts and virtual instruments.</span></p>

<h2 id="simready-assets" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Build Simulation Scenes With Open Source SimReady Assets </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#simready-assets"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-5" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4?_=5" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With SimReady Central Library, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-central-assets-robotics-ai-factories"><span style="font-weight: 400;">announced at GTC Berlin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, developers can access more than 2,000 simulation-ready USD assets from equipment manufacturers and asset providers for robotics simulations and AI factory digital twins workflows.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Paul Kind, a member of NVIDIA’s Omniverse documentation team, pointed an AI agent to the library and guided scene assembly through conversation. His kitchen scene included 180 placements drawn from 63 unique library assets, alongside 18 custom geometry pieces. Kind reviewed renders produced with Isaac Sim and guided further adjustments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For robotics and factory workflows, developers can select SimReady assets with the simulation properties embedded to fit their required task then test in an assembled environment.</span></p>
<p><em><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/nvidia/simready-central"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Find assets in SimReady Central Library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-central-assets-robotics-ai-factories#prepare-and-contribute-content"><span style="font-weight: 400;">learn how to contribute</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>

<h2 id="warehouse" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Stress-Test a Warehouse Simulation at Scale </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#warehouse"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-6" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4?_=6" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A warehouse simulation needs to keep running smoothly as more robot arms, conveyors and boxes fill the scene. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When it slows down, developers need to identify what’s holding it back. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bhavik Sanganee, principal engineer at NVIDIA, used Astra with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse?size=n_12_n&amp;sort-field=featured&amp;sort-direction=desc"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to build a warehouse simulation, increase its complexity and investigate slowdowns.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Performance first fell below the target when the scene doubled in size. Running physics calculations, image rendering and video preparation concurrently restored performance and enabled further scaling.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With 80 conveyors, 40 arms and 800 boxes, the simulation sustained 30 frames per second at full HD resolution during a two-minute test. The arms completed 640 successful box placements.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Doubling the scene again reduced performance. Testing traced the slowdown to the physics and robot-control pipeline, giving Sanganee a specific area to improve.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start building with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse?size=n_12_n&amp;sort-field=featured&amp;sort-direction=desc"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>

<h2 id="cable-routing" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Test Robot Control for Flexible Cable Routing </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#cable-routing"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-7" width="1200" height="406" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4?_=7" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In electronics assembly, a robot may need to position a flexible cable around components before plugging it into a port. The cable bends as the robot moves it, and the connector must line up precisely to fit.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Oyindamola Omotuyi, robotics technical marketing manager at NVIDIA, directed Astra to build a robot simulation using </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> physics and Omniverse rendering with a</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K2ui16FdLZw"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Robotiq gripper</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> handling the cable.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Astra also developed a</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/reinforcement-learning/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">reinforcement learning</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> system that combined learned cable-routing movements with position-and-orientation feedback and corrective adjustments for USB-C insertion. The simulation gave Omotuyi a way to test cable handling and connector alignment before moving to physical-robot experiments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The controller used information supplied directly by the simulation. Next steps include testing a friction-held grasp and using information a physical robot could obtain from cameras and sensors.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Get started with robot learning in </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>

<h2 id="cad" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Turn Robot Computer-Aided Design Into a SimReady Asset </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#cad"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before testing whether a robot can pick up and move a box, developers need a simulation model with joints that move correctly and with grippers that can hold the object. A computer-aided design (CAD) model provides the robot’s shape, but it still needs physics properties to perform the task.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Using an ABB Robotics YuMi robot, developers directed Astra with NVIDIA Omniverse content libraries to convert CAD files into OpenUSD, match the robot’s appearance to reference images, and configure joints and collision geometry. They checked the asset against SimReady Foundation requirements before testing it in NVIDIA Isaac Sim.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Both arms and grippers completed four cube pick-and-place cycles, using simulated finger contact and friction. These tests demonstrated behavior under the simulated conditions — estimated physical properties were not calibrated against the real robot.<br />
</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Follow the five-step </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/5-steps-to-create-simready-assets-for-robotics-with-frontier-ai-models/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SimReady tutorial</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></em></p>

<h2 id="humanoid-simulator" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Build a Humanoid Simulator for a Warehouse Environment </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#humanoid-simulator"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-8" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4?_=8" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore a gamified, physics-based control of a humanoid robot in first- and third-person view.</span></em><i></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before automating warehouse tasks, developers need an interactive simulation environment to explore task behavior and evaluate how the work gets done. Frank DeLise, Omniverse product manager at NVIDIA, used Astra to turn a </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/simready/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SimReady</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> warehouse and humanoid robot into an interactive simulator with first- and third-person views.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DeLise directed Astra to connect</span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for physics (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX/blob/main/ovphysx/README.md"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovphysx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), scene updates (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovstage"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovstage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), rendering (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">) and the user interface (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-omniverse/ovui"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovui</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">). He also used Astra with SimReady (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia/simready-foundation"><span style="font-weight: 400;">simready-foundation</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">) to create the physical scene in simulation. Astra then generated animation and application code to bring those capabilities together.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Learn how to </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/5-steps-to-create-simready-assets-for-robotics-with-frontier-ai-models/"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">prepare and validate SimReady robot assets</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with frontier AI models and NVIDIA Omniverse libraries.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="autonomous-driving-testing" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Connect an Autonomous-Driving Testing Workflow </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#autonomous-driving-testing"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-9" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4?_=9" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Changing a scene, sensor or driving model can significantly affect autonomous-vehicle simulations. Doyub Kim, a manager on the simulation technology team at NVIDIA, asked Astra to build Zero to Alpamayo — a reusable simulation environment based on San Francisco’s Market Street.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kim directed Astra to map out the workflow, then connect asset creation, traffic, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/integrate-nvidia-omniverse-rtx-sensor-simulation-into-existing-apps/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse RTX sensor simulation</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/solutions/autonomous-vehicles/alpamayo/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alpamayo driving</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in stages, checking each integration. The resulting prototype was a testing ground for comparing models and tracing how scene or sensor changes affect downstream driving behavior. A separate Cosmos3-Nano experiment varied weather and lighting in recorded simulation videos, allowing Kim to compare the driving model’s responses to the same scenario under different conditions.</span></p>

<h2 id="sensor-differences" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Use Sensor Differences to Create and Improve Digital Twins </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#sensor-differences"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-10" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4?_=10" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Comparing recorded and simulated camera and lidar outputs gives developers key performance indicator-based feedback for refining a scene.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To test robots and autonomous vehicles, developers need to know how closely simulated sensors match real ones. Ashley Reid, who works on RTX sensor validation at NVIDIA, directed Astra and Claude Fable 5 agents to compare </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> camera and raw LiDAR outputs with recorded data. The agents created two digital twins from scratch and improved two existing ones.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Over about three days, Reid guided an iterative workflow in which agents measured differences, created or modified OpenUSD scenes, and checked the results. Changes addressed missing objects, geometry and materials, with acceptance depending on camera and LiDAR metrics. This gives developers a way to use measured discrepancies to guide scene creation and improvement.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start by rendering an OpenUSD scene with the </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx/tree/main/examples/python/minimal"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx minimal Python example</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, then define a sensor measure to compare with recorded data.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="robo-olympics" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Robo Olympics: Test Robot Skills With Simulation </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#robo-olympics"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Teaching robots new movements means checking whether those actions work under physical constraints. Tae Kim, who leads NVIDIA Omniverse engineering and product, used sports videos and natural-language instructions to guide Astra in building Robo Olympics, an experimental project that tests simulated Unitree G1 humanoids performing sports movements.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Under Kim’s direction, Astra built controllers and refined them through physics trials. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton Physics Engine</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> simulated behavior, the open source </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/warp-python"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Warp</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> framework accelerated calculations and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> rendered scenes and virtual-camera images. In one experiment, the robot cleared a single hurdle in 64 of 100 simulation trials. The trials gave Kim feedback for improving the robot’s timing and control.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Watch Kim </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbwlRzjLwqc"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">explore building simulations</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with Astra and NVIDIA Omniverse libraries.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="international-space-station" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Bring the International Space Station Into the Browser </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#international-space-station"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Turning 3D models into an application requires connecting assets, live data and an interface. Nic Johns, an engineering director at NVIDIA, prompted Astra to assemble NASA assets into an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/openusd/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenUSD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> International Space Station model with telemetry. Johns built the application with a single prompt, then used a follow-up prompt to shift the scene to Earth’s daytime side so the planet was visible.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The workflow used Blender for asset preparation and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for rendering (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), scene runtime (ovstage) and streaming (ovstream). The application brings 3D models and operational data into the browser, with Johns guiding its development through prompts and corrections.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Try the </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA/skills/tree/main/skills/omniverse-realtime-viewer"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse Real-Time Viewer skill</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="testing-environments" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Turn Captured Rooms Into Testing Environments </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#testing-environments"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A digitally reconstructed room needs editable objects and accurate physical behavior before developers can test interactions. The studio connects captured geometry to interaction testing, making gaps and object behavior easier to inspect. </span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400">Chris Alexiuk has a simple rule about open source AI: If you believe in it, be loud about it.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“We should be shouting about this technology from the rooftops,” said Alexiuk, a Toronto-based senior product research engineer in deep learning at NVIDIA who specializes in making open large language models (LLMs) practical for working developers. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Alexiuk’s preferred “rooftop” is <a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL5B692fm6--vEL0FwctKghCpyEnBGAQJA">YouTube</a>, where he’s built a following teaching curious thousands how to build, fine-tune, evaluate and ship production LLM applications and agents. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“I think the people who will most help us understand how to build and apply AI likely haven’t even heard of it yet,” he said. “Trying to get knowledge and information out to as many people as humanly possible is what continues to drive me.”</span></p>
<h2><b>Bringing NVIDIA Nemotron to Developers Everywhere</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Having spent years teaching developers to build with open models, Alexiuk has concluded that transparency isn’t a feature of good AI; it’s the precondition for it.</span><span style="font-weight: 400"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400">“Fully open source AI is necessary for the democratization of intelligence,” he said. “You should have access to the weights, the data that went into it, the training recipes, everything.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">That’s why, on his LinkedIn profile, Alexiuk styles himself “No. 1 Nemotron Fan,” referring to the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400"> family of open source, multimodal models built for long-running, self-evolving and specialized AI agents.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“I think Nemotron is the most important project that exists,” said Alexiuk, who also uses the pseudonym “Joe Nemotron” across social media. “I’m a firm believer that openness is a very important thing to undertake, so I’m going to be the No. 1 fan of whoever is doing that the best. And that is Nemotron.”</span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99663-11" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ChrisAlexiuk_captions_9MB.mp4?_=11" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ChrisAlexiuk_captions_9MB.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ChrisAlexiuk_captions_9MB.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">It’s this matter-of-fact directness and affable approachability that makes Alexiuk’s teaching style so appealing. On the social media platform X, he goes by the handle “llm_wizard.” His avatar: a laughing cartoon caricature sporting a slouchy sorcerer’s hat and brandishing a magic wand. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">It’s no surprise, then, that Alexiuk likens Nemotron to a magical library — its users fellow wizards in search of knowledge that can move mountains.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“You’re a wizard in search of prepared spells, and Nemotron has a spell for everything,” he said. “Just choose the one you need.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">In non-wizard terms?</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“There’s a category of model that’s going to be close to the kind of work you want to accomplish,” Alexiuk said, explaining that Nemotron supports that whole ecosystem of AI users: from individual developers using a model like Nemotron 3.5 Lightning for repetitive, high-frequency execution work to a company deploying Nemotron Ultra for long-running autonomous agents, deep research and enterprise tasks.</span></p>
<figure id="attachment_99666" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-99666" style="width: 1200px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img fetchpriority="high" decoding="async" class="wp-image-99666 size-large" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-act-07-build-a-claw-BVB03323-1-1680x1120.jpg" alt="" width="1200" height="800" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-act-07-build-a-claw-BVB03323-1-1680x1120.jpg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-act-07-build-a-claw-BVB03323-1-960x640.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-act-07-build-a-claw-BVB03323-1-1280x853.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-act-07-build-a-claw-BVB03323-1-1536x1024.jpg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-act-07-build-a-claw-BVB03323-1-scaled.jpg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-act-07-build-a-claw-BVB03323-1-630x420.jpg 630w" sizes="(max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-99666" class="wp-caption-text">NVIDIAN Chris Alexiuk (left) engages with developers during the Build-a-Claw event at GTC Berlin.</figcaption></figure>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“I think that openness helps more people understand AI and hopefully leads to more people doing really ingenious things with these tools at the limit,” Alexiuk said. “If a tool is amplifying skill, it’s amplifying human expression.”</span></p>
<h2><b>From Birding to Model-Building</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Alexiuk first became fascinated with machine learning in a computational physics class at Trent University in Ontario, Canada.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“After that, I learned about TensorFlow and that you could build image classifiers that ran on your GPU,” he recalled. As an initial one-off project, Alexiuk built a classifier that would alert him whenever his friend’s distinctive pet bird, a Yellow-Faced Gray Budgerigar, appeared in frame on his webcam during shared video game sessions.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“Because we were playing video games, I wasn’t focused on my friend’s webcam, but I didn&#8217;t want to miss seeing the bird when it appeared,” Alexiuk said. “And then my whole life changed, because in that instance I realized it’s crazy what you could do with technology.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Since then, he’s been embedding himself in the open source AI community. </span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Larger models, longer contexts and agentic applications are increasing GPU memory pressure while supply tightens and prices rise — a trend expected to continue through the near term.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA works closely with memory partners to rapidly adopt the latest memory technologies. NVIDIA was first to adopt HBM3e and HBM4 technologies into an accelerated computing stack. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">But the approach isn’t to simply pack more memory onto the GPU. It’s full-stack codesign. NVIDIA intentionally designs GPUs, CPUs, networking, storage and software together to get more useful work out of memory at every layer of the AI factory.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That matters because an AI factory runs on a memory and storage hierarchy. GPU high-bandwidth memory (HBM) is the fastest, most expensive and most contested tier — with model weights and KV cache competing for limited capacity. Below it sit CPU memory, local SSDs and shared storage, each trading speed for more capacity at a lower cost per bit. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">How much useful work a byte does depends on how well the whole hierarchy is used: shrinking what must be stored, placing it on the right tier and bringing it back to the GPU before it’s needed. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Byte for byte, NVIDIA makes the most efficient use of memory at every layer — and the results show it. From NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra to the Vera Rubin platform, HBM capacity per GPU rose roughly 3%, while agentic throughput per dollar rose by up to 30x on SemiAnalysis AgentX.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Agentic Inference Is Putting More Pressure on HBM</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Frontier models now have trillions of parameters — the values that hold what an AI model learned in training.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">KV cache, which holds the context the model needs to understand each active request without reprocessing the same information, is also expanding. Agentic trajectories carry prior context across repeated model and tool calls, in some cases processing up to 3 million tokens, so each request’s working memory grows over time. </span></p>
<p><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/what-is-retrieval-augmented-generation/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> adds relevant enterprise information to prompts, creating even more context to process and retain. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">During inference, HBM primarily holds both model parameters and KV cache. The result: greater pressure on HBM. </span></p>
<p><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-99653" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-pressure.gif" alt="" width="1920" height="1080" /></p>
<h2><strong>Why AI Factories Need a Memory Hierarchy </strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Adding more HBM capacity per GPU is the obvious response to that pressure, but capacity delivers only what bandwidth can move. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Think of HBM as a warehouse and memory bandwidth as its loading docks. A larger warehouse holds more inventory, but the docks set how much can move in and out at a time. Once the docks are at capacity, moving more inventory takes longer and slows response times. If delays exceed the required service level, the added space can’t be used effectively, even as it adds cost and draws power. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That’s also why no operation keeps all its inventory in the closest, fastest space. What’s in motion stays near the line. What’s waiting moves to a larger, cheaper space nearby and returns before it’s needed. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">An AI factory faces the same limit. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">More HBM can hold more KV cache and support more concurrent users. Every added user shares the same compute and memory bandwidth. But when each user expects fast responses, eventually memory bandwidth is unable to supply data as fast as compute can use it: a limit engineers call the memory wall. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Past that point, extra HBM capacity can’t be put to work, yet it still adds costs and draws power. The data still needs a place to live, but not all of it needs the fastest, most expensive tier. This is why an AI factory manages data across a memory and storage hierarchy. </span></p>
<p><img decoding="async" class="alignnone size-large wp-image-99674" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-1680x945.jpg" alt="" width="1200" height="675" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-1680x945.jpg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-960x540.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-1280x720.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-1536x864.jpg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-1290x725.jpg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-630x354.jpg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-300x169.jpg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1-400x225.jpg 400w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-hbm-throughput-plateau-1920x1080-1.jpg 1920w" sizes="(max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></p>
<h2><strong>Maximizing the Value of Every Byte With Full-Stack Codesign</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA’s full-stack codesign makes every byte of HBM count by relieving pressure from model weights, KV cache and RAG data. It does this with techniques that each target a different source of inefficiency: </span></p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone wp-image-99656 size-large" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-1680x945.jpg" alt="" width="1200" height="675" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-1680x945.jpg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-960x540.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-1280x720.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-1536x864.jpg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-1290x725.jpg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-630x354.jpg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-300x169.jpg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack-400x225.jpg 400w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/end-to-end-social-memory-blog-launch-5869521-memory-optimization-stack.jpg 1920w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></p>
<p><b>Shrink model weights and distribute them across the NVL72 domain.</b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/3-ways-nvfp4-accelerates-ai-training-and-inference/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVFP4</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> format reduces the space required for weights compared to FP8, the lowest precision possible on the NVIDIA Hopper platform. Recent models, including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://bfl.ai/blog/flux2-klein-towards-interactive-visual-intelligence"><span style="font-weight: 400;">FLUX.2 [klein]</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Black Forest Labs</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/introducing-inkling/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Inkling</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Thinking Machines Lab, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/reflection/Beam-1.0-501B-A23B-NVFP4"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Beam 1.0</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from Reflection</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/scale-agentic-coding-workflows-with-metas-muse-spark-open-on-nvidia-gb300-nvl72/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Meta Muse Spark</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Meta</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/Step-5-Preview-NVFP4"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Step 5 Preview</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from StepFun</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-nemotron-3-5-super-vl-powers-specialized-agents-with-multimodal-reasoning/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> have been released with NVFP4 checkpoints, demonstrating growing model-builder adoption of 4-bit precision for faster, more memory-efficient inference. </span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Within an NVL72 system, model weights can be </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/mixture-of-experts-frontier-models/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">distributed across up to 72 GPUs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, so each GPU holds a smaller share of the model — leaving more HBM for KV cache and concurrent users. </span></li>
</ul>
<p><b>Shrink KV-cache and offload inactive context to lower-cost storage tiers.</b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Applying</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-inference-for-long-context-and-large-batch-sizes-with-nvfp4-kv-cache/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVFP4 to the KV cache</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> reduces its footprint compared with FP8, so more context can stay resident in HBM.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-cpu/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera CPU</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> helps in two ways. Faster tool execution shortens the time KV cache sits inactive during tool calls, and its large LPDDR memory, connected to NVIDIA Rubin GPUs over NVIDIA NVLink-C2C interconnect, gives paused context a nearby place to wait and return quickly. </span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">When context isn’t actively in use,</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/dynamo/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Dynamo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> inference software moves it to</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/ai-storage/cmx/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CMX</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> context memory storage — a tier built for capacity at far lower cost per bit than HBM — and returns it before reuse. Unlike local SSDs, CMX is shared across servers, and unlike enterprise storage, it skips the overhead of long-term data protection that short-lived context doesn’t need. NVIDIA CMX context memory storage partners include </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cloudian</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.ddn.com/press-releases/ddn-accelerates-inference-and-lowers-cost-per-token-while-expanding-multi-tenant-training-for-ai-factories/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">DDN</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.dell.com/en-us/dt/corporate/newsroom/announcements/detailpage.press-releases~usa~2025~11~dell-technologies-and-nvidia-advance-enterprise-ai-innovation.htm#/filter-on/Country:en-us"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dell Technologies</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://blog.purestorage.com/news-events/pure-kva-integrates-nvidia-dynamo-for-scalable-low-latency-llm-inference/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Everpure</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (previously Pure Storage), </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.hpe.com/us/en/newsroom/press-release/2026/03/hpe-unveils-next-generation-ai-factory-and-supercomputing-advancements-with-nvidia.html#:~:text=HPE%20AI%20Factory%20with%20Mission%20Control"><span style="font-weight: 400;">HPE</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://community.ibm.com/community/user/blogs/vincent-hsu/2026/01/05/accelerating-nvidia-dynamo-with-ibm-storage-scale#:~:text=IBM%20is%20working%20with%20NVIDIA%20to%20combine,technologies%20to%20unlock%20scalable%2C%20high%2Dperformance%20LLM%20inference."><span style="font-weight: 400;">IBM</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.netapp.com/newsroom/press-releases/news-rel-20250318-145499/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NetApp</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.vastdata.com/blog/how-nvidia-dynamo-vast-unlock-context-reuse-at-scale"><span style="font-weight: 400;">VAST</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.weka.io/blog/ai-ml/weka-accelerates-ai-inference-with-nvidia-dynamo-and-nvidia-nixl/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">WEKA</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dynamo’s KV Cache Reuse ties the tiers together, managing KV cache as one system-wide resource across GPU memory, CPU memory and storage, so context computed once can be reused across the system. </span></li>
</ul>
<p><b>Compact RAG embeddings and keeping the index outside HBM.</b></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">RAG turns enterprise documents into embeddings — numerical representations that let a system search by meaning rather than keyword — organized into a searchable index. NVIDIA Nemotron Retriever models produce smaller embeddings with higher retrieval accuracy, while the NVIDIA cuVS library enables that index to be built and served across CPU and storage. The knowledge base never occupies HBM, and only the passages the model needs enter context. </span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The result is less HBM used for model weights, less HBM tied up by idle context and more of it available to serve concurrent users. </span></p>
<h2><strong>More Useful Output per Dollar With Each Generation</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Across successive NVIDIA platform generations, tokens per second per dollar has risen far faster than HBM capacity. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On the SemiAnalysis AgentX DeepSeek V4 Pro workload, NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 delivers up to 10x higher throughput per dollar than NVIDIA H200 platform at 100 tokens per second per user, with HBM capacity per GPU roughly doubling. At 125 tokens per second per user, Vera Rubin NVL72 delivers up to 30x higher throughput per dollar than GB300 NVL72, with HBM capacity per GPU up about 3%. </span></p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-large wp-image-99657" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-keynote-LD2_0199-1-1680x1120.jpg" alt="" width="1200" height="800" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-keynote-LD2_0199-1-1680x1120.jpg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-keynote-LD2_0199-1-960x640.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-keynote-LD2_0199-1-1280x854.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-keynote-LD2_0199-1-1536x1024.jpg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-keynote-LD2_0199-1-scaled.jpg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/26gtc-eu-keynote-LD2_0199-1-630x420.jpg 630w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For AI factory operators, the metric that matters is how many useful tokens the factory can deliver within its power budget while meeting user response-time needs. More tokens per second per watt means more work and revenue from the same power, and a lower cost for every token. NVIDIA’s full-stack codesign lowers token costs by increasing the useful work every byte of memory delivers. </span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Demand for advanced chips is rising. The processes to build them are getting more complex. And the margin for error is shrinking.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For engineers, understanding how a process change will behave, how a material will interact with a new step, how a defect maps back to a root cause are critical, challenging steps in semiconductor manufacturing — and these analyses can take days. Physical experiments on a fab line are slow and expensive. A wrong call on yield or process quality has real consequences.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI is transforming semiconductor manufacturing, impacting what engineers can do — and how fast. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Accelerated computing shortens simulation time, allowing engineers to evaluate more alternatives before committing to physical trials. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Physics-based AI helps engineers predict process behavior without running a full simulation every time. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI agents handle sequences of engineering tasks that once consumed hours. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/digital-twin/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">digital twins</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> let teams test changes in a virtual environment before committing to physical trials.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Together, these capabilities describe an AI-defined fab: a </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/industries/semiconductor/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">semiconductor</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> manufacturing factory where AI helps engineers optimize processes, catch problems earlier and make better decisions across the full manufacturing chip lifecycle. Engineers direct the work and make decisions based on their expertise while spending less time waiting for answers.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="How NVIDIA AI Is Transforming Chip Design and Manufacturing" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/rBN0PDfJWIc?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<h2><strong>Shorter Simulation Cycles Change What Engineers Can Explore</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Accelerated computing doesn’t just speed up individual calculations — it changes how many alternatives engineers can test before committing. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Multiphysics simulations model interactions among physical effects, such as heat transfer, electrical behavior and mechanical stress. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Using the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/cudss"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuDSS</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> library, multiphysics simulation software provider </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.comsol.com/blogs/accelerating-semiconductor-manufacturing-simulation-with-nvidia-cudss"><span style="font-weight: 400;">COMSOL</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is accelerating such simulations for semiconductor manufacturing by up to 10x. This helps engineers optimize equipment and processes, compare alternatives and decide which changes to test physically.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Applied Materials and NVIDIA are collaborating to accelerate atomic-scale materials discovery and process development. By integrating NVIDIA cuDSS, Applied’s Ginestra simulation platform runs up to 10x faster than CPU-only approaches. And with NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo, Applied’s ACE+ multiphysics platform delivers topography simulations up to 35x faster.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In some cases, acceleration doesn’t just shorten the calculation — it enables it altogether. Computational lithography — which defines how circuit patterns are printed onto silicon — involves calculations too intensive to complete on CPU at production scale. Siemens is integrating</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/culitho"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuLitho</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> into its Calibre lithography software, delivering up to 17x acceleration on key simulation workloads. That expands what engineers can verify before a wafer goes to print.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As such, faster calculations can help researchers explore material behavior and reaction pathways that influence next-generation semiconductor processes.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Predicting Process Behavior Without Running Every Simulation</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In addition to accelerating simulations, AI trained on physics can predict how materials and processes will behave — without running a full simulation each time.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The payoff is most visible in complex calculations that are expensive even on accelerated hardware.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Plasma — the electrically charged gas used in semiconductor etch processes — is one example. Modeling plasma behavior requires solving equations that capture how electrical, chemical and thermal processes interact, making repeated simulation costly. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/physicsnemo"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> can assist in reducing plasma-prediction simulation time from days to as little as milliseconds. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lam Research intends to integrate NanoVDB into its advanced semiconductor process modeling platform SEMulator3D, part of the company’s Semiverse Solutions family of virtual products. The addition of NanoVDB is expected to accelerate select wafer-etch modeling by up to 30x.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span></p>
<figure id="attachment_99610" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-99610" style="width: 1200px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="size-large wp-image-99610" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-1680x945.jpg" alt="" width="1200" height="675" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-1680x945.jpg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-960x540.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-1280x720.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-1536x864.jpg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-1290x725.jpg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-630x354.jpg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-300x169.jpg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1-400x225.jpg 400w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-simulation-1.jpg 1920w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-99610" class="wp-caption-text">Applied Materials uses digital twins built on NVIDIA Omniverse libraries to simulate fab-level optimizations in layout, throughput, cost and process control.</figcaption></figure>
<h2><strong>Agents Handle the Steps Between Decisions</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Engineers turn analyses into decisions — revising a design, investigating a test failure, adjusting a production process and more. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI agents can help carry out the steps between those decisions.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Evaluating which AI model to apply to a given task is one example: a sequential, manual process that can consume hours on the test floor. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Advantest is using </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/route-ai-agent-workloads-across-models-with-nvidia-nemo-switchyard/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA NeMo Switchyard</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> AI agents to automate that selection, reducing response times from hours to minutes.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Testing Factory Changes Before They Reach the Line</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Digital twins let teams simulate equipment configurations, layouts and process changes in a virtual environment before committing to physical trials. This reduces experimentation costs, especially as fabs become more complex and the stakes of a wrong change get higher.</span></p>
<figure id="attachment_99611" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-99611" style="width: 960px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="size-medium wp-image-99611" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-2-960x450.png" alt="" width="960" height="450" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-2-960x450.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-2-1680x788.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-2-1280x601.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-2-1536x721.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-2-630x296.png 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/applied-materials-2.png 1999w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-99611" class="wp-caption-text">A photorealistic fab digital twin built with NVIDIA Omniverse libraries, used to simulate material flow, spot bottlenecks and validate operational strategies before deployment. Image courtesy of Applied Materials.</figcaption></figure>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/advancing-semiconductor-innovation-across-materials-engineering-and-manufacturing/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Applied Materials</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> uses</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/omniverse/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to build physically accurate digital twins of entire fabs. Teams can simulate material movement, identify bottlenecks and evaluate layouts and operating strategies — extending virtual experimentation beyond individual processes to how the factory works as a whole.</span></p>
<h2><strong>The Connected Loop</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The future of semiconductor manufacturing lies in connecting these capabilities: using production data to inform models and models to evaluate changes, then bringing those findings back to the engineers making production decisions. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That’s the promise of the AI-defined fab: where AI helps people learn from each production cycle and act on that knowledge sooner.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cities generate enormous amounts of data from cameras, sensors and operational systems, but turning it into useful action — for planning, maintenance and public services — remains a challenge.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Physical and agentic AI offer new ways to close that gap, from digital twins for infrastructure planning to vision and service agents, while keeping local governments in control of how sensitive data is processed and used.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building on</span> <a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/european-cities-agents-urban-operations/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">initiatives announced at GTC Berlin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, NVIDIA and its partners are expanding physical AI and AI agents to cities worldwide. Shared computing infrastructure gives city departments, neighboring municipalities and local developers a common foundation to build on — extending services without each organization having to acquire and manage resources of their own.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="How NVIDIA AI Is Transforming Cities, Ports, and Communities Worldwide" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/tyhy-GrkgO8?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<h2><b>Agentic AI for Cities</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">From resident services to vision agents, cities are adopting NVIDIA agentic AI to deliver faster, more responsive public services. City teams don’t need a massive infrastructure investment to get started building these AI services: depending on the workload, NVIDIA RTX workstations or a small GPU system may be enough. </span></p>
<p><b>In Spain, </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">1MillionBot</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> combines open source ALIA models trained at the </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Barcelona Supercomputing Center</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and other models in resident-service agents for </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Madrid Digital (Comunidad de Madrid), Administració Oberta de Catalunya </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and 150+ deployments across Spain, handling municipal procedures, appointment requests, urban-planning incidents and service inquiries.</span></p>
<p><b>In</b> <b>the San Francisco Bay Area</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://livex.ai/insights/livex-unveils-ai-city-bringing-physical-ai-to-everyday-spaces-with-nvidia"><span style="font-weight: 400;">LiveX</span></a> <span style="font-weight: 400;">is bringing its AI City vision to life with</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> NVIDIA <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/">Cosmos</a> world models and Nemotron open models, and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/omniverse/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/intelligent-video-analytics-platform/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Metropolis</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> libraries on NVIDIA RTX PRO GPUs. The interactive, conversational AI hosts understand people’s needs, provide answers and take action, and can be tailored to local languages and service needs in airports, hospitals, local events and other public spaces.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vision AI agents can turn existing city camera feeds into operational insights, helping teams identify mobility and safety issues and coordinate emergency responses across city services.</span></p>
<p><b>In </b><b>Wrocław, Poland</b><b>, </b><a target="_blank" href="https://www.k2k.ai/ikm-state-of-the-art/k2k%C2%AE-eliminates-data-silos-with-dynamic-intelligent-knowledge-map-for-city-operators"><span style="font-weight: 400;">K2K</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is building shared AI infrastructure using NVIDIA accelerated computing, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Cosmos</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> world reasoning models, and NVIDIA Metropolis Blueprint for video search and summarization (VSS) to transform existing camera streams into actionable intelligence for mobility, safety and event response — using the same infrastructure across several city functions.</span></p>
<p><b>In </b><b>Malaysia</b><b>,</b> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.itmax.com.my/newsroom/itmax-uses-ai-to-give-kuala-lumpur-commuters-time-back-with-nvidia"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ITMAX</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> bundles infrastructure and agentic AI applications into long-term managed services for cities, including for traffic management. Built with reference to Cosmos, Nemotron and the Metropolis VSS blueprint, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">ITMAX’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> SamurAI V2 vision agents are scaling on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPUs to support 17,000+ cameras by 2027. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">ITMAX</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> integrates TeraSim, an open source traffic simulation platform from <a target="_blank" href="https://www.laplaceintelligence.com/blog/terasim-dt-open-source-traffic-simulation">Laplace Intelligence</a>, to test signal timing, reducing peak-hour corridor travel times by up to 26%.</span></p>
<h2><b>Plan and Operate Critical Infrastructure With Digital Twins</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Beyond public-facing services, digital twins can help cities expedite infrastructure planning, minimizing disruption to local communities. City digital twins can also serve as a real-time operational simulation to facilitate faster decision-making. </span></p>
<p><b>At Dangote’s Lekki refinery in Nigeria, </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">Honeywell Technologies’ </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Forge Plant Cognition uses Nemotron-powered AI agents to understand operational data. The platform, built on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dell</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> PowerEdge servers with NVIDIA RTX PRO accelerators, helps operators identify bottlenecks, anticipate equipment and process issues, and optimize plant performance.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">While science fiction lore tells us that humans will meet extraterrestrial life in the form of little green men, the detail that should raise hairs this Halloween is this: the search for extraterrestrial intelligence took a major leap forward with AI, and it increasingly focuses on AI agents built by humans listening for AI agents built by aliens.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some scientists have suggested that extraterrestrial civilizations, if they exist, could be billions of years older than humanity. Such civilizations might have developed technologies far beyond humanity’s, including forms of AI.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Breakthrough Listen and the SETI Institute are bringing agentic AI into the daily search for extraterrestrial intelligence. At the</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.seti.org/seti-allen-telescope-array-ata"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Allen Telescope Array</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in Northern California, agents can now steer the instrument, enhance and correct incoming data, and apply AI models to the incoming stream — all from a single natural language request. The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/edge-computing/holoscan/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Holoscan</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> platform processes the massive streams of sensor data, giving agents live data to act on.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The result is a faster, more adaptable search for potential signals from extraterrestrial life and technologies.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">“As our new agentic observatories reach out into the universe, what they find may have much more in common with themselves than with the humans who created them,” said Andrew Siemion, Bernard M. Oliver chair for SETI at the SETI Institute and principal investigator of Breakthrough Listen. “But this isn&#8217;t something to fear. Rather, it’s a breakthrough in translation.”</span></p>
<h2><strong>Terabits a Second, Processed in Flight</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Radio telescopes generate enormous volumes of data, creating a challenge for scientists who need to identify potentially interesting signals as observations unfold.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At the Allen Telescope Array, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/daqiri"><span style="font-weight: 400;">DAQIRI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a Holoscan platform library, enables the telescope to pull terabits per second of sensor data straight into GPU memory so observations are processed in flight rather than filling hard drives. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://cyberether.org"><span style="font-weight: 400;">CyberEther</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a signal-processing framework developed at the SETI Institute, does the math using NVIDIA CUDA-X libraries including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/cufft"><span style="font-weight: 400;">cuFFT</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/cublas"><span style="font-weight: 400;">cuBLAS</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://stelline.space"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nexus</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a web-based observatory orchestrator, acts as the agent harness. It gives agents a single source of truth for instrument state, compute resources and pipelines and lets researchers monitor packet loss, read live spectrograms and rewrite signal-processing blocks — all by prompting in natural language. The system can handle 2.4 terabits per second on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs, with inference running on premises, next to the instrument.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Put together, the system enables an agent to steer a telescope’s antennae, calibrate and focus the instrument for a clean signal, catch and fix data errors and plot the result. Agents compress what would take humans weeks or years into hours.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Science Fact, Not Fiction</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The pipeline has already proven itself, achieving the first real-time AI detection of a pulsar — a rapidly spinning stellar remnant that pulses like a cosmic lighthouse — in the Crab Nebula. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Next, the team is evaluating open models including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for cases where data locality and turnaround matter most and for observatories that may someday run offline on the far side of the moon.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Introduced today, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is a new, open weight speech model that turns recorded conversations into useful information. <p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers guide the model with plain-language prompts. For example, for a single recorded meeting, users can ask it to:</span></p>

<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">“Translate this audio into English.”</span></li>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Prompts can also include product terms and formatting conventions, which helps the model handle specialized vocabulary in fields like healthcare, financial services and customer service. 										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">There are 57 million people worldwide living with dementia today. For the researchers working to help them, the obstacle isn’t a lack of effort — it’s too much data, too few experts to synthesize it and a pace of discovery that can’t keep up with the disease. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Consortium for Biomedical Research and AI in Neurodegeneration (C-BRAIN), working with NVIDIA, is using AI to change that.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alzheimer’s disease is one of today’s leading global health challenges, with over 10 million new dementia cases diagnosed each year. Despite a 7x increase in National Institutes of Health funding in the last 15 years — reaching $3.9 billion annually — no treatment has been found to stop, prevent or reverse the disease yet. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The bottlenecks are not enough high impact scientific findings, too much data living in institutional silos and too slow a pace of synthesis to keep up with the research that is published. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">C-BRAIN, led by Dr. Randall Bateman and a consortium of 18 member institutions across academic, philanthropic, pharma and research organizations, launched in November 2025 to help scientists make better, faster discoveries by accelerating the research that makes new treatments possible. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“We have a window of opportunity with artificial intelligence to do research fundamentally different than we’ve ever done before, in a way that accelerates discoveries to help patients,” said Bateman, founder and director of C-BRAIN and the Charles F. and Joanne Knight Distinguished Professor of Neurology at the Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis. He described their work as “AI tools built by scientists, for scientists.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The consortium is building a system that harnesses previously untapped Alzheimer’s research data to support scientists’ work.</span></p>
<h2><b>Speeding Research From Weeks to Hours</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Essential assets in Alzheimer’s research are datasets that have never been publicly accessible to AI tools or human researchers. This includes unpublished findings, negative results and governed proprietary data from member institutions. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“A lot of life sciences data doesn’t make its way into the frontier models,” said Dr. Ade Ojewole, chief technology officer of C-BRAIN. “The consequence of that is we’re leaving a lot of intelligence on the table.” </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">C-BRAIN refers to this as “dark data,” which slows down Alzheimer’s research.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some dark data is directly deposited into secure computer systems for AI tools to access. To use this data without compromising patient privacy or institutional governance, C-BRAIN uses a hybrid centralized and federated approach — where AI models learn from data at each institution without that data ever having to leave its walls. This kind of infrastructure is technically demanding to build and scale, which is part of what NVIDIA brings to the collaboration.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">C-BRAIN currently runs 64 </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA data center GPUs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on premises alongside cloud-based nodes on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft Azure,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with additional NVIDIA GPU capacity, including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/blackwell-architecture/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Blackwell systems</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, on the horizon as the consortium’s ambitions scale.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To explore how AI and accelerated computing can advance Alzheimer’s research while navigating its technical demands, C-BRAIN is working with NVIDIA to incorporate technologies including the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-BioNeMo"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA BioNeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> open developer platform and the MONAI framework for C-BRAIN’s research pipelines.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“The C-BRAIN collaboration with NVIDIA aims to unlock those models in a way that they can deliver something quite impactful and important,” said Bateman. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“A great deal of optimization is going to be needed as we push the boundaries of what today’s spatial omics machine learning architectures can model,” Ojewole said. “NVIDIA software is definitely going to come into play there.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Researchers at C-BRAIN have already built two tools: OpenScientist — built with</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory in an effort led by research scientist Justin Reese — and InsightEngine</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, built with 387 Labs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenScientist is an open source agentic AI co-scientist that takes a scientist’s research question and dataset, then semi-autonomously generates hypotheses, runs analyses and produces verifiable, auditable insights. InsightEngine is an open source research platform that uses agentic AI to test hypotheses against the scientific literature, analyzing user-supplied datasets, exploring a biomedical knowledge graph and checking every claim against evidence to catch hallucinations before researchers commit to costly experiments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In published case studies, OpenScientist completed analyses in minutes that domain scientists estimated would take weeks to months, at a cost of under $10 per run. At C-BRAIN’s current reach of over 200 research groups, this capability could release substantial research capacity, potentially exceeding one million hours annually at scale.</span></p>
<h2><b>From Correlation to Cause</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Looking ahead, C-BRAIN aims to build a multimodal biological foundation model trained on the full breadth of its data — neuroimaging, multi-omics, fluid biomarkers and longitudinal clinical measures. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">By year three, it aims to discover new causal mechanisms of brain aging and Alzheimer’s disease, moving to testable scientific principles using the full scope of available research and data. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bateman said, “</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI that can think and reason has the potential to accelerate discoveries that will lead to powerful and implementable preventions and treatments for the entire population.”</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kazakhstan’s AI ecosystem is moving from infrastructure plans to deployment. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The shift is visible across the full AI stack — from infrastructure to models to applications.</span></p>
<p>The announcements below — coinciding with a visit to Astana by NVIDIA founder and CEO Jensen Huang — point to the nation’s increasing supply of accelerated computing, along with its growing demand for what comes next.</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On Friday, Oct. 23, NVIDIA founder and CEO Jensen Huang visited Astana, meeting with President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev at the Akorda Presidential Palace and joining Deputy Prime Minister Zhaslan Madiyev — Kazakhstan’s minister of AI and digital development — for a public fireside chat at the </span><a target="_blank" href="http://alem.ai"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alem.AI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> program. </span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<h2><b>Jensen Huang and Kazakhstan’s AI Minister Speak at Alem.AI</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In their fireside chat at Alem.AI, Kazakhstan’s international center for artificial intelligence, Madiyev and Huang discussed the country’s push to become an AI nation — building the energy and computing infrastructure to support homegrown AI and applying it across the economy.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Madiyev said Kazakhstan wants AI that reflects its language and culture. “We want AI that understands and speaks Kazakh and captures cultural contexts and cultural specifics,” he said, describing the country’s work on Kazakh-language models. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang said Kazakhstan could turn its energy resources into a foundation for AI production — and a new kind of export. “Instead of just exporting energy, in the future, you could transform energy into intelligence and export intelligence,” he said.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">He also argued that local data is central to that ambition: it embodies a country’s culture, history and values. “Your country’s data is your natural resource. It belongs to you.” </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The conversation also turned to physical AI. Madiyev pointed to Kazakhstan’s factories, mines, agriculture, transport and logistics as potential areas for development, asking how the country could become a hub for training models for robots and other physical systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang called the idea “fantastic,” saying physical AI could transform industries from mining and mineral processing to agriculture. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang said AI and robotics can help people do more, but ambition will shape how fully they seize that opportunity. He applied the idea to Kazakhstan, saying AI could help lift the country and its people.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“If you have ambition, and I believe you do, AI will lift your nation,” Huang said. “AI will lift your people.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang also praised the founders of Higgsfield AI, the Kazakhstan-founded startup, and it was announced NVIDIA is making an investment into the startup during the event. “The first thing and the last thing is you invest in people, not companies,” he said, describing the founders as visionary and pointing to their effort to compete globally. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Asked about AI security, Huang said the technology must be developed and deployed responsibly. He described NVIDIA’s OpenShell as a way to contain AI agents by limiting their access to files and tools and monitoring their activity.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-99486" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-scaled.jpeg" alt="" width="2048" height="1152" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-scaled.jpeg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-960x540.jpeg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1680x945.jpeg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1280x720.jpeg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1536x864.jpeg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1290x725.jpeg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-630x354.jpeg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-300x169.jpeg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-400x225.jpeg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 2048px) 100vw, 2048px" /></span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For young people entering a fast-changing field, Huang recommended learning how AI works — not just how to use it. He said aspiring AI programmers should understand the technology’s principles, strengths and weaknesses, combining technical knowledge with the ability to work effectively with AI. </span></p>
<p>“I envy being 18 years old today… because the world is changing so rapidly, and there’s a complete reset because of technology.”</p>
<h2><b>The Infrastructure Layer Is Scaling<br />
</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The visits coincided with a signing ceremony marking Firebird’s investment agreement with the government of Kazakhstan, held at the presidential palace. </span><b></b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building on its first AI factory in Armenia, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Firebird</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is expanding into Kazakhstan, where it has secured a U.S. export license for 45,000 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs. The first phase is under construction with Kazakhtelecom at Data Center Valley, targeting commercial launch in the third quarter of 2027.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A planned second phase would bring Firebird’s total potential AI infrastructure capacity in Kazakhstan to approximately 500 megawatts. Firebird also plans to establish Firebird Labs Kazakhstan to support local AI startups, research and talent development through funding, access to compute and research partnerships.</span></p>
<p>Firebird cofounder and CEO Razmig Hovaghimian attended the signing ceremony at the Akorda Presidential Palace alongside government officials.</p>
<p>“What began as a seed of an idea 180 days ago has grown into a major investment in Kazakhstan’s AI future,” said Razmig Hovaghimian, co-Founder and CEO of Firebird. “President Tokayev’s vision gave us the confidence to think bigger about what we can build together. With Jensen Huang here today, that ambition feels more tangible than ever. We’re building the infrastructure for Kazakhstan’s talent and ideas to reach the world.”</p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://telecom.kz/en"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kazakhtelecom</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the nation’s largest telecommunications company, has already deployed 400 NVIDIA H200 and 480 NVIDIA B300 GPUs in Astana and Almaty. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Next year, the company plans to deploy more than 1,000 GPUs in the <a target="_blank" href="https://dc-valley.com/en">Data Center Valley</a>, along with more than 1,000 NVIDIA Vera Rubin GPUs in the Ekibastuz region, serving diverse workloads with infrastructure that enterprises, developers and institutions can use at scale.</span></p>
<h2><b>An Honorary Black Belt for Huang</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At the end of their meeting, President Tokayev presented Huang with the Order of Dostyk, First Class, for his contribution to the development of artificial intelligence in Kazakhstan. <em>Dostyk</em> means friendship and the Order honors peace, friendship and cooperation.</span></p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-99502" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-scaled.jpeg" alt="" width="2048" height="1152" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-scaled.jpeg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-960x540.jpeg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1680x945.jpeg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1280x720.jpeg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1536x864.jpeg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1290x725.jpeg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-630x354.jpeg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-300x169.jpeg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-400x225.jpeg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 2048px) 100vw, 2048px" /></p>

The belt, embroidered in gold stitching, was a special thanks for Huang’s support of Kazakhstan’s AI ambition and for leadership aligned with the spirit and rigor of the martial art.</span></p>
<h2><b>AI-Native Companies Scale With Compute</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And that development is accelerating. The nation’s AI-native companies are also building out their compute capacity — and the applications that run atop them.<br />
</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Higgsfield is expanding its AI infrastructure with NVIDIA Vera Rubin running on cloud service providers, giving the company additional capacity as models, products and customer demand for its generative media applications grow.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://blog.indrive.com/article/indrive-and-pony-ai-announce-collaboration-to-explore-autonomous-mobility-in-kazakhstan-built-on-nvidia-hyperion"><span style="font-weight: 400;">inDrive and Pony.ai</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are also collaborating to explore robotaxi services in Kazakhstan, with Astana as the first deployment city. The companies will assess the feasibility of bringing Pony.ai’s seventh-generation robotaxis to the inDrive app, supported by NVIDIA AI infrastructure, built on the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/solutions/autonomous-vehicles/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Hyperion</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> sensor and compute platform with the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-trust-center/halos/autonomous-vehicles/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Halos</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> safety system.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">More local capacity gives developers and companies room to train, fine-tune and serve increasingly capable models. Successful applications, in turn, create demand for more compute.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kazakhstan’s AI opportunity isn’t defined by GPU counts alone. Connecting that infrastructure with developers, universities, startups and industries can turn accelerated computing into useful applications.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Today’s announcements </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">represent different layers of the AI stack in Kazakhstan— infrastructure deployment, access to compute and AI-native product development — where the AI foundation is reaching new scale.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Launched today, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/networking/products/software/minerva/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Minerva</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is an AIOps platform built to help teams find problems earlier and keep AI factories productive. It brings together telemetry from infrastructure and AI workloads, then applies purpose-built AI models to turn that data into timely, actionable insight.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI factories are among the most complex operations in the world, with vast numbers of GPUs working across high-speed networks to train models and serve AI applications. At that scale, a single degraded component can slow an entire job. The warning signs may be easy to miss, such as a GPU running hotter than its peers or an NVMe drive losing performance under load.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The platform focuses on AI factory yield, a measure of the useful work infrastructure produces through GPU hours, goodput, cluster uptime and token production.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2><b>Delivering a Dynamic View of an AI Factory</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Traditional monitoring tools often divide the data center into separate views. Networking, compute, site reliability and machine learning teams each see a different part of the operation. Minerva connects those signals across NVIDIA GPUs, networking, storage, schedulers and workloads.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With these signals, Minerva builds a dynamic knowledge graph that logically maps the infrastructure — GPUs, SuperNICs, switches and cables — and the jobs running on it. This context helps operators trace a slow training job to the component affecting it, even across a factory with hundreds of thousands of GPUs.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It analyzes billions of data points per hour using supervised and unsupervised machine learning. It learns normal behavior, detects previously unseen anomalies, predicts emerging failures and correlates related events based on the factory’s topology. Instead of sending every symptom to an operator, it can group an event storm into a focused incident with a likely root cause.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Minerva comes with a native AI assistant and connects to external agentic AI tools. This adds a natural-language interface grounded in live telemetry. Operators can investigate cluster health and receive recommended remediation through conversation. Teams can keep a person in the loop to manually implement fixes or connect Minerva to automation through infrastructure application programming interfaces and external systems.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Results That Maximize AI Factory Yield</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The ability to correlate workload and infrastructure issues along with its agentic AI capabilities help simplify AI factory operations. Instead of dealing with a blizzard of alerts, Minerva aggregates related problems into a single actionable item. This allows AIOps teams to move from reactive to proactive maintenance. And all of this helps AI factories maximize GPU uptime.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Minerva delivers value for the key metrics AI factories focus on:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><b>Longer time between interruptions</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Minerva spots trouble before it happens, so jobs run longer without incident.</span></li>
<li><b>Faster time to repair</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: When an incident does happen, Minerva pinpoints the problem, leading to a significantly quicker resolution.</span></li>
<li><b>Greater insight</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Modern AI infrastructure generates a flood of data; Minerva filters it out, leaving only the handful of alerts that actually matter.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">IREN is among the NVIDIA partners using Minerva to optimize its AI factories. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“As we scale our AI factories, the key to providing the best service for our customers is maximizing the productivity of our GPUs,” said </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Denis Skrinnikoff, chief technology officer of IREN. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">“NVIDIA Minerva gives our operations team one view across the network, the GPUs and the jobs running on them, so we focus more on proactive maintenance rather than troubleshooting. 										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At GTC Berlin, banks, payment companies and financial data providers are showing what production-scale intelligence looks like — built on NVIDIA accelerated computing, powered by open weight models and running on their own infrastructure.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Revolut</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is rebuilding its technology stack on a single foundation model </span><a target="_blank" href="https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-financial-services-industry/revolut-builds-a-transaction-foundation-model-1185435"><span style="font-weight: 400;">PRAGMA</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, powered by NVIDIA accelerated computing. Using the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/topics/ai/data-science/cuda-x-data-science-libraries/cudf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuDF</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> toolkit and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/dynamo-triton"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dynamo-Triton software</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/blackwell-architecture/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Revolut is scaling PRAGMA in production, servicing an initial 80 million customers and engineered to scale 3x.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“Revolut processes billions of transactions — the opportunity was always there, but the question was whether foundation models could operate at that scale and across domains. PRAGMA answers that,” said Pavel Nesterov, head of AI at Revolut. “Built in collaboration with NVIDIA, it’s the clearest proof yet that the gap between a research breakthrough and a production banking system can be closed.”</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<h2><b>Training on Accelerated Infrastructure</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Starling Bank</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the first bank to train an agentic model on U.K. sovereign cloud infrastructure, has developed “Starling Intelligence” — the intelligence layer that underpins its AI capability — on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Civo’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> U.K. cloud using the bank’s own transaction data. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lloyds Banking Group</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> built </span><a target="_blank" href="https://medium.com/ai-at-lloyds-banking-group/introducing-atum-a-foundation-model-for-banking-4d5291577d50"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ATUM</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a transaction foundation model that converts billions of banking transactions into reusable intelligence — accelerated with NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs and improving efficiency across bank-wide use cases.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">SBS</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> launched a banking sovereign intelligence platform so banks can run generative and agentic models without their data leaving their own walls.</span></p>
<h2><b>Open Models at a Fraction of the Cost</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Open weight models give European financial institutions proprietary intelligence built on data they own, at costs they control. NVIDIA’s accelerated computing platform is the engine running beneath them.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Neobank </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">bunq’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> agentic assistant Finn now runs on open weight models. Powered by NVIDIA accelerated computing, deploying on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nebius</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> matches the quality of leading closed models at 60% of the cost per answer. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NatWest</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is building technology designed to recognize the full range of British accents, dialects and financial terminology, developed with NVIDIA’s speech models, including Nemotron 3.5 ASR. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Modulr</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is building specialized models for distinct parts of payment operations, based on the NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B open model.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Santander</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is an early adopter of the new NVIDIA Kumo family of open weight foundation models for structured financial data, using them to build specialized intelligence for core banking workloads.</span></p>
<h2><b>Accelerated Computing Transforms Financial Research</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">LSEG</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is working with NVIDIA to help financial institutions analyze large volumes of historical tick data up to 10x faster with the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.pola.rs/user-guide/lazy/gpu/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Polars GPU engine</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, accelerated by NVIDIA cuDF on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RavenPack</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> built a dataset generation pipeline on Bigdata.com that takes a plain-language research hypothesis and returns a labeled, backtest-ready dataset — running on a fine-tuned NVIDIA Nemotron model. 										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Companies around the world are building on the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DSX AI Factory Platform</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to plan, simulate and run the grid and the AI factory as one system, from the substation to the rack. Their adoption of the platform responds to an acute problem.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Power is now the biggest bottleneck in infrastructure. More than $300 billion in equipment is backlogged, and over 200 gigawatts of projects are waiting in U.S. interconnection queues. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One root cause of the bottleneck is siloed systems. The grid, the facility and the compute inside have long been designed independently from one another, creating the conditions for delays, risk and stranded capacity.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To help address this, NVIDIA DSX brings these systems together:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>NVIDIA DSX Sim and SimReady assets</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from leading energy manufacturers let engineers simulate the grid, substation and AI factory together, validating generators, switchgear, power systems and cooling before breaking ground.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/flex/"><b>NVIDIA </b><b>DSX Flex</b></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> syncs AI factory demand with grid conditions and onsite generation, turning AI factories from static loads into flexible grid assets.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>NVIDIA DSX MaxLPS and DSX Exchange</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> squeeze more compute from every watt and securely share signals across compute, networking, energy, cooling and operations.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><b>GE Vernova</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is deploying its grid-to-facility and rack power expertise to complement NVIDIA DSX systems, enabling customers to transform power constraints into competitive advantages while maximizing compute and token output, sustainability and grid reliability. </span></p>
<p><b>Hitachi Energy</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">’s battery energy storage system has qualified as </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/dsx-ready/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DSX Ready</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">,  helping customers identify power infrastructure that meets applicable NVIDIA DSX reference architecture design requirements for resilient, reliable and scalable AI factories.</span></p>
<p><b>Schneider Electric</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">, together with AVEVA and ETAP, is advancing NVIDIA DSX with validated </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/rubin/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera Rubin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> power and cooling reference architectures and an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/omniverse/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">-powered AI factory digital twin. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Combining </span><b>AVEVA</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">’s engineering software expertise with NVIDIA SimReady assets, OpenUSD and Omniverse enables connecting simulations, real-time operational data and AI-driven optimization across AVEVA, Schneider Electric and third-party solutions. Together, these capabilities help organizations design, simulate, operate and optimize data centers across their lifecycle, improving energy efficiency and workload performance while accelerating time to token. </span></p>
<p><b>Siemens Energy</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is expanding its collaboration with NVIDIA by developing a reference design for resilient, scalable and grid-aware power infrastructure across power generation, transmission and distribution. This design is aligned with the requirements of the NVIDIA DSX Vera Rubin AI factory design. The company is also exploring usingNVIDIA technologies such as RAPIDS, Metropolis and Isaac Sim for its Noedra digital twin platform, evaluating how the technologies could enhance grid infrastructure monitoring, identify emerging risks and support unplanned outage prevention.</span></p>
<p><b>Phaidra</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> integrated DSX Flex, DSX Max-Q and DSX Exchange into its self-learning agents, optimizing cooling and power allocation to maximize tokens per watt, improve grid flexibility and deliver about 10% more compute within the same power envelope.</span></p>
<p><b>Emerald AI</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> integrated its Conductor platform into DSX Flex to help utilities tap underused grid capacity, an approach modeled to unlock up to 100 gigawatts on the existing U.S. grid.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">European retailers are using open models and physical intelligence across use cases — from a commerce platform cutting inference costs by 90% to to a fulfillment center where collaboration of different robotic fleets is simulated inside a digital twin. </span></p>
<h2><b>Frontier Results With Open Models</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Retail leaders and technology companies across Europe are using NVIDIA-accelerated open and frontier models to advance agentic commerce, digital product discovery and retail R&amp;D.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Shopify </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">post-trains open models to reach frontier-level accuracy at a fraction of the cost. Its engineers use the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/megatron-bridge/0.1.0/index.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Megatron Bridge</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> library for faster fine-tuning and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/products/nemo/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA NeMo RL</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for reinforcement learning. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Shopify </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">has post-trained </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Lightning</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for the GraphQL subagent integrated into Sidekick, Shopify&#8217;s AI assistant for merchants. The subagent retrieves store data for several Sidekick features, including Sidekick Pulse, which researches a merchant&#8217;s store and recommends ways to improve it. </span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Shopify&#8217;s </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">internal benchmark, the post-trained model surpasses a leading frontier model in accuracy while cutting serving costs by 90% and delivering responses 7x faster. Beyond Sidekick, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.centml.ai/home/introduction/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CCluster</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> vLLM inference optimization software helped Shopify achieve 4.5x faster performance and up to 12x lower inference cost across five additional use cases: catalog enrichment, merchant assistant, e-commerce shopping simulation, product taxonomy classification, and merchant and buyer recommendations.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Zooplus</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Europe’s leading online pet retailer, with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Infosys</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and NVIDIA technologies rebuilt its product discovery stack for the agentic era. A production ready prototype deployed inside </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Zooplus</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">’ </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Amazon Web Services (AWS) </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">environment achieved </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">86% search accuracy</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> using the NVIDIA Nemotron Retriever library for semantic search and the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/retail-catalog-enrichment"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Blueprint for retail catalog enrichment</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for generated, search-ready product attributes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Photoroom</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> results from its deployment with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vestiaire Collective</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, where its Image Editing API now standardizes every primary image,  about 40,000 a day across 70 countries, at the point of listing. Listings processed by </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Photoroom</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.photoroom.com/inside-photoroom/vestiaire-collective-liquidity-photoroom"><span style="font-weight: 400;">sold at a 5% higher rate of liquidity, with 3% to 7% more unique views per product page</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. The pipeline runs on NVIDIA GPUs with TensorRT and Triton Inference Server, and NVIDIA Dynamo Snapshot can help users quickly respond to demand spikes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omi</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> launched ProductDrop, a workflow that anchors every generated image to an approved digital twin, letting brands </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.omi.so/resources/blog/omi-productdrop-brings-product-accuracy-to-genai-with-nvidia"><span style="font-weight: 400;">scale creative output across entire catalogs without introducing product errors</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">L’Oréal </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">is using this approach, combining </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omi</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">’s digital twins with generated brand environments within its CreAItech program. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omi</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> runs TRELLIS 3D asset generation on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs, speeding up inference by 10x and reducing pipeline latency by 4x.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In addition, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">L&#8217;Oréal </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">is accelerating the discovery of the best ingredient combinations, moving from months of experimentation to hours of computational screening, with the integration of NVIDIA ALCHEMI into its research workflow. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">L&#8217;Oréal </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">is also testing this approach with CurieOS, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Orbital Industries</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">&#8216; operating system for science and engineering, using OrbMol-v2, its open source molecular simulation model. Integrated with the NVIDIA ALCHEMI Toolkit and powered by NVIDIA accelerated computing, OrbMol-v2 is five times faster than comparable models and delivers 21% lower error than the next-best model of its kind. This AI-based workflow enables researchers to identify the most promising ingredient combinations for solubilizing UV filters and optimizing performance before committing to laboratory testing.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mirakl</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a software company for intelligent commerce, runs open models on NVIDIA GPUs, both on its own infrastructure and through European cloud providers like </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Scaleway</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">. This keeps its </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.mirakl.com/blogs/agentic-commerce/why-mirakl-builds-on-open-source-ai-and-what-it-delivers-for-ecommerce-businesses/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">technology sovereign, avoids vendor lock-in and delivers the speed and scale its customers expect</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mirakl</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">’s Catalog Transformer pairs fine-tuned open models with proprietary services and has onboarded more than 70 million products, turning messy vendor data into accurate, well-organized catalogs. </span></p>
<h2><b>Physical Intelligence Enables Training in Simulation</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA physical AI, digital twins and open models help simulate retail environments, automate warehouse operations and deliver virtual try-on experiences for European retailers and fashion brands.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Otto Group</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> advanced its autonomous warehouse under human supervision initiative — built with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Reply </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">on NVIDIA Omniverse libraries and running on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Google Cloud</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> — to the group’s largest fulfillment center in Haldensleben, Germany. New capabilities let logistics teams </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.ottogroup.com/en/stories/story/otto-group-advance-the-autonomous-warehouse-with-coala-and-nvidia-omniverse.php">design, test and optimize warehouse operations</a> entirely in simulation before any physical implementation</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">ALLSIDES</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> launched its 3D Digital Twin Factory: an </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.allsides.tech/resources/allsides-3d-digital-twin-factory">automated pipeline</a> that transforms physical objects into SimReady 3D digital twins</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in minutes, capturing sub-millimeter geometric accuracy, measured physical properties and fully relightable materials that maintain accuracy under any lighting condition. Those digital twins serve as the foundation for simulating entire store layouts in 3D, as well as for tracking products reliably throughout the shopping journey and at checkout regardless of ambient light. Built on NVIDIA CUDA, OptiX and TensorRT, the platform already serves brands including </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">adidas</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Amazon</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nike</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Zalando</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Zara</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">CATCHES</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> achieved production results from its RealFit deployment with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">AMIRI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> of </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261022064337/en/CATCHES-Opens-to-Retailers-with-PredictiveFit-Virtual-Try-On-Launching-on-THE-OUTNET-After-RealFit-Lifted-AMIRI-Online-Conversion-by-55"><span style="font-weight: 400;">conversion up 55%, add-to-cart up 80% and gross sales up 56%</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, all after seasonal adjustment against like-for-like styles. The company launched PredictiveFit, extending physics-based virtual try-on to retailers, as well as CASI, a personal fashion agent that shops, styles and sizes for customers over WhatsApp, powered by open weight models including NVIDIA Nemotron 3, built on NVIDIA Omniverse and the Newton Physics Engine.</span></p>
