										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">New </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/rtx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA RTX Spark compact desktops</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from ASUS, Dell and MSI are available today, bringing local AI agents, image and video generation, and model fine-tuning to a dedicated Windows PC.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With up to 128GB of unified memory and NVIDIA’s AI software stack, these systems provide room for larger local models alongside RTX-accelerated creative and development tools. A dedicated desktop can keep agents and scheduled tasks running while a laptop travels or handles other work.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alongside the new desktops, an expanding software ecosystem brings more creative and development workflows to RTX Spark laptops and compact desktops.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Native Windows on Arm applications from Adobe, the NVIDIA DLSS Ray Reconstruction feature in Blender and local generation workflows in ComfyUI expand what creators and developers can do on RTX Spark — all on one Windows PC.</span></p>
<h2><b>A Windows PC Built for Local AI</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As workflows expand to include local AI models and agents, more work can continue beyond an active editing session — from generating assets to processing long renders or scheduled tasks. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RTX Spark compact desktops give developers, creators and AI enthusiasts a dedicated Windows PC for local models and always-on agents. They bring together image and video generation, model fine-tuning, creative applications and development tools in one compact system.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Running supported models locally keeps locally processed code and files on the PC and avoids per-token cloud application programming interface (API) charges for those tasks.</span></p>
<figure id="attachment_100083" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-100083" style="width: 1280px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1.jpg"><img fetchpriority="high" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-100083" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1.jpg" alt="" width="1280" height="680" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1-960x510.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1-630x335.jpg 630w" sizes="(max-width: 1280px) 100vw, 1280px" /></a><figcaption id="caption-attachment-100083" class="wp-caption-text">RTX Spark compact desktops like the ASUS ProArt GR1X mini PC are built to run personal AI agents 24/7.</figcaption></figure>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RTX Spark desktops are equipped with a 20-core NVIDIA Grace CPU, an NVIDIA Blackwell RTX GPU with 6,144 CUDA cores and up to 128GB of unified memory, delivering up to 1 petaflop of AI performance. Ten-gigabit Ethernet connectivity enables fast transfers of large datasets, model files and creative assets between RTX Spark compact desktops and networked storage.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This advanced hardware unlocks incredible capabilities for creators, developers and AI enthusiasts, including:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Running massive 120-billion-parameter large language models locally</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rendering 90GB+ 3D scenes in real time</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Smoothly editing 4:2:2 video in real time</span></li>
</ul>
<figure id="attachment_100090" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-100090" style="width: 1280px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series.png"><img decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-100090" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series.png" alt="" width="1280" height="328" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series-960x246.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series-630x161.png 630w" sizes="(max-width: 1280px) 100vw, 1280px" /></a><figcaption id="caption-attachment-100090" class="wp-caption-text">The MSI EdgeMesa N AI+ Series RTX Spark compact desktop.</figcaption></figure>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">New compact desktops are available for purchase from ASUS, Dell and MSI, joining the RTX Spark family of laptops available today. Mini PCs from Acer, GIGABYTE and HP are coming soon, along with the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/surface/devices/surface-rtx-spark-dev-box"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft Surface RTX Spark Dev Box</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<h2><b>From Local Agents to AI Creation</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Native CUDA support and Linux development tools through Windows Subsystem for Linux give developers a foundation for building and running local AI workflows. Developers can set up agents with Hermes Agent or NVIDIA NemoClaw blueprints and fine-tune supported models using Unsloth.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RTX Spark can run creative agents that connect familiar applications and local AI tools, helping creators move from an idea to finished work with natural-language instructions.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Switchyard, now available in Hermes, automatically routes agent tasks between local and cloud models. Everyday tasks can run on device, while advanced reasoning draws on cloud models, helping manage cloud costs across creative workflows.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These capabilities come together in creative workflows that connect Photoshop and ComfyUI through Hermes Agent. Using natural-language instructions, creators can turn sketches into rendered images and animations, or animate an image entirely on device by isolating a subject and defining its motion.</span></p>
<p><iframe title="Adobe MAX 2026 NVIDIA RTX Spark Booth Activation" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/nypDHkAyJ5E?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<p style="text-align: center;"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA brought these workflows to life at Adobe MAX, where attendees used natural-language instructions to turn sketches into rendered images and generate video for a shared digital Miami landscape.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The RTX Video Frame Generation and RTX Video HDR features, already available in ComfyUI, extend the pipeline: a generated clip can be expanded to 60 frames per second and converted to high dynamic range for color grading, all within a single ComfyUI workflow on RTX Spark.</span></p>
<h2><b>Adobe Accelerates the Full Creative Stack for RTX Spark</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA and Adobe are expanding native Windows on Arm support and RTX acceleration across Adobe’s creative applications, helping video editors, photographers, designers and 3D artists work more efficiently on RTX Spark.</span></p>
<p><b>Adobe Premiere </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">accelerates a range of AI-powered editing features through </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/tensorrt-rtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA TensorRT-RTX</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, including Enhance Speech, Object Mask, Scene Edit Detection and Auto Reframe — so editors spend less time waiting and more time cutting. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GPU acceleration also extends to Film Impact transition and effects plug-ins. The update improves support for professional video formats, including Canon RAW, Red RAW, ARRI RAW, ARRICORE, ARRI HDE, H.264 and HEVC in MXF, ProRes RAW and long-GOP video. This lets editors realize more than 50% quicker turnaround on everyday H.264 video deliverables.</span></p>
<p><b>Adobe Photoshop </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">adds TensorRT-RTX acceleration for the Remove Tool and ArmRTX Spark-native Camera Raw support, helping photographers remove unwanted objects faster and work with RAW images without relying on emulation. At Adobe MAX, Adobe previewed a next-generation image processing engine with extensive GPU acceleration, including interactive Gaussian blur and Light Leaks filters.</span></p>
<p><b>Adobe Substance 3D Painter 12.2</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is now optimized for RTX Spark with native support on Arm for the first time in public beta. Artists in games, visual effects and visualization can run Substance Painter natively on RTX Spark laptops and compact desktops, with no compatibility layer required.</span></p>
<p><b>Adobe Illustrator</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><b>InDesign</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> now offer native Windows on Arm versions optimized for RTX Spark. Adobe Illustrator delivers up to 30% higher performance than its emulated version, helping accelerate illustration, graphic design and page-layout workflows on RTX Spark.</span></p>
<h2><b>Blender 5.3 Adds DLSS Ray Reconstruction to the Viewport</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Blender 5.3 integrates DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction into the Cycles viewport, helping artists evaluate lighting and materials with near-final-quality ray-traced previews as they work. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The practical difference is immediate: instead of navigating a noisy, resolving preview, Ray Reconstruction delivers near-final-quality lighting, reflections and materials in the interactive viewport in real time. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DLSS 4.5 Ray Reconstruction support requires Blender 5.3 and the latest NVIDIA Game Ready Driver or NVIDIA Studio Driver. Download the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/software/nvidia-app/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA App</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to get automatic notifications when the latest drivers are available, optimize games and applications from a unified GPU control center, and discover the latest NVIDIA tools and software.</span></p>
<h2><b>Windows Server 2028 Coming to DGX Station for Windows</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Announced at Microsoft Ignite, Windows Server is evolving for organizations that need to run AI closer to their enterprise applications and data.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft is expanding hardware choice through support for the latest AI silicon, helping customers take advantage of accelerated inference and increasingly capable models in their own environments. NVIDIA DGX Station for Windows platform powered by the GB300 Grace Blackwell Ultra Desktop Superchip is expanding to include support for Windows Server 2028. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">New accelerator platforms and the breadth of the Windows silicon ecosystem provide customers options at different capability levels and price points for advanced AI development, local inference, and agent workloads at scale.</span></p>
<h2><b>Windows ML Updates Give Developers More Options</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Also announced at Microsoft Ignite, developers can now bring any GGUF model from Hugging Face and run it within Windows ML using the llama.cpp backend.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The DxCGC backend, now available as an experimental release, is an integration for game developers that compiles and runs machine learning models through DirectX 12, allowing them to execute AI workloads alongside graphics.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Windows APIs let applications integrate platform-provided AI capabilities adding GPU support for AION Instruct and VSR.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers can also build high-performance AI models for Windows ML using the command line interface. </span></p>

		<title>Ian Buck Kicks Off NVIDIA’s Week at SC26 as Science Enters the Age of Agents</title>
		<link>https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ian-buck-sc26/</link>
		
		<dc:creator><![CDATA[Brian Caulfield]]></dc:creator>
		<pubDate>Tue, 17 Nov 2026 14:00:10 +0000</pubDate>

					<description><![CDATA[NVIDIA’s Ian Buck kicked off the company’s week at SC26 in Chicago on Monday with new tools for AI agents, software that can plan experiments, run code and check results on researchers’ own systems. In NVIDIA’s special address, Buck announced Nemotron for Science, the Rubin HPC GPU and an expanded Vera CPU platform for high-performance [&#8230;]]]></description>
										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA’s Ian Buck kicked off the company’s week at SC26 in Chicago on Monday with new tools for AI agents, software that can plan experiments, run code and check results on researchers’ own systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In NVIDIA’s special address, Buck announced</span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-introduces-nemotron-for-science-to-accelerate-discoveries-with-agents"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron for Science</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the Rubin HPC GPU and an</span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-vera-cpu-platform-to-accelerate-scientific-discovery"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">expanded Vera CPU platform for high-performance computing</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. He also detailed a new collaboration in quantum computing with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-collaboration-with-ibm-and-fermi-national-accelerator-laboratory-to-launch-quantum-classical-codesign-platform-plus-error-correction-breakthrough-to-advance-practical-quantum-computing"><span style="font-weight: 400;">IBM and Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“The frontier of computing and the new scientific stack is going to be simulation, as always, but combined with AI and the future of quantum,” said Buck, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing at NVIDIA. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck opened the talk by looking back to his first public talk on CUDA, at an SC06 tutorial in Tampa, Florida, 20 years ago. NVIDIA now offers more than 1,000</span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/20-years-cuda/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">CUDA-X libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, he said.</span></p>
<p><b>Open Models for Science Agents</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“[Agents] have been just a game changer in how we interface with our supercomputers, with our devices, our instruments and how we can do the research,” Buck said.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Simulation remains the foundation of scientific computing, “which will always be on the top,” Buck said, with AI, quantum and now agents working alongside it.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron for Science combines the Nemotron 3.5 Super VL open model with a new open chemistry dataset and post-training recipes for life sciences and chemistry, Buck explained. Researchers can run it locally on a DGX Spark, a workstation or their own supercomputer.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At Argonne National Laboratory, Buck said, an agent built with the toolkit took a single prompt, planned and tested hypotheses, chose which simulations to run and identified two new binding sites.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“You own your data. You own the model,” Buck said.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck highlighted other agents already in use:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>GROMACS:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> An agent from KTH Royal Institute of Technology repairs broken GROMACS setups on its own. In one overnight run on a single DGX Spark, it fixed 81% of test cases.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>SETI Institute:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> At the Allen Telescope Array, an agent took a one-line prompt to plot a methanol line. Buck explained that it then retuned for Doppler shift on its own [NGC 869 vs. 896, check slide].</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Nsight AI Porting Agent:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> NVIDIA’s new agent ported a routine from the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model to GPUs, Buck said, speeding it up more than 5x using about 150 million tokens.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><b>CPUs for the Agent Loop</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Agents lean on CPUs to run tool calls between model steps, Buck explained.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“That’s exactly what agents need,” he said. “They need a CPU that can run, evaluate, run the tool, perform the operations, gather the results.” </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DKRZ, the German Climate Computing Center, has selected Vera CPUs for its HLRE-5 supercomputer, expected in early 2028. Buck said it joins Vera systems already announced at Texas Advanced Computing Center. </span></p>
<p><b>A New GPU for Science</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rubin HPC is a new GPU in the Vera Rubin platform, built for science, engineering, analytics and AI, Buck said. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It supports precisions from FP64 to FP4, adds sparse-data acceleration and connects over seventh-generation NVIDIA NVLink. With more than 50,000 CUDA cores, it has about three times as many as Hopper, Buck said. It is expected in 2028.</span></p>
<p><b>Quantum Error Correction</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck said a simulation on Jülich’s JUPITER supercomputer showed that a technique called magic state cultivation can cut the operations needed for one logical step tenfold. The work is being added to CUDA-Q.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA, IBM and Fermilab announced plans today for a quantum computing co-design platform to support error-correction and applications research. NVIDIA and IBM are pairing NVIDIA DGX B200 systems with IBM quantum computers, including those powered by IBM Quantum Nighthawk. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck also introduced a new breakthrough for quantum decoding. The goal, Buck explained, is to cut decoding latency from about 300 microseconds to 10. A new CUDA-Q decoding algorithm, TCBP-JOBS, cuts error-correction research computation from 12 weeks to 45 minutes, he said.</span></p>
<p><b>Networking</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck also showed one of the first deployments of </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/lambda-photonics/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Photonics co-packaged optics switches, at Lambda</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. He said they are 15x more reliable on bit errors and link flaps. </span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At SC26, an annual supercomputing conference taking place this week in Chicago, NVIDIA is introducing the DGX Spark HPC Developer Kit for researchers, academics and developers.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The kit is a four-node cluster of </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/dgx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DGX Spark systems</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, each connected directly to its two neighbors in a ring, with an NVIDIA HPC SDK installation playbook coming soon. Researchers get a place at their desk to run multi-node scientific workloads, AI models and agents, instead of waiting for time on a shared GPU cluster. The kit puts the GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip on researchers’ desks. </span></p>
<h2><b>A Supercomputing Lab on Any Desk</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The DGX Spark Developer Kit changes how researchers work.</span></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It scales from one system, or node, to four — linked directly to one another over NVIDIA ConnectX-7 networking, with no network switch to buy or set up. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At four nodes, it provides 512GB of combined memory and 16TB of NVMe storage that provides researchers more capacity to distribute models and scientific workloads across the cluster. With NVFP4, researchers can deploy models with up to 700B parameters locally across the four DGX Spark clusters, such as DeepSeek V4.1.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With that much power at the desk, researchers with a fixed compute and server allocation no longer have to wait in a queue for every job. Teams can develop and debug on their own, saving server hours for production runs and keeping unpublished results, and proprietary datasets on premises.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The playbook for NVIDIA HPC SDK is coming soon, bringing GPU-accelerated C, C++ and Fortran compilers, libraries and message passing interface tools to the DGX Spark cluster — the same development tools used on NVIDIA-powered supercomputers. Teams can build and test scientific applications across four nodes, check how computation and data are distributed, and debug communication issues before scaling to larger systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The kit brings scientific simulation and AI models and agents to the desktop, with demonstrated four-system workloads including LAMMPS with Kokkos for materials simulation and NAMD for molecular dynamics. The broader DGX Spark community has validated GROMACS for molecular dynamics, OpenFOAM for fluid dynamics, WRF for weather modeling and NVIDIA NIM microservices such as OpenFold3 and Boltz-2 for predicting biomolecular structures.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Running on DGX Spark, new open dataset and post-training recipes for </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-introduces-nemotron-for-science-to-accelerate-discoveries-with-agents"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron 3.5 Super VL</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> help researchers adapt the model for life sciences and chemistry, enabling secure scientific workflows on infrastructure organizations control.</span></p>
<h2><b>What Researchers Can Do With It</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The kit opens up new possibilities for researchers. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Researchers can explore agent-assisted workflows that prepare simulation inputs, launch runs and summarize results, while the scientists validate the setup and conclusions.  </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Researchers can also train AI models that approximate slow simulations, then use them to refine wing designs toward minimum drag. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before committing supercomputer time, a team can narrow a 10-million-cell simulation to the 50-500 dimensions that matter.</span></p>
<ul>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://www.kvantify.com/blog/nvidia-dgx-spark"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kvantify</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using NVIDIA cuQuantum, and reports running tensor-network simulations of quantum-chemistry circuits with up to 70 qubits and 975 gates on a single DGX Spark, benchmarking circuits generated for penicillin ground-state energy calculations.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/case-studies/cyran-iit-delhi/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">CYRAN AI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, an IIT Delhi spin-out, uses DGX Spark and its PixelFlux library to accelerate JPEG 2000 decoding for large satellite images, supporting local geospatial analysis.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://openfold.io/blog-finetuning-openfold3.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenFold</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> shows how researchers can fine-tune OpenFold3 on NVIDIA DGX Spark to improve protein-ligand structure predictions for drug discovery.</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.therubinlab.org/research"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rubin Lab at IGI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> at University of California, Berkeley is using a DGX Spark and NVIDIA Evo 2 model to predict bacterial traits directly from genome sequences by comparing embeddings from genomic and protein language models against the classical baselines. With no 11-hour cluster queue times, results arrived 15x sooner on DGX Spark.</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://hartwig.cchem.berkeley.edu/publications/?topic=10"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hartwig Group at UC Berkeley</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using a DGX Spark to perform LoRA fine-tuning on the Nemotron 3 Super 120B-A12B model for chemistry machine learning, training a prediction head on experimental values. By running locally on the Spark using NVFP4 quantization, the lab successfully fit a 120B-class model on a single device and bypassed multi-day cluster queue times, allowing them to run continuous eight- to 24-hour training fits back-to-back.</span></li>
</ul>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Partners include Acer, ASUS, Dell Technologies, GIGABYTE, H3C, HP, Lenovo, MSI and xFusion.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">An NVIDIA playbook for building and running the NVIDIA HPC SDK, a software development kit for scientific computing, is coming soon. Once available, it will give scientists and researchers a local platform to develop multi-node GPU applications, explore FP32 and mixed-precision algorithms and run molecular dynamics and materials simulations using LAMMPS with Kokkos before scaling to data center systems.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p>Deb Shoquist leads operations at NVIDIA, overseeing the supply chain and manufacturing behind its AI platforms. That description sums up the job, but it doesn’t begin to encompass what Shoquist has made possible over decades.</p>
<p>Shoquist, her team and their global network of manufacturing partners enable NVIDIA to introduce world-leading technologies year-round.</p>
<p>Much of the team’s success comes down to her deep relationships with some of the world’s most accomplished manufacturers, including TSMC, Foxconn, Wistron, SPIL, King Yuan Electronics (KYEC), Unimicron, Lumentum, Coherent and Amkor. TSMC has partnered with NVIDIA since 1998. SPIL and KYEC were assembling and testing its chips more than two decades ago.</p>
<p>For Shoquist, those relationships grow stronger through each challenge solved together on the factory floor, as every generation of technology demands more from the people and tools behind it.</p>
<p>Ask her about the global network she relies on, and she starts with the people. There’s the supplier in Taiwan so eager to get production moving that he calls her at 3 a.m. with a fix to the latest problem. There are the factory workers in Vietnam whose only ask of her are more tools to get more work done. She remembers the partners that supported NVIDIA when it was small, and the work they’ve done to help each other grow.</p>
<p>Shoquist meets with key suppliers quarterly and spends six weeks each year on the road visiting partners in Taiwan, China, Korea, Vietnam and the U.S., where conversations move from meeting rooms to factory floors.</p>
<p>Recently, she paused her travels to accept the <a target="_blank" href="https://designthesolution.org/lisa-su-woi-award/">2026 Dr. Lisa Su Woman of Innovation Award</a> — and to share how she finds the right people and companies to imagine and produce at massive scale technology that has never been built before.</p>
<figure id="attachment_100023" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-100023" style="width: 1920px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="wp-image-100023 size-full" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1.jpeg" alt="" width="1920" height="1080" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1.jpeg 1920w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-960x540.jpeg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-1680x945.jpeg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-1280x720.jpeg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-1536x864.jpeg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-1290x725.jpeg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-630x354.jpeg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-300x169.jpeg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/20261114_033237000_iOS-1-400x225.jpeg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1920px) 100vw, 1920px" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-100023" class="wp-caption-text">Foxconn</figcaption></figure>
<p><strong>Q: How would you describe your job to someone outside NVIDIA?</strong></p>
<p>A: At the most basic level, our job is to convert designs into the physical world. Our design team creates a blueprint, and our job is to transition it into a reliable, high-performance supply chain.</p>
<p>A matchmaker is probably the best analogy. You have to find the partner that matches your ramp style and has the technology that can meet the needs of very precise designs. The supplier has to be financially strong enough to invest in our ramps. They have to be highly technical. And they have to be willing to do something that has never been done before.</p>
<p>If you don’t have all three, you can’t create products at the volume and speed that we need. You need those three legs, and in some cases, if you only have two, you have to invest in the company to make them a partner.</p>
<p><strong>Q: What’s an example of that in practice ?</strong></p>
<p>A: Amphenol’s cable cartridge is the most complex interconnect assembly they have ever built. When I recently visited the Vietnam factory, now in full production, I was struck by the level of precision required to build a product of this complexity.</p>
<p>In this large cable cartridge, there are 1,500 small cable legs. Each one requires a very precise assembly to yield a quality level of less than 1 DPPM, or defect part per million. Amphenol built over a hundred automated lines to produce this product at the speed and scale we require. That takes know-how and technology, but it also takes heart, willingness and dedication.</p>
<p>Creating anything of that complexity and precision with a supplier forges the partnership. The partners NVIDIA maintains are the ones who supported us when we were small, and yet are willing to scale with us today.</p>
<p><strong>Q: What changed as NVIDIA went from selling chips to building systems and platforms?</strong></p>
<p>A: When we were primarily a chip manufacturer, we were small, and the semiconductor industry was at scale. In that environment, it was about picking the best technology match for the products we envisioned.</p>
<p>As we became a data center systems company, we needed more partners. But we’ve also now entered into new technology areas where the scale of the supply base is still developing.</p>
<p>For example, our optical component partners have the technology and the willingness, but they don’t yet have the scale or capital to build fabs for the volume we need. To grow, we needed to support and invest, like we did with <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-coherent-announce-strategic-partnership-to-develop-optics-technology-to-scale-next-generation-data-center-architecture">Coherent</a> and <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-strategic-partnership-with-lumentum-to-develop-state-of-the-art-optics-technology">Lumentum</a>.</p>
<p>Beyond that, with products like CPO (co-packaged optics), we’re creating entirely new supply chains with scale partners that are new to the optics world. Leading the integration of talent across the supply chain is the key to driving this incredible technology to high volume quickly.</p>

<p><strong>Q: You clearly enjoy spending time with supply partners, and in particular on the factory floor. What have you learned there over time that can’t be learned anywhere else?</strong></p>
<p>A: Over 20 years, you meet many individuals key to the success of your supply. You meet not only the CEOs, but also the people that are running the production floor. These are the people that really know the problems of manufacturing.</p>
<p>If you don&#8217;t have a tight working relationship with the people deeper in the organization, then it’s hard to have an open dialogue about any challenges. But when you have tight relationships, they want to help you, and they feel comfortable enough telling you, “I need better technical support on the floor. I need more quality engineers. We need a better design. We need to automate this. We need more buffers of material.”</p>
<p>That’s what 20 years of working with our suppliers has taught us. It’s year after year of learning from people, getting to know people deeper in the organization and creating the trust where they can tell us what we need to do to be successful with them.</p>
<p><strong>Q: Demand for NVIDIA technology is already massive and growing. Your job is to make sure NVIDIA’s volume can keep up. How do you make it happen?</strong></p>
<p>A: It goes back to relationships. When you decide that you&#8217;re going to push a line hard to increase volume, you have to have the relationships. You have to know their processes in detail. You have to know their people in detail, and they have to trust you. Only then are you in a position to ask them for special favors, because you can do it surgically, and your ask is credible and reasonable.</p>
<p>And when you’re successful together, they&#8217;re happy, you&#8217;re happy, and the appreciation for the partnership deepens. We all appreciate those that make us successful.</p>

<p><strong>Q: How do you prioritize resources across the supply chain?</strong></p>
<p>A: You need to first understand what the most critical components are, and protect and prioritize production around them. We need wafers like we need air. And the critical component is the TSMC chip. As it comes out of assembly every week, we decide what factory gets what chip.</p>
<p>Every week, we look at factory performance. If a particular factory yield is low, if their time of ownership is not at the level we want, they don’t get more chips that week.</p>
<p>We keep track of every chip, from the identification number on the die all the way to the customer invoice. If you want to be our supplier, you have to support that traceability process. With that process, we took the time it takes from chip out at TSMC to system out at Foxconn and Wistron from five to two weeks when supply and demand requires it.</p>
<p>Think of the supply chain as a large circulatory system with pathways everywhere — and when you need to accelerate any branch of it, you have the power to do that. We have the capability to do that. It’s empowering and exciting.</p>

<p><strong>Q: What’s an example of a time things didn’t go as planned? How did you, the team and your partners find a way forward?</strong></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To train an AI model, thousands of GPUs have to work together, requiring a network that’s fast, efficient and resilient.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lambda</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is tackling the network piece of the puzzle with NVIDIA Photonics co-packaged optics (CPO) switches. The cloud provider is using the switches to connect </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gb300-nvl72/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA GB300 NVL72</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> racks with more than 10,000 GPUs, delivering lower power, higher resiliency and longer mean time between interruptions (MTBI) to an AI factory at scale.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“Networking has to keep pace with the demands of increasingly large AI clusters,” </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">said Dave Ward, president of cloud services at Lambda.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> “Bringing co-packaged optics into a deployment of this scale is an important step toward building the infrastructure needed to support the next generation of AI.” </span></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href=" https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In the company’s tests using the switches, network latency — the time needed for moving data — dropped by about 4% at rack scale and by up to about 10% at pod scale. Across rack, scalable unit and pod configurations, the network maintains the same throughput as conventional pluggable optics.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="Lambda Deploys NVIDIA Photonics in Production AI Factory" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/_SuSm8tu5AY?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<h2><strong>Delivering Power Efficiency and Resiliency at Scale</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Improving power efficiency and resiliency matters because the network sits at the center of AI factory productivity. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In a large training job, one interrupted link can stall thousands of GPUs. With agentic inference, one network disruption can introduce cascading latency effects on many agents. Every interruption costs time, and every watt spent on networking is a watt not available for compute. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Photonics addresses both power efficiency and resiliency. By replacing pluggable transceivers with co-packaged optical engines on the switch package, it delivers up to 5x better power efficiency and up to 15x higher reliability than conventional pluggable optics. It also uses 4x fewer lasers, which means fewer components that can fail.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Cooler Optics, Replaceable Light Sources Increase Resiliency</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lambda’s deployment uses NVIDIA Photonics switches, each with 144 ports running at 800Gb/s. Liquid cooling carries heat away from the switch chip and optical engines. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The company reports a median optics temperature of 36°C, compared with 51°C for conventional pluggable optics, and its tests find more stable signal integrity.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Photonics also brings the electrical-to-optical conversion closer to the switch chip. Each switch has 18 removable external light sources, which supply light to the optical engines. Optical engines sit alongside the chip on the same package and convert electrical signals into light that carries data over fiber between parts of the cluster. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Technicians can replace the light-source modules in the field, unlike traditional pluggable optics, which require replacing the entire module if a laser fails.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Co-packaging shortens the electrical path from inches to millimeters, which reduces signal loss. It also removes the need for retimer chips, cutting both power draw and the number of parts in the signal path.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Quantum processor deployments at supercomputing centers have grown nearly sevenfold over the past five years, with nearly 30 supercomputing centers around the world now in the process of deploying over 50 quantum processors. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many of these centers use </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/software/slurm/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Slurm</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to run scientific supercomputing workloads, so expanding its support for quantum processors builds on familiar workflows and tools, enabling computational chemists, nuclear physicists and materials scientists to bring quantum computing into their research domains without friction.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In a new release, Slurm builds on previous work with IBM, Pasqal and the wider quantum computing community to allow QPU builders to create a single interface to their hardware for every supercomputing center, through a simple file. This makes it easier than ever for HPC centers and QPU builders to integrate and serve their hardware. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The expansion is already being adopted by </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alice &amp; Bob</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">IBM</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">IQM</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Pasqal</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Quantinuum</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">MQSC</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and supports the widely used</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">QRMI</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">QDMI</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">interfaces.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href=" https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2><b>Beating the Bottleneck</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Until now, integrating a QPU into a supercomputing center meant a new software challenge for every new QPU or supercomputing center. By building on existing quantum-HPC interfaces such as QRMI and QDMI, the Slurm expansion allows QPU builders to reuse their integrations across all their deployments. It also means that supercomputing centers can add new quantum processors to their existing scheduling and resource-management workflows without developing bespoke QPU-specific solutions.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For quantum computing to advance science, it needs to be used by scientists. Now scientists can run quantum-classical applications developed with platforms such as NVIDIA CUDA-Q with Slurm coordinating access to all the processing they need.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Slurm 26.11 will be available later this month. </span></p>
<h2><b>Orchestrating Resources in an Open Quantum-GPU Platform</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/integrating-qpus-into-supercomputing-centers-using-slurm-hres-plugins/">Slurm expansion</a> adds a community-developed orchestration layer to NVIDIA’s open platform for quantum-GPU supercomputing, which centers around the CUDA-Q open platform for hybrid quantum-classical computing. At SC26, new releases expand all aspects of the platform:</span></p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q QEC</strong> allows researchers developing quantum error correction to rapidly prototype and test new ideas, including with a new <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/blogs/blog/2026/11/16/cudaq-qec-decoding-server/">decoder server</a>. A new TCBP-JOBS decoder is 13,000x faster than the industry standard.</p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q</strong> gets an expanded layer of fault-tolerant primitives that application builders can use to realistically compile their ideas. Fermilab has already used CUDA-Q to perform studies in three weeks that would normally have taken five months.</p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q Algorithms </strong>provides reusable components for builders of quantum applications, including new capabilities in helping design the loading data into quantum circuits and tools specifically for chemistry and materials applications</p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q Pulse</strong> gives quantum hardware builders a layer for <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/blogs/blog/2026/11/16/introducing-cudaq-pulse/">shaping the ‘pulses’</a> needed to control qubits on a QPU, including tools to GPU accelerate their generation.</p>
<p>The quantum ecosystem around the world is continuing to build with this platform. Quantum Motion integrated its QUICHE chemistry framework with CUDA-Q. Altera and Oxford Quantum Circuits designed an FPGA-based controller connecting qubit control and readout to GPUs through NVQLink.</p>
<p>At supercomputing scale, QunaSys used cuQuantum and 1,024 NVIDIA Hopper GPUs on ABCI-Q to simulate more than 300 million quantum operations, reaching chemical accuracy for a four-hydrogen-atom model. <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA researchers used </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">JUPITER</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to simulate 104 qubits under realistic noise, testing fault-tolerant designs before they’re built.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA also ran the benchmark on 10,240 GPUs with GB300 systems, representing the largest scale in this round. Microsoft Azure matched that scale with 10,240 NVIDIA GPUs with GB200 systems. </span></p>

<h2><b>NVIDIA Vera Rubin Leads Agentic Inference on MLPerf Endpoints</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">An AI agent for travel that plans and books a whole trip — or an IT agent that finds and fixes a problem before anyone files a ticket — may reason, call tools and check its work many times to finish a single task. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Each of those steps generates tokens. Because AI data centers are limited by power, the more tokens a system can generate each second for every watt of power, the more agents it can serve and the more value it creates. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">MLPerf Endpoints tests systems on real agentic coding and enterprise workflows, and checks that the agents still complete their tasks correctly. NVIDIA Vera Rubin leads across all of its agentic benchmarks.</span></p>

<h2><b>Bringing Black Holes Into Focus</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Seeing a black hole takes a telescope the size of Earth. The Event Horizon Telescope links radio dishes worldwide, generating petabytes of data that can take months to process.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With NVIDIA cuPhoton.xBeam, a module within the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/high-performance-computing/ai-for-science/cuphoton/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">cuPhoton Toolkit</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, black hole observations from the Event Horizon Telescope can now be correlated orders of magnitude faster than ever. Analysis that once took over a month now takes eight minutes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">By combining global radio dishes into Earth-sized aperture, cuPhoton delivers unprecedented astronomical imaging resolution. cuPhoton successfully correlated observations of powerful black hole jets J1924 and 3C279 in the night sky — making it a reality to reconstruct a Messier 87* black hole galaxy in real time. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The entire pipeline, from data loading and processing to visualization, runs on distributed multi-GPU systems or a compact </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/dgx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DGX Spark</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> system, helping scientists study how gas spirals inward under a black hole’s powerful gravitational pull.</span></p>

<h2><b>Steering Fusion Simulations</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sustaining plasma at millions of degrees is essential to achieving stable, continuous fusion energy. Experiments also generate data faster than traditional pipelines can process it, often leaving scientists waiting until experiments end to analyze results.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Pacific Fusion researchers used NVIDIA cuPhoton.xLens module to extract insights while plasma simulations run, helping them visualize what’s happening as results emerge. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The NVIDIA cuPhoton.xLens module extracts insights while plasma simulations run, helping researchers understand what’s happening as results emerge. <h2><b>Accelerating Materials Research</b></h2>

<h2><b>From SC06 to SC26</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA </span><a target="_blank" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20220706095459/https://www.clustermonkey.net/Conference-Reports/sc06-jeffs-blog-day-1-sunday/Print.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">announced CUDA in November 2006</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and brought it to the scientific community at SC06, giving scientists and developers a familiar, high-level programming model for using GPUs. <h2><b>Honoring the Architects of Progress</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Now, at SC26, NVIDIA is looking back at some of the CUDA luminaries who’ve spearheaded breakthroughs in science and AI:</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, biophysicist Klaus Schulten’s team helped connect GPU computing with molecular biology through </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.download.nvidia.com/compute/academia/whitepapers/Achievement2014_UIUC.pdf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nanoscale Molecular Dynamics and Visual Molecular Dynamics</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, tools for molecular simulation and visualization. In 2013, researchers at Illinois, the University of Pittsburgh and Vanderbilt University used CUDA-accelerated tools to help resolve the HIV capsid’s atomic structure. They combined experiments with simulations on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.ks.uiuc.edu/Highlights/?highlight=2013-06&amp;section=2013"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Blue Waters</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, one of the world’s most powerful supercomputers at the time.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In a critical breakthrough in AI, University of Toronto researchers Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey Hinton used CUDA to </span><a target="_blank" href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c399862d3b9d6b76c8436e924a68c45b-Paper.pdf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">train AlexNet</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on just two NVIDIA GPUs. Their image-recognition system </span><a target="_blank" href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c399862d3b9d6b76c8436e924a68c45b-Paper.pdf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">won the 2012 ImageNet competition</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, helping demonstrate the potential of GPU computing for deep learning. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That connection between computing and scientific discovery is seen with AlphaFold, which predicts protein structures. Demis Hassabis and John Jumper shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for protein structure prediction. Their team’s </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold"><span style="font-weight: 400;">publicly released AlphaFold 2 software</span></a> <a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold/blob/main/docker/Dockerfile"><span style="font-weight: 400;">uses CUDA for GPU execution</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These are just a few of many examples that show how one generation’s work creates opportunities for the next. With 1,000+ NVIDIA CUDA-X libraries, developers are now reshaping industries and giving machines the power to see, learn and reason — from discovering new medicines and designing next-generation factories to advancing autonomous vehicles, modeling financial markets and unlocking quantum computing — and more.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Announced today at SC26, NVIDIA Rubin HPC is a new GPU in the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/rubin/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera Rubin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> platform designed to accelerate simulation and data processing for science, engineering and analytics workflows. </span></p>

<h2><b>Turning Faster Computation Into Faster Discovery</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Across climate models, engineering simulations, genomic analysis and financial risk, scientific computing workloads take a similar shape: an application reaches into an immense dataset, gathers a handful of scattered values, calculates a result and repeats this on an enormous scale. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To help address the challenge, Rubin HPC will offer a balanced design, scaling data movement and compute together rather than increasing compute alone. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That balance is what optimizes Rubin HPC for scientific computing. It keeps the GPU’s record number of CUDA cores supplied with data, so they spend their time computing rather than waiting. This accelerates recurring patterns of every simulation workload — from sparse matrix operations to stencil computations to sorting and reductions. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These patterns are prevalent across data analytics, science and engineering workloads, which is why a purpose-built GPU delivering the broadest acceleration can help unlock cross-industry breakthroughs.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/technologies/cuda-x/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CUDA-X</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> libraries offer a growing set of optimized implementations of complex algorithms for agents to use, including those most relevant to simulation and analytics. Used together with Rubin HPC, the libraries can give agents the fastest glide path to reasoning outcomes. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And faster agent-invoked simulations mean faster feedback — shortening the loop of hypothesis, simulation and refinement, so researchers and engineers can choose next steps sooner and point lab work at the most promising candidates.</span></p>

<h2 id="proximie" class="wp-block-heading"><b>Proximie Helps Surgical Teams Spend Less Time on Logistics and More Time With Patients</b><b></b></h2>
<h3><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-100065" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-960x536.jpg" alt="" width="960" height="536" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-960x536.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-1680x938.jpg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-1280x714.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-1536x857.jpg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-scaled.jpg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-630x352.jpg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Image-1_OR-Intelligence-300x169.jpg 300w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" /></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">As AI advances healthcare, surgery is a high-stakes, highly regulated setting for its use — and one with significant opportunity for efficiency gains. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Only about 10% of time spent in the operating room is clinical. The rest is devoted to scheduling, turnover and documentation. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">With the help of NVIDIA technology, Proximie is introducing AI into operating room (OR) logistics so that clinicians can focus on taking care of patients.</span></p>
<h3><b>A New Vantage Point</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Proximie is using </span><a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos3/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA Cosmos 3</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, the frontier open world foundation model family for physical AI, to power a platform used in more than 700 ORs today. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Trained on a decade of data, it captures real-time audio, video and device signals from a close-up view of the surgery as well as a wide view of the whole room. The comprehensive view offers context held by no single person in the room, whether the surgeon, scrub nurse or coordinator.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Agentic notifications and voice AI tools can call the next patient when a room is ready, document alerts and capture practitioner notes. To protect privacy, Proximie’s models blur identifying details, such as faces, tattoos and onscreen text before footage leaves the OR. Surgeons control the blurring to ensure that recordings used for training still show what trainees need to see.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Across health systems where Proximie’s technology has been applied, the platform has enabled clinical teams to handle 8% more cases, turn over the OR 45% faster and start procedures on time 26% more often. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Next, the company hopes to turn its technology into an OR copilot that assists with staging equipment ahead of time, estimating time remaining and running implant safety checks. Proximie will also use NVIDIA Cosmos 3 to generate realistic synthetic scenes grounded in real ORs, so models can learn from the rarest, most critical scenarios. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">The goal is to create a room that runs itself, so practitioners can spend more time with patients and dedicate less time to documentation and preparation. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Proximie is working with UC San Diego Health to build an AI model that learns from many kinds of surgical data at once using NVIDIA open models. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Before being trained on Proximie’s data, Cosmos 3 could already identify 80% of key equipment in the OR with high accuracy. Training it further on Proximie’s footage made the model more accurate and smaller, so several models can run at once on a single computer in the OR alongside tools to protect patient privacy.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Proximie is also among the first participants in</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.open-h.org/"> <span style="font-weight: 400">Open-H-Hospital</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, an NVIDIA-led, community-driven open initiative that brings real hospital operation data together with simulation data to train the next generation of hospital AI and robotics. </span></p>

<p><em>Tuesday, Nov. 17, 8:00 a.m. PT <b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/how-ai-is-helping-care-teams/#concertai"><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></a></b></em></p>
<h2 id="concertai" class="wp-block-heading"><b>ConcertAI Matches Cancer Patients to Clinical Trials Faster</b><b></b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-100066" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Picture1-960x459.png" alt="" width="960" height="459" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Picture1-960x459.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Picture1-1680x804.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Picture1-1280x612.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Picture1-1536x735.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Picture1-scaled.png 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Picture1-630x301.png 630w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" />Interest in clinical trials is high among people with cancer. <p><span style="font-weight: 400">The issue isn’t the availability of trials, but the time it takes to match patients to them. Care teams have to manually review months or years of medical records and pull together information like physician notes, pathology reports and genomic results from different systems. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Built on NVIDIA AI, ConcertAI’s Cara AI engine and TriaLinQ module are speeding up that process, identifying eligible patients roughly 3x faster than manual methods. They assess trial eligibility across the CancerLinQ oncology network, ConcertAI’s data network that works across nearly 1,000 sites of care and over 13 million patient records.</span></p>
<h3><b>From Scattered Records to One View</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Cara pulls all relevant patient data into a single view for each person and updates it weekly with disease stage, biomarker status, treatment response and line of therapy. TriaLinQ, a module of the CancerLinQ suite, uses that information to assess a patient&#8217;s eligibility for a trial.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“We use a chain of AI models and agents to parse all the information, understand really challenging concepts like progression or timelines within a diagnosis journey, and summarize it into a structured data model that can be queried from our software stack,” said Eron Kelly, CEO of ConcertAI.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">ConcertAI uses healthcare-specific NVIDIA microservices, software libraries and AI agent tools to accelerate its work.</span></p>
<h3><b>Clinicians Stay in Charge </b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">ConcertAI brings new technology to care teams, but treatment decisions stay with clinicians.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“The promise of technology in oncology isn’t measured by how sophisticated the platform is, but instead by whether it meaningfully supports the clinicians delivering care and positively impacts patient outcomes,” said Dr. Shaalan Beg, chief medical officer of oncology at ConcertAI. “That’s the standard we hold ourselves to: AI that earns its place at the bedside.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Every eligibility finding links back to its source in the patient’s record, so oncologists and care teams can make fully informed treatment decisions. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">By integrating into the way care teams and clinicians already work, ConcertAI’s systems support up to 50% higher trial enrollment, helping more patients find treatment options when they need them. </span></p>

<p><em>Monday, Nov. 16, 8:00 a.m. PT <b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/how-ai-is-helping-care-teams/#artisight"><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></a></b></em></p>
<h2 id="artisight" class="wp-block-heading"><b>Artisight Helps Nurses Prevent Patient Falls With In-Room AI Device</b><b></b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone wp-image-100002 size-large" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Fall-Risk-Dashboard-1680x955.png" alt="" width="1680" height="955" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Fall-Risk-Dashboard-1680x955.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Fall-Risk-Dashboard-960x546.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Fall-Risk-Dashboard-1280x728.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Fall-Risk-Dashboard-1536x874.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Fall-Risk-Dashboard-scaled.png 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Fall-Risk-Dashboard-630x358.png 630w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1680px) 100vw, 1680px" />Roughly 700,000 to 1 million patients fall in U.S. hospitals each year. A fall with injury costs over $14,000 on average and can add days to a patient’s hospital stay. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Artisight’s All-in-One device alerts nurses when a patient at high risk of falling starts to get out of bed. The early warning gives nurses time to reach the patient before they stand — meaning fewer injuries, shorter hospital stays and lower bills for patients, while saving nurses about five hours per unit per day to focus on other care tasks. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">The device uses audio and video to capture data about a patient’s movement that can be used to recognize when a patient moves towards the edge of the bed. When the patient starts to get out of bed, it alerts staff and automatically timestamps and charts the alert. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Each device runs an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-orin/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, a compact edge computer, to process audio, video and movement in the room. Several AI models run at the same time to recognize the movements and to send alerts quickly. AI model performance is optimized with </span><a target="_blank" href="http://developer.nvidia.com/cuda"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA CUDA</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/tensorrt"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA TensorRT</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/triton-inference-server/user-guide/docs/index.html"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA Dynamo-Triton</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400"> managing how the models run. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">That data stays on the device and in the hospital, which keeps alerts quick and protects patient privacy.</span></p>

<h2 id="sword-health" class="wp-block-heading"><b>Sword Brings Dependable AI Guidance to Nearly a Million Members</b><b></b></h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-100080" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-960x444.png" alt="" width="960" height="444" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-960x444.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-1680x777.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-1280x592.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-1536x710.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-scaled.png 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-630x291.png 630w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" /></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Many patients have faced the challenge of deciding whether a new symptom warrants a clinician visit or can be safely managed with the right adjustments at home. Sword, a leading AI health company, built its Phoenix AI health partner to help make that call for nearly a million people and over 2,500 organizations. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">After Sword trained an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400"> open model to its clinical standards for musculoskeletal (MSK) care, Phoenix’s performance improved significantly — making the right call in 10 test runs nearly 80% of the time, up from under 50%. Sword estimates that using an open model costs 5-6x less than frontier models, saving hospitals and patients valuable resources. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“Clinicians don’t trust an AI teammate because it’s right once,” said Megan Hill, head of clinical specialists at Sword. “We trust it because it makes the same call on Tuesday at 1 a.m. that it made on Saturday at 7 p.m.” </span></p>
<h3><b>An AI Teammate That Knows Its Role</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Phoenix works alongside each person’s dedicated clinician. For every message, it chooses to answer or bring the clinician in — and decides whether to speak or stay quiet to respect the clinician and patient’s conversation.</span></p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-100034" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1680x1371.png" alt="" width="1200" height="979" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1680x1371.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-960x783.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1280x1044.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1536x1253.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-630x514.png 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2.png 1999w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Using an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/hgx/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA HGX B300 </span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">system</span><span style="font-weight: 400">, Sword trained Phoenix with reinforcement learning — a technique where the model practices, gets graded and repeats what earns high marks — on de-identified care conversations. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">An AI judge scores every conversation on seven criteria defined by physical therapists, nurses and dietitians on Sword’s team, including tone, clinical boundaries and respect for the clinician’s role. If Phoenix crosses a clinical boundary, the safety score automatically drops to the minimum. </span></p>
<h3><b>Consistent, Repeatable Results</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">To test the model, Sword runs Phoenix on situations where the right call is predetermined, running each situation 10 times. With NVIDIA Nemotron, first-try accuracy for MSK scenarios rose from 78% to 93%, while in women’s health, the model’s accuracy rose from 79% to 87% correct on the first try and from 50% to 72% correct on all 10 runs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Sword can retrain Phoenix whenever it identifies a problem, and member data is kept private. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">This work builds on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://sword.com/newsroom/dawn-1-intelligence-for-mental-health?_storyblok=228222201085072&amp;_storyblok_c=articleNewsroom&amp;_storyblok_version=&amp;_storyblok_lang=default&amp;_storyblok_release=0&amp;_storyblok_rl=1791501236075&amp;_storyblok_tk%5Bspace_id%5D=325797&amp;_storyblok_tk%5Btimestamp%5D=1791501235&amp;_storyblok_tk%5Btoken%5D=57dc446845064c27023fd59adf34f6b03a2edc7a"><span style="font-weight: 400">Dawn 1</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, Sword’s previous model focused on mental health. By pairing open models with rigorous clinical oversight, Sword is proving that AI can expand access to care, extend clinician time for the patients who need it most, and support safe, reliable care at global scale.</span></p>

<p><em>Friday, Nov. 13, 5:00 a.m. PT <b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/how-ai-is-helping-care-teams/#dyania-health"><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></a></b></em></p>
<h2 id="dyania-health" class="wp-block-heading"><b>Dyania Health Uses AI to Turn Decades of Patient Records Into Better Care </b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Hospitals hold decades of patient history, most of it written in free text,</span><span style="font-weight: 400"> as physicians&#8217; notes, operative reports, imaging and pathology reads and discharge summaries. To understand one patient’s full story, a clinician would need to read through years of charts</span><span style="font-weight: 400">. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Dyania Health</span><span style="font-weight: 400">, a New Jersey-based clinical AI company, built Synapsis AI to do just that. Synapsis AI is a clinical reasoning engine: It works across every note, report and lab result in a patient&#8217;s record, connecting findings that may be years apart to answer clinical questions the way a specialist reviewing the chart would. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">The output is a set of clinical signals, each traceable to its source in the record, that tells care teams which patients need attention and why. Clinicians review the evidence and make every decision. </span></p>
<h3><b>Keeping Patient Data Inside the Hospital Walls</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Synapsis AI runs on NVIDIA-accelerated computing inside a health system’s own firewall. The entire health system’s patient records can be processed onsite, so patient data is never shared and never leaves the hospital. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Processing records this way takes chart review off the desks of clinical teams and lets them spend that time with patients. </span><span style="font-weight: 400">But Synapsis AI still requires clinician input — it’s an assistive tool that surfaces evidence from medical records that a clinician must still confirm and act on. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Dyania builds and trains its clinical models with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/products/nemo/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA NeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400"> open libraries, then uses NVIDIA TensorRT and TensorRT-LLM to optimize them to run faster and more efficiently on the hospital’s own GPU servers. </span></p>
<h3><b>Synapsis AI in the Real World</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Cleveland Clinic</span><span style="font-weight: 400"> used Synapsis AI to support a study of more than 7,800 aortic valve replacement patients between 2005 and 2021. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Synapsis AI reasoned across each patient’s record, including every echocardiogram added in the years after surgery, and used this information to build a predictive model of valve deterioration and reintervention risk at the three-, five- and ten-year mark after a patient undergoes an aortic valve replacement procedure. The AI platform completed chart review across the full cohort that would have taken clinicians years to complete by hand. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“Using technology to bring together and analyze information that can help us predict the duration of implanted heart valves is a promising step towards enhanced outcomes and more personalized care for patients,” said </span><span style="font-weight: 400">Dr. Lars Svensson</span><span style="font-weight: 400">, chief of the Heart, Vascular &amp; Thoracic Institute at Cleveland Clinic.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">The same approach can be applied to any healthcare challenge where the answer sits inside the medical record, such as identifying patients eligible for clinical trials, closing care gaps in cardiology and oncology, and populating clinical registries.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p>Kubernetes, the open source system that manages how applications run across thousands of AI factories, has become the foundation for AI, and the demands on it are only growing.</p>
<p>To help meet them, NVIDIA is deepening its commitment to open source at <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/events/kubecon-and-cloudnative-con-north-america/">KubeCon + CloudNativeCon</a>, the flagship conference for the Kubernetes and cloud-native communities.</p>
<p>NVIDIA is contributing <a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/ai-openshell">NVIDIA OpenShell</a>, an open source runtime for securing and governing autonomous agents, to the Cloud Native Computing Foundation (CNCF), a nonprofit that is part of the Linux Foundation and the host of KubeCon. As a CNCF Sandbox project, OpenShell will be governed by the organizations building it into their products and strengthened by the expertise of security professionals worldwide.</p>
<p>NVIDIA is also contributing to <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/agent-substrate-advancing-kubernetes-for-agents-at-scale/">Agent Substrate</a>, an open source Google-led framework for operating long-running agent workloads at scale on Kubernetes that has been accepted into the CNCF Sandbox.</p>
<p>Additionally, NVIDIA announced new components of the <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/">NVIDIA DSX AI Factory Platform</a>: <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/manage-kubernetes-clusters-for-gpu-infrastructure-with-nvidia-kubernetes-engine">NVIDIA Kubernetes Engine</a>, NVIDIA Kubernetes Recipes and <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/start-and-restore-gpu-workloads-in-seconds-with-nvidia-snapshot"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Snapshot</span></a>. These tools help teams deploy, manage and scale GPU infrastructure on Kubernetes for AI factories.</p>
<p>Together, these advancements mark a step forward in making Kubernetes the leading foundation for AI workloads at scale — empowering developers to build safer, more efficient AI infrastructure for the future.</p>
<h2><strong>Securing AI Agents in the Open</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA’s contribution of OpenShell to CNCF puts security infrastructure for autonomous agents in the community’s hands to build, inspect and improve — accelerating development and hardening its safeguards. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“The cloud-native community has a new infrastructure challenge: giving agents secure environments in which to operate,” said Jim Zemlin, CEO of the Linux Foundation. “NVIDIA’s contribution of OpenShell to CNCF gives developers a shared foundation to build on and an opportunity to shape how agent security evolves. Bringing this work into the community enables broader collaboration on infrastructure that will be critical to the next generation of cloud-native applications.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenShell gives organizations control over what AI agents can do. It applies deterministic controls to agent activity, so teams can enforce policies as agents use tools, access inference services and execute workloads. Its extensible architecture lets teams connect their own compute, inference providers, credential storage and monitoring tools. This allows organizations to apply governance across different deployment environments while integrating OpenShell with their existing enterprise systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hundreds of organizations have put OpenShell to use, and the project has received tens of thousands of contributions in less than a year since launch. </span></p>
<h2><strong>Scaling Agent Execution on Kubernetes</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Governing agents is only part of the challenge. Organizations also need to deploy them at scale.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA is contributing to Agent Substrate, a </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Google</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">-originated project in the CNCF Sandbox, and ensuring it works with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/dynamo"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Dynamo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for model inference and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://kai-scheduler.dev/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">KAI Scheduler</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for shared cluster resource allocation. This effort brings together agent execution, inference serving and resource scheduling on Kubernetes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Agent Substrate preserves agent environments between tasks while releasing compute when agents are idle. By suspending and resuming those environments across a shared pool of agents, it lets teams scale agent workloads without keeping dedicated compute running for every session. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Google</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are also collaborating on an integration of OpenShell and Agent Substrate, so enterprises can secure and govern agents across clouds and on-premises systems.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Building an Open Foundation for AI Factories</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI factories need to offer customers managed Kubernetes services while retaining control of their infrastructure, software and customer experience. Three new open source projects extend the DSX AI Factory Platform to make Kubernetes easier to run and inference faster to scale.</span></p>
<figure id="attachment_99798" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-99798" style="width: 1680px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="wp-image-99798 size-large" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-1680x945.png" alt="The NVIDIA DSX AI Factory Platform software stack, from GPU infrastructure and Kubernetes management to workload orchestration and model serving." width="1680" height="945" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-1680x945.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-960x540.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-1280x720.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-1536x864.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-scaled.png 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-1290x725.png 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-630x354.png 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-300x169.png 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsx-stack-kubecon-blog-16-9-400x225.png 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1680px) 100vw, 1680px" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-99798" class="wp-caption-text">The NVIDIA DSX AI Factory Platform software stack, from GPU infrastructure and Kubernetes management to workload orchestration and model serving.</figcaption></figure>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/dsx-ai-factory/nvke"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Kubernetes Engine</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (NVKE) and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/dsx-ai-factory/nvkr"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Kubernetes Recipes</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (NVKR) — now available in public preview — provide open source building blocks for delivering managed Kubernetes services on GPU infrastructure. They automate cluster lifecycle management and provide signed, version-pinned images.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVKE and NVKR are launching with ecosystem support that extends their reach across AI infrastructure platforms and cloud services. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mirantis, Netris, OpenNebula Systems, Rafay</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Spectro Cloud</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">vCluster, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">together with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Firmus </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">SK Telecom</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, have aligned with NVIDIA on integration plans to bring NVKE and NVKR capabilities into their offerings.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/ai-dynamo/snapshot">NVIDIA Snapshot</a> addresses the challenge of bringing additional inference capacity online quickly. Loading models and warming up workloads can delay scaling and recovery. The open source, modular component restores checkpointed replicas in seconds, without model reload or warmup, supporting faster startup, recovery and elasticity for inference and agentic workloads on Kubernetes. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Design partners </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.crusoe.ai/resources/blog/nvidia-snapshot-crusoe-cloud"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Crusoe</span></a> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://saturncloud.io/blog/benchmarking-nvidia-dynamo-snapshot/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Saturn Cloud </span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">have evaluated Snapshot on their own workloads using <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/hgx/">NVIDIA HGX B300 systems</a>. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Crusoe</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, an upstream contributor to Snapshot, tested GLM-5.3 on Crusoe Cloud and reduced the time for the workload to become ready from roughly eight minutes to 31 seconds. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Saturn Cloud</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a token factory platform, benchmarked Snapshot for scale-from-zero inference and reduced vLLM startup times from between two and 8.5 minutes to six and 34 seconds.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Simulation, which has long been the proving ground for robots learning to walk, roll and hop, is now helping researchers teach robots tasks that require a finer touch: aligning gears, connecting cables and assembling small components. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The shift from locomotion to manipulation is taking center stage at this week’s </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/events/corl/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL)</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in Austin, Texas, alongside the rise of agentic robotics — a new paradigm that integrates frontier agents to create interpretable code for physical robotics systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At CoRL, NVIDIA is introducing new open robotics technologies, including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/world-action-model/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">world action models</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, simulation tools, benchmarks and agentic workflows. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Two NVIDIA papers are among six finalists out of thousands of submissions for the Best Paper Awards at CoRL: </span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dair/grail/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">GRAIL</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (Generating Humanoid Loco-Manipulation from World Models), which presents a digital generation pipeline for 3D assets and simulator-ready scenes for robot learning.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/robottt/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">RoboTTT: Context Scaling for Robot Policies</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which introduces a robot model and training recipe that scales visuomotor context three orders of magnitude beyond state-of-the-art policies without growing inference latency.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These advancements help researchers train robots on complex tasks, evaluate their performance across changing conditions and use failures to guide further learning — building the precision, adaptability and autonomy needed to move from lab demonstrations to industrial deployment. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA is working with partners like </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.ambirobotics.com/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ambi Robotics</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bosch Research</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.dexmate.com/blog/an-anatomy-of-coding-agents-tackling-robotics-tasks"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dexmate</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dyna Robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">FieldAI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Flexion</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Noitom Robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Siemens</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="http://skild.ai/blogs/steel-solver"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Skild AI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">RLWRLD</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">UC Berkeley</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Walden Robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">the University of Washington</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to bring these advancements to the broader robotics community.  </span></p>
<h2><b>World Action Models Enable Next Level of Robot Autonomy</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The next level of robot autonomy requires robots to perceive their surroundings, anticipate how their actions will change the environment and execute complex, multistep tasks with a high degree of precision. World action models enable exactly that.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At CoRL, NVIDIA is introducing </span><a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/gr00t-2"><span style="font-weight: 400;">GR00T 2</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, an open frontier world action model built on NVIDIA Cosmos. Trained on diverse robot and human data, including 100,000 hours of </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/nvidia/PhysicalAI-Human-1M-Explorer"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA open source human egocentric data</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the model enables robots to learn dexterous manipulation skills. Key features include:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Out-of-the-box performance:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Completed 40 of 50 evaluated tasks at least once without task-specific demonstrations.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Skill transfer:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Learns from human demonstrations and transfers skills between different robot bodies without robot demonstrations</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Generalization with little data:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Achieved 71% average success across 50 tasks with just one demonstration per task, increasing to 95% with five.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Agent control:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> AI agents can use GR00T 2’s application programming interface and supporting tools to run skills in sequence and recover from errors.</span></li>
</ul>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-1" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/groot-2-shipping-label.mp4?_=1" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/groot-2-shipping-label.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/groot-2-shipping-label.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">GR00T 2 currently ranks among the top three models across all vision language action and world action model categories for the RoboCasa365, RoboLab, RoboDojo and MolmoSpaces benchmarks, with multiple leaderboard wins.  </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nexuni</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is testing it for robotic laundry handling, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Foxconn</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is applying GR00T 2 to factory automation for server manufacturing and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Siemens</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is evaluating it across industrial use cases.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">MicroAGI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using GR00T 2, post-trained through reinforcement learning, to perform Ethernet cable swapping with a YAM robotic arm. Its agentic robotics approach automates the creation and calibration of simulation environments using Isaac Lab 3.0 and Newton, where the policy is trained.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Training capable robots requires diverse data. Open NVIDIA workflows and models equip researchers with better tools for data collection and annotation:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">For researchers building robot-training datasets, NVIDIA’s new open Video to Data workflow turns human video into simulated robot demonstrations, with a </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidia-isaac.github.io/video_to_data/v2d_challenge/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">challenge</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> inviting researchers to advance it.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/nvidia/EgoHand-v1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA EgoHand open model</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> — being demoed at CoRL and with over 40,000 </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hugging Face</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> downloads in the two weeks since launch — reconstructs 3D hand motion, trained with data from </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Noitom Robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></li>
</ul>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-2" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/egohand-demo.mp4?_=2" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/egohand-demo.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/egohand-demo.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2><b>Where the Training and Scaling Happens — in Simulation</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A pretrained model gives robots a core set of skills. Simulation lets researchers train, refine and evaluate skills at scale before deploying them on physical robots. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, an open source robot-learning framework, provides the foundation for that work. Its latest release, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab/releases/tag/v3.0.0"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Isaac Lab 3.0</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, allows researchers to use agents to quickly set up training environments with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/simready/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">simulation-ready assets</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, switch between </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/physx-sdk"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA PhysX</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton physics solvers</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, choose renderers for perception-based training and apply a new reinforcement post-training library to foundation models like GR00T.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/use-cases/physics-simulation-for-robotics/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Accurate physics</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is critical to transferring learned skills from simulation to the real robot. Tighter integration with Newton</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, an open source physics engine, now allows Isaac Lab to model deformable objects such as cables and cloth, along with complex articulated systems such as robotic hands with many interacting joints. </span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-3" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/newton-strawberry.mp4?_=3" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/newton-strawberry.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/newton-strawberry.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><em>Integration with Newton simulates how a strawberry deforms under a robot’s grip.</em></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Together, Isaac Lab and Newton combine accurate physics with scale to enable researchers to train thousands of simulated robots in parallel. In a vision-based dexterous manipulation benchmark, Isaac Lab 3.0 with Newton achieved up to 2.5x the training throughput of Isaac Lab 2.3 with PhysX.</span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-4" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/newton-physx-web.mp4?_=4" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/newton-physx-web.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/newton-physx-web.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Isaac Lab supports robot simulation with Newton and NVIDIA PhysX.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers are extending Newton’s capabilities. At CoRL, </span><a target="_blank" href="http://skild.ai/blogs/steel-solver"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Skild AI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> announced Scalable Throughput-optimized Engine for Embodied Learning, or STEEL, a new rigid-body physics solver built on Newton to accelerate robot training with Isaac Lab.</span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-5" width="1200" height="598" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/robotics-montage-8s.mp4?_=5" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/robotics-montage-8s.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/robotics-montage-8s.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><em><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Skild AI’s STEEL Solver.</span></i></em></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Flexion</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> showcased how Isaac Lab and Newton help train Reflect, its robot-intelligence model combining reasoning and whole-body manipulation. </span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-6" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/flexion-newton-isaac-lab.mp4?_=6" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/flexion-newton-isaac-lab.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/flexion-newton-isaac-lab.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Flexion uses Newton for RL with Isaac Lab.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">FieldAI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using Newton — as well as Isaac Lab and Isaac Sim, built on NVIDIA Omniverse libraries — to train and validate robot policies for deployment in unstructured real-world environments. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dyna Robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> will demonstrate how parallel simulation in Isaac Lab helps train whole-body controllers for its </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.dyna.co/dyna-2.1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">semi-humanoid Taku robot</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for work in warehouses, hotels and food services.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Walden Robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using Isaac Sim’s photorealistic rendering to train and evaluate its general-purpose robot in virtual environments for manufacturing and logistics, while </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.dexmate.com/blog/an-anatomy-of-coding-agents-tackling-robotics-tasks"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dexmate</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is exploring how coding agents can rehearse and refine manipulation strategies in Isaac Sim before executing them on its Vega robot. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://alpha-nist.github.io/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">AlphaNIST</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, built in Isaac Lab, demonstrates how efficient practice can translate into precision. A single policy learns all 20 tasks on the NIST Assembly Task Board, manipulating  millimeter-scale parts using only a yes-or-no success signal. It achieves more than 98% training success, compared with 81% for separate per-task policies, using roughly 39x less GPU time.</span></p>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-7" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/alphanist-6-16s-web.mp4?_=7" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/alphanist-6-16s-web.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/alphanist-6-16s-web.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">AlphaNIST uses Isaac Lab to train a single robot policy across 20 precision tasks on the NIST Assembly Task Board. </span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Behind those results is </span><a target="_blank" href="https://sgs-rl.github.io/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Success-Guided Sampling</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, or SGS, from </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">the University of Washington</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and NVIDIA. The method focuses practice on tasks a policy succeeds at about half the time, directing training toward skills it’s still learning. With SGS, robot training can scale beyond a million parallel environments. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">University of Texas at Austin’s School of Computing and Texas Robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> uses Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab to train robotic skills in simulation, letting students start developing before physical hardware is available.</span></p>
<h2><b>New Industrial Benchmarks for Robot Evaluation</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Learning a skill is one challenge. Performing it reliably across changing conditions is another. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab-arena"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab-Arena</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, an open source framework for scaled policy evaluation, enables developers to compose diverse and repeatable evaluations, then run policies across thousands of variations in parallel. Analyses expose which conditions cause failures, so researchers know what to fix in their next training run. The developer preview of its latest version, 0.4, is arriving at CoRL. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DexBench and VABAR, two new open benchmarks, are among the first designed to measure robot dexterity and adaptability across industrial tasks using Isaac Lab-Arena and Newton.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://dexbench.ai/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">DexBench</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, developed by </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.rlwrld.ai/en"><span style="font-weight: 400;">RLWRLD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in collaboration with NVIDIA, takes dexterity evaluation off the tabletop and into real-world workflows. Built using NVIDIA Isaac Lab-Arena, it features 15 tasks across four scenes spanning retail, logistics, manufacturing and hospitality, testing generalization across object, spatial and physics variations. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Genesis AI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">mimic robotics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and other industry partners are evaluating DexBench through early access.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Teams can run DexBench on <a target="_blank" href="https://docs.manifold.bifrost.ai/benchmarks/dexbench">Manifold</a>, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bifrost’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> evaluation platform for scaling robotics simulations, with no setup required, built-in failure analysis and free credits for initial evaluations. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://berkeleyautomation.github.io/vabar-website/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">VABAR</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> — the Variational Automation Benchmark for Agentic Robotics — from </span><a target="_blank" href="https://news.berkeley.edu/2026/11/09/recent-advances-in-ai-are-creating-a-paradigm-shift-for-robotics/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">UC Berkeley</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and NVIDIA, evaluates robots across six industrial tasks. Each carries standardized variations, difficulty tiers and success metrics. </span></p>
<div style="width: 760px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-8" width="760" height="570" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/task-tool-sorting.mp4?_=8" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/task-tool-sorting.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/task-tool-sorting.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">A simulated tool-sorting task demonstrates how VABAR evaluates robot manipulation skills for industrial workflows.</span></i></p>
<h2><b>Agents Turn Skills Into Robot Programs</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Successful task completion requires robots to coordinate multiple skills and adapt when something goes wrong. Building reliable programs for these multistep tasks remains a challenge.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Agentic robotics helps solve that challenge.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">UC Berkeley’s</span><a target="_blank" href="https://graph-robots.github.io/gap/"><span style="font-weight: 400;"> new </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Graph-as-Policy</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> (GaP) framework uses agents to connect individual skills into robot programs. Agents test these programs using NVIDIA Isaac open simulation technologies and refine them based on failures. This helps researchers scale development with less data while keeping the programs interpretable for engineers to review and verify before deployment.</span></p>
<div style="width: 960px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-99751-9" width="960" height="600" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/wipe-the-bar.mp4?_=9" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/wipe-the-bar.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/wipe-the-bar.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">GaP uses agents and simulation to turn individual robot skills into coordinated actions, such as wiping a bar.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“To me, agentic robotics is the most exciting technological development since the internet in 1995,” said Ken Goldberg, professor of robotics at UC Berkeley and a lead on the VABAR and GaP projects.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Korea’s Ministry of Science and ICT and NVIDIA hosted the </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">MSIT</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">-NVIDIA AI Startup Partnership Forum at COEX.</span></p>

<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-99794" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-scaled.jpeg" alt="" width="2048" height="1152" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-scaled.jpeg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-960x540.jpeg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-1680x945.jpeg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-1280x720.jpeg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-1536x864.jpeg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-1290x725.jpeg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-630x354.jpeg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-300x169.jpeg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/3A5B0B4C-2FCB-4F7B-96D6-2A56312BBB2C_1_201_a-400x225.jpeg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 2048px) 100vw, 2048px" /></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA also announced an investment in Korean startup </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Point2</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, with additional investments planned in </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Motif Technologies</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">RLWRLD and Upstage. </span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Motif Technologies</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a Seoul-based AI lab, develops foundation models for building AI agents that carry out complex tasks, including Motif-3, a large language model released with open weights.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RLWRLD, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">a physical AI startup based in San Francisco, Seoul and Tokyo, develops robotics foundation models that enable humanoid and industrial robots to perform complex real-world tasks that require five-finger dexterity, such as precisely inserting components, threading nuts onto bolts and kitting parts from moving conveyors. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Upstage</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a Korean enterprise AI company, develops Solar language models and document AI tools that help businesses extract information from complex documents, streamline workflows and build AI applications for everyday operations.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The companies represent work across the </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/ai-5-layer-cake/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">full AI stack</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, from infrastructure to models to robotics applications.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The planned investments build on NVIDIA’s work with Korea’s startup and venture community, including more than 1,000 Korea-based members of </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/startups/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Inception</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a program that helps startups grow with access to preferred pricing, technical expertise and opportunities for investor introductions.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Tapping into the country’s strengths in manufacturing and engineering, NVIDIA is helping advance Korea’s physical AI ecosystem. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The country’s industry leaders are applying accelerated computing, AI and simulation to everything from semiconductor manufacturing to robotics and autonomous vehicles. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These efforts are helping bring physical AI from development into real-world production.</span></p>
<h2><b>Samsung</b><b> Advances Semiconductor Engineering With Accelerated Computing and AI Physics</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Samsung Electronics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is <a target="_blank" href="https://semiconductor.samsung.com/news-events/tech-blog/samsung-showcases-ai-driven-semiconductor-engineering-research-and-collaboration-with-nvidia-at-nvidia-ai-day-seoul-2026/">expanding its collaboration with NVIDIA</a> to advance semiconductor innovation through accelerated computing and AI physics. The work spans materials research, process development and device engineering.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In technology computer-aided design, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Samsung</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is working with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-nvidia-agent-toolkit-with-nvidia-physicsnemo-and-cuda-x-libraries-to-transform-how-the-world-engineers-designs-and-builds"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuISS</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/cudss"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuDSS</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to accelerate computations involving sparse linear systems. The collaboration also extends to GPU acceleration for process and optical simulations. In materials research, Samsung’s sMOL platform has run selected quantum chemical calculations up to 5x faster end to end with</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuest/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuEST</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. Samsung is also exploring</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuequivariance/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuEquivariance</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to speed up tensor operations used in training and inference for atomistic AI models.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Samsung</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is developing chip-scale thermal and mechanical analysis workflows with</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/physicsnemo"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. In one AI homogenization case, combining AI-predicted effective material properties, coarser grids and GPU acceleration resulted in a roughly 1,600x speedup. Simulation time fell from about 18 hours for a fine-grid solve on a 32-core CPU to 41 seconds for a GPU-accelerated coarse-grid solve.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Looking ahead,</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-nvidia-agent-toolkit-with-nvidia-physicsnemo-and-cuda-x-libraries-to-transform-how-the-world-engineers-designs-and-builds"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Agent Toolkit</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> could connect these simulation tools and AI models, helping future engineering agents run analyses and compare design options.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.kakaomobility.com/newsroom/detail/%EC%B9%B4%EC%B9%B4%EC%98%A4%EB%AA%A8%EB%B9%8C%EB%A6%AC%ED%8B%B0-%EC%97%94%EB%B9%84%EB%94%94%EC%95%84%EC%99%80-%EB%A0%88%EB%B2%A84-%EC%9E%90%EC%9C%A8%EC%A3%BC%ED%96%89-%EA%B8%B0%EC%88%A0%ED%98%91%EB%A0%A5-MOU-%EC%B2%B4%EA%B2%B0-411">Kakao Mobility</a> plans to build on NVIDIA’s autonomous vehicle computing and safety technologies to advance development of a level 4 autonomous driving system. <h2><b>LG Electronics</b><b> Advances Robot Learning and Factory Simulation</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA and LG Electronics successfully demonstrated NVIDIA Isaac GR00T-powered robotic manipulation and manufacturing tasks using LG’s CLOiD robot in an actual manufacturing environment. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The collaboration implemented a physical AI data flywheel connecting real-world data collection, NVIDIA Cosmos-based data augmentation, GR00T model training, NVIDIA Jetson AGX Thor onboard computing and simulation-based evaluation, establishing reusable workflows for continued development and validation of CLOiD’s robotic capabilities. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The teams are using this work to refine a repeatable process for training, evaluating and improving robots for manufacturing.</span></p>
