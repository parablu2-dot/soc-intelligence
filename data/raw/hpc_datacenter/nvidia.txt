										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Turning a simulation idea into a working application means assembling assets, connecting physics and rendering, and checking that the scene behaves as intended. Developers are combining frontier AI models with</span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to help carry out that work — building applications for exploring scenarios, investigating failures and improving designs.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers direct AI agents through natural-language instructions, review results and guide changes. Omniverse libraries provide GPU-accelerated physics, rendering and sensor simulation capabilities.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore the projects below to see frontier AI models such as GPT-6 Astra at work, and check back for new examples from NVIDIA teams and developers across the ecosystem.</span></p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="building-model" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Create an Editable Building Model From Site Scans </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#building-model"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-1" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/relay-video-v14-1080p.mp4?_=1" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/relay-video-v14-1080p.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/relay-video-v14-1080p.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Planning warehouse automation requires a model of the building itself, but shelving and machinery can obscure its structure in site scans. Omniverse brings together real-to-sim tools and agent skills to help developers turn real-world captures into editable starting environments for physical AI simulation.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">David Wehr, from NVIDIA’s simulation technology team, built on that work using Astra to develop a workflow for digitally reconstructing a building without its contents. The goal is an editable, simulation-ready building model where developers can add or reposition shelving and machinery to evaluate warehouse layouts and automation plans before making physical changes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Wehr guided Astra to interpret panoramic photos, create building geometry and align it with point-cloud measurements. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/openusd/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenUSD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> stores the models, while </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse’s ovrtx library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> renders results for inspection.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse agent skills</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to help agents build with Omniverse libraries.</span></p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="gas-turbine" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Test Gas-Turbine Operating Scenarios With a Digital Twin </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#gas-turbine"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-2" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4?_=2" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before changing how industrial equipment operates, engineers need a way to explore the consequences. Pradeep Kaushik, a member of the NVIDIA Omniverse developer relations team, directed Astra to build a what-if analysis application around a simulated gas turbine and generator.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The application lets users adjust conditions such as load, ambient temperature and fuel composition, then compare power, efficiency, fuel consumption and equipment response. Six test procedures explore scenarios including load changes, a stuck fuel valve and an exhaust-sensor error.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The prototype gives engineers an interactive way to investigate how operating changes and faults could affect equipment behavior, supporting decisions about which scenarios warrant further testing.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Astra used tools like Cantera for thermochemistry and SciPy for equipment dynamics. The application maps solver results to rotor animation and color-coded temperature, pressure and flow-velocity indicators in a 3D turbine. NVIDIA Omniverse libraries update the scene (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovstage"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovstage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), render it with RTX (ovrtx) and stream the view to the browser (ovstream). Engineers can rotate and zoom the cutaway, switch between indicators, and pause or scrub through a simulated response alongside charts and virtual instruments.</span></p>

<h2 id="simready-assets" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Build Simulation Scenes With Open Source SimReady Assets </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#simready-assets"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-3" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4?_=3" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With SimReady Central Library, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-central-assets-robotics-ai-factories"><span style="font-weight: 400;">announced at GTC Berlin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, developers can access more than 2,000 simulation-ready USD assets from equipment manufacturers and asset providers for robotics simulations and AI factory digital twins workflows.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Paul Kind, a member of NVIDIA’s Omniverse documentation team, pointed an AI agent to the library and guided scene assembly through conversation. His kitchen scene included 180 placements drawn from 63 unique library assets, alongside 18 custom geometry pieces. Kind reviewed renders produced with Isaac Sim and guided further adjustments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For robotics and factory workflows, developers can select SimReady assets with the simulation properties embedded to fit their required task then test in an assembled environment.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/nvidia/simready-central"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Find assets in SimReady Central Library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-central-assets-robotics-ai-factories#prepare-and-contribute-content"><span style="font-weight: 400;">learn how to contribute</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>

<h2 id="warehouse" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Stress-Test a Warehouse Simulation at Scale </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#warehouse"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-4" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4?_=4" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A warehouse simulation needs to keep running smoothly as more robot arms, conveyors and boxes fill the scene. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When it slows down, developers need to identify what’s holding it back. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bhavik Sanganee, principal engineer at NVIDIA, used Astra with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse?size=n_12_n&amp;sort-field=featured&amp;sort-direction=desc"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to build a warehouse simulation, increase its complexity and investigate slowdowns.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Performance first fell below the target when the scene doubled in size. Running physics calculations, image rendering and video preparation concurrently restored performance and enabled further scaling.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With 80 conveyors, 40 arms and 800 boxes, the simulation sustained 30 frames per second at full HD resolution during a two-minute test. The arms completed 640 successful box placements.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Doubling the scene again reduced performance. Testing traced the slowdown to the physics and robot-control pipeline, giving Sanganee a specific area to improve.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start building with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse?size=n_12_n&amp;sort-field=featured&amp;sort-direction=desc"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>

<h2 id="cable-routing" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Test Robot Control for Flexible Cable Routing </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#cable-routing"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-5" width="1200" height="406" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4?_=5" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In electronics assembly, a robot may need to position a flexible cable around components before plugging it into a port. The cable bends as the robot moves it, and the connector must line up precisely to fit.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Oyindamola Omotuyi, robotics technical marketing manager at NVIDIA, directed Astra to build a robot simulation using </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> physics and Omniverse rendering with a</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K2ui16FdLZw"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Robotiq gripper</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> handling the cable.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Astra also developed a</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/reinforcement-learning/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">reinforcement learning</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> system that combined learned cable-routing movements with position-and-orientation feedback and corrective adjustments for USB-C insertion. The simulation gave Omotuyi a way to test cable handling and connector alignment before moving to physical-robot experiments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The controller used information supplied directly by the simulation. Next steps include testing a friction-held grasp and using information a physical robot could obtain from cameras and sensors.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Get started with robot learning in </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>

<h2 id="cad" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Turn Robot Computer-Aided Design Into a SimReady Asset </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#cad"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before testing whether a robot can pick up and move a box, developers need a simulation model with joints that move correctly and with grippers that can hold the object. A computer-aided design (CAD) model provides the robot’s shape, but it still needs physics properties to perform the task.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Using an ABB Robotics YuMi robot, developers directed Astra with NVIDIA Omniverse content libraries to convert CAD files into OpenUSD, match the robot’s appearance to reference images, and configure joints and collision geometry. They checked the asset against SimReady Foundation requirements before testing it in NVIDIA Isaac Sim.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Both arms and grippers completed four cube pick-and-place cycles, using simulated finger contact and friction. These tests demonstrated behavior under the simulated conditions — estimated physical properties were not calibrated against the real robot.<br />
</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Follow the five-step </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/5-steps-to-create-simready-assets-for-robotics-with-frontier-ai-models/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SimReady tutorial</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></p>

<h2 id="humanoid-simulator" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Build a Humanoid Simulator for a Warehouse Environment </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#humanoid-simulator"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-6" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4?_=6" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore a gamified, physics-based control of a humanoid robot in first- and third-person view.</span></em><i></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before automating warehouse tasks, developers need an interactive simulation environment to explore task behavior and evaluate how the work gets done. Frank DeLise, Omniverse product manager at NVIDIA, used Astra to turn a </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/simready/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SimReady</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> warehouse and humanoid robot into an interactive simulator with first- and third-person views.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DeLise directed Astra to connect</span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for physics (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX/blob/main/ovphysx/README.md"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovphysx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), scene updates (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovstage"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovstage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), rendering (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">) and the user interface (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-omniverse/ovui"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovui</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">). He also used Astra with SimReady (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia/simready-foundation"><span style="font-weight: 400;">simready-foundation</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">) to create the physical scene in simulation. Astra then generated animation and application code to bring those capabilities together.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Learn how to </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/5-steps-to-create-simready-assets-for-robotics-with-frontier-ai-models/"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">prepare and validate SimReady robot assets</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with frontier AI models and NVIDIA Omniverse libraries.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="autonomous-driving-testing" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Connect an Autonomous-Driving Testing Workflow </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#autonomous-driving-testing"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-7" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4?_=7" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Changing a scene, sensor or driving model can significantly affect autonomous-vehicle simulations. Doyub Kim, a manager on the simulation technology team at NVIDIA, asked Astra to build Zero to Alpamayo — a reusable simulation environment based on San Francisco’s Market Street.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kim directed Astra to map out the workflow, then connect asset creation, traffic, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/integrate-nvidia-omniverse-rtx-sensor-simulation-into-existing-apps/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse RTX sensor simulation</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/solutions/autonomous-vehicles/alpamayo/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alpamayo driving</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in stages, checking each integration. The resulting prototype was a testing ground for comparing models and tracing how scene or sensor changes affect downstream driving behavior. A separate Cosmos3-Nano experiment varied weather and lighting in recorded simulation videos, allowing Kim to compare the driving model’s responses to the same scenario under different conditions.</span></p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="sensor-differences" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Use Sensor Differences to Create and Improve Digital Twins </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#sensor-differences"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-8" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4?_=8" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Comparing recorded and simulated camera and lidar outputs gives developers key performance indicator-based feedback for refining a scene.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To test robots and autonomous vehicles, developers need to know how closely simulated sensors match real ones. Ashley Reid, who works on RTX sensor validation at NVIDIA, directed Astra and Claude Fable 5 agents to compare </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> camera and raw LiDAR outputs with recorded data. The agents created two digital twins from scratch and improved two existing ones.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Over about three days, Reid guided an iterative workflow in which agents measured differences, created or modified OpenUSD scenes, and checked the results. Changes addressed missing objects, geometry and materials, with acceptance depending on camera and LiDAR metrics. This gives developers a way to use measured discrepancies to guide scene creation and improvement.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start by rendering an OpenUSD scene with the </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx/tree/main/examples/python/minimal"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx minimal Python example</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, then define a sensor measure to compare with recorded data.</span></i></p>

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2 id="robo-olympics" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Robo Olympics: Test Robot Skills With Simulation </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#robo-olympics"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Teaching robots new movements means checking whether those actions work under physical constraints. Tae Kim, who leads NVIDIA Omniverse engineering and product, used sports videos and natural-language instructions to guide Astra in building Robo Olympics, an experimental project that tests simulated Unitree G1 humanoids performing sports movements.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Under Kim’s direction, Astra built controllers and refined them through physics trials. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton Physics Engine</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> simulated behavior, the open source </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/warp-python"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Warp</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> framework accelerated calculations and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> rendered scenes and virtual-camera images. In one experiment, the robot cleared a single hurdle in 64 of 100 simulation trials. The trials gave Kim feedback for improving the robot’s timing and control.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Watch Kim </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbwlRzjLwqc"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">explore building simulations</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with Astra and NVIDIA Omniverse libraries.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="robot-disassembly" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Test Robotic Disassembly With Computer-Aided Design and Simulation </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#robot-disassembly"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before a robot can disassemble a product, developers need to know whether its tools can reach and remove the parts. Jens Jebens, a senior product manager for OpenUSD at NVIDIA, directed Astra to model a car suspension in PTC Onshape and configure it in <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/sim/">NVIDIA</a></span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/sim/"><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Isaac Sim</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With Astra, Jebens explored </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/computer-aided-engineering/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">computer-aided design</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and tooling revisions for robots informed by simulation. The agent measured the available space and designed a wrench the robot could use to reach the suspension’s bolts. Jebens reported successful removal of a suspension component in simulation. This connects design and tooling decisions to disassembly results, offering a starting point for robot policy training.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore the </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.omniverse.nvidia.com/extensions/latest/ext_onshape.html"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Onshape importer guide</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="international-space-station" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Bring the International Space Station Into the Browser </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#international-space-station"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Turning 3D models into an application requires connecting assets, live data and an interface. Nic Johns, an engineering director at NVIDIA, prompted Astra to assemble NASA assets into an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/openusd/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenUSD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> International Space Station model with telemetry. Johns built the application with a single prompt, then used a follow-up prompt to shift the scene to Earth’s daytime side so the planet was visible.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The workflow used Blender for asset preparation and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for rendering (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), scene runtime (ovstage) and streaming (ovstream). The application brings 3D models and operational data into the browser, with Johns guiding its development through prompts and corrections.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Try the </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA/skills/tree/main/skills/omniverse-realtime-viewer"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse Real-Time Viewer skill</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="testing-environments" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Turn Captured Rooms Into Testing Environments </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#testing-environments"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A digitally reconstructed room needs editable objects and accurate physical behavior before developers can test interactions. <p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The workflow combined PyCuSFM, FoundationStereo and nvblox for reconstruction, with user review guiding object selection and placement. Isaac Sim tests guided collision and contact revisions for doors and drawers. The studio connects captured geometry to interaction testing, making gaps and object behavior easier to inspect. </span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Introduced today, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/nvidia/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Transcribe-30B-A4B"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is a new, open weight speech model that turns recorded conversations into useful information. <p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers guide the model with plain-language prompts. For example, for a single recorded meeting, users can ask it to:</span></p>

<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">“Translate this audio into English.”</span></li>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Prompts can also include product terms and formatting conventions, which helps the model handle specialized vocabulary in fields like healthcare, financial services and customer service. 										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">There are 57 million people worldwide living with dementia today. For the researchers working to help them, the obstacle isn’t a lack of effort — it’s too much data, too few experts to synthesize it and a pace of discovery that can’t keep up with the disease. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Consortium for Biomedical Research and AI in Neurodegeneration (C-BRAIN), working with NVIDIA, is using AI to change that.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alzheimer’s disease is one of today’s leading global health challenges, with over 10 million new dementia cases diagnosed each year. Despite a 7x increase in National Institutes of Health funding in the last 15 years — reaching $3.9 billion annually — no treatment has been found to stop, prevent or reverse the disease yet. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The bottlenecks are not enough high impact scientific findings, too much data living in institutional silos and too slow a pace of synthesis to keep up with the research that is published. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">C-BRAIN, led by Dr. Randall Bateman and a consortium of 18 member institutions across academic, philanthropic, pharma and research organizations, launched in November 2025 to help scientists make better, faster discoveries by accelerating the research that makes new treatments possible. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“We have a window of opportunity with artificial intelligence to do research fundamentally different than we’ve ever done before, in a way that accelerates discoveries to help patients,” said Bateman, founder and director of C-BRAIN and the Charles F. and Joanne Knight Distinguished Professor of Neurology at the Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis. He described their work as “AI tools built by scientists, for scientists.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The consortium is building a system that harnesses previously untapped Alzheimer’s research data to support scientists’ work.</span></p>
<h2><b>Speeding Research From Weeks to Hours</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Essential assets in Alzheimer’s research are datasets that have never been publicly accessible to AI tools or human researchers. This includes unpublished findings, negative results and governed proprietary data from member institutions. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“A lot of life sciences data doesn’t make its way into the frontier models,” said Dr. Ade Ojewole, chief technology officer of C-BRAIN. “The consequence of that is we’re leaving a lot of intelligence on the table.” </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">C-BRAIN refers to this as “dark data,” which slows down Alzheimer’s research.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some dark data is directly deposited into secure computer systems for AI tools to access. To use this data without compromising patient privacy or institutional governance, C-BRAIN uses a hybrid centralized and federated approach — where AI models learn from data at each institution without that data ever having to leave its walls. This kind of infrastructure is technically demanding to build and scale, which is part of what NVIDIA brings to the collaboration.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">C-BRAIN currently runs 64 </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA data center GPUs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on premises alongside cloud-based nodes on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft Azure,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with additional NVIDIA GPU capacity, including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/blackwell-architecture/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Blackwell systems</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, on the horizon as the consortium’s ambitions scale.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To explore how AI and accelerated computing can advance Alzheimer’s research while navigating its technical demands, C-BRAIN is working with NVIDIA to incorporate technologies including the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-BioNeMo"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA BioNeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> open developer platform and the MONAI framework for C-BRAIN’s research pipelines.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“The C-BRAIN collaboration with NVIDIA aims to unlock those models in a way that they can deliver something quite impactful and important,” said Bateman. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“A great deal of optimization is going to be needed as we push the boundaries of what today’s spatial omics machine learning architectures can model,” Ojewole said. “NVIDIA software is definitely going to come into play there.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Researchers at C-BRAIN have already built two tools: OpenScientist — built with</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory in an effort led by research scientist Justin Reese — and InsightEngine</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, built with 387 Labs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenScientist is an open source agentic AI co-scientist that takes a scientist’s research question and dataset, then semi-autonomously generates hypotheses, runs analyses and produces verifiable, auditable insights. InsightEngine is an open source research platform that uses agentic AI to test hypotheses against the scientific literature, analyzing user-supplied datasets, exploring a biomedical knowledge graph and checking every claim against evidence to catch hallucinations before researchers commit to costly experiments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In published case studies, OpenScientist completed analyses in minutes that domain scientists estimated would take weeks to months, at a cost of under $10 per run. At C-BRAIN’s current reach of over 200 research groups, this capability could release substantial research capacity, potentially exceeding one million hours annually at scale.</span></p>
<h2><b>From Correlation to Cause</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Looking ahead, C-BRAIN aims to build a multimodal biological foundation model trained on the full breadth of its data — neuroimaging, multi-omics, fluid biomarkers and longitudinal clinical measures. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">By year three, it aims to discover new causal mechanisms of brain aging and Alzheimer’s disease, moving to testable scientific principles using the full scope of available research and data. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bateman said, “</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI that can think and reason has the potential to accelerate discoveries that will lead to powerful and implementable preventions and treatments for the entire population.”</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kazakhstan’s AI ecosystem is moving from infrastructure plans to deployment. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The shift is visible across the full AI stack — from infrastructure to models to applications.</span></p>
<p>The announcements below — coinciding with a visit to Astana by NVIDIA founder and CEO Jensen Huang — point to the nation’s increasing supply of accelerated computing, along with its growing demand for what comes next.</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On Friday, Oct. 23, NVIDIA founder and CEO Jensen Huang visited Astana, meeting with President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev at the Akorda Presidential Palace and joining Deputy Prime Minister Zhaslan Madiyev — Kazakhstan’s minister of AI and digital development — for a public fireside chat at the </span><a target="_blank" href="http://alem.ai"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alem.AI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> program. </span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<h2><b>Jensen Huang and Kazakhstan’s AI Minister Speak at Alem.AI</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In their fireside chat at Alem.AI, Kazakhstan’s international center for artificial intelligence, Madiyev and Huang discussed the country’s push to become an AI nation — building the energy and computing infrastructure to support homegrown AI and applying it across the economy.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Madiyev said Kazakhstan wants AI that reflects its language and culture. “We want AI that understands and speaks Kazakh and captures cultural contexts and cultural specifics,” he said, describing the country’s work on Kazakh-language models. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang said Kazakhstan could turn its energy resources into a foundation for AI production — and a new kind of export. “Instead of just exporting energy, in the future, you could transform energy into intelligence and export intelligence,” he said.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">He also argued that local data is central to that ambition: it embodies a country’s culture, history and values. “Your country’s data is your natural resource. It belongs to you.” </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The conversation also turned to physical AI. Madiyev pointed to Kazakhstan’s factories, mines, agriculture, transport and logistics as potential areas for development, asking how the country could become a hub for training models for robots and other physical systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang called the idea “fantastic,” saying physical AI could transform industries from mining and mineral processing to agriculture. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang said AI and robotics can help people do more, but ambition will shape how fully they seize that opportunity. He applied the idea to Kazakhstan, saying AI could help lift the country and its people.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“If you have ambition, and I believe you do, AI will lift your nation,” Huang said. “AI will lift your people.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Huang also praised the founders of Higgsfield AI, the Kazakhstan-founded startup, and it was announced NVIDIA is making an investment into the startup during the event. “The first thing and the last thing is you invest in people, not companies,” he said, describing the founders as visionary and pointing to their effort to compete globally. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Asked about AI security, Huang said the technology must be developed and deployed responsibly. He described NVIDIA’s OpenShell as a way to contain AI agents by limiting their access to files and tools and monitoring their activity.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-99486" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-scaled.jpeg" alt="" width="2048" height="1152" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-scaled.jpeg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-960x540.jpeg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1680x945.jpeg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1280x720.jpeg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1536x864.jpeg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-1290x725.jpeg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-630x354.jpeg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-300x169.jpeg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/S-17-edit-400x225.jpeg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 2048px) 100vw, 2048px" /></span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For young people entering a fast-changing field, Huang recommended learning how AI works — not just how to use it. He said aspiring AI programmers should understand the technology’s principles, strengths and weaknesses, combining technical knowledge with the ability to work effectively with AI. </span></p>
<p>“I envy being 18 years old today… because the world is changing so rapidly, and there’s a complete reset because of technology.”</p>
<h2><b>The Infrastructure Layer Is Scaling<br />
</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The visits coincided with a signing ceremony marking Firebird’s investment agreement with the government of Kazakhstan, held at the presidential palace. </span><b></b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building on its first AI factory in Armenia, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Firebird</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is expanding into Kazakhstan, where it has secured a U.S. export license for 45,000 NVIDIA Grace Blackwell GPUs. The first phase is under construction with Kazakhtelecom at Data Center Valley, targeting commercial launch in the third quarter of 2027.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A planned second phase would bring Firebird’s total potential AI infrastructure capacity in Kazakhstan to approximately 500 megawatts. Firebird also plans to establish Firebird Labs Kazakhstan to support local AI startups, research and talent development through funding, access to compute and research partnerships.</span></p>
<p>Firebird cofounder and CEO Razmig Hovaghimian attended the signing ceremony at the Akorda Presidential Palace alongside government officials.</p>
<p>“What began as a seed of an idea 180 days ago has grown into a major investment in Kazakhstan’s AI future,” said Razmig Hovaghimian, co-Founder and CEO of Firebird. “President Tokayev’s vision gave us the confidence to think bigger about what we can build together. With Jensen Huang here today, that ambition feels more tangible than ever. We’re building the infrastructure for Kazakhstan’s talent and ideas to reach the world.”</p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://telecom.kz/en"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kazakhtelecom</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the nation’s largest telecommunications company, has already deployed 400 NVIDIA H200 and 480 NVIDIA B300 GPUs in Astana and Almaty. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Next year, the company plans to deploy more than 1,000 GPUs in the <a target="_blank" href="https://dc-valley.com/en">Data Center Valley</a>, along with more than 1,000 NVIDIA Vera Rubin GPUs in the Ekibastuz region, serving diverse workloads with infrastructure that enterprises, developers and institutions can use at scale.</span></p>
<h2><b>An Honorary Black Belt for Huang</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At the end of their meeting, President Tokayev presented Huang with the Order of Dostyk, First Class, for his contribution to the development of artificial intelligence in Kazakhstan. <em>Dostyk</em> means friendship and the Order honors peace, friendship and cooperation.</span></p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-99502" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-scaled.jpeg" alt="" width="2048" height="1152" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-scaled.jpeg 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-960x540.jpeg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1680x945.jpeg 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1280x720.jpeg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1536x864.jpeg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-1290x725.jpeg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-630x354.jpeg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-300x169.jpeg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/3736D9B7-EE59-4167-9E7B-59109DA6DB4C_1_201_a-400x225.jpeg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 2048px) 100vw, 2048px" /></p>

The belt, embroidered in gold stitching, was a special thanks for Huang’s support of Kazakhstan’s AI ambition and for leadership aligned with the spirit and rigor of the martial art.</span></p>
<h2><b>AI-Native Companies Scale With Compute</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And that development is accelerating. The nation’s AI-native companies are also building out their compute capacity — and the applications that run atop them.<br />
</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Higgsfield is expanding its AI infrastructure with NVIDIA Vera Rubin running on cloud service providers, giving the company additional capacity as models, products and customer demand for its generative media applications grow.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://blog.indrive.com/article/indrive-and-pony-ai-announce-collaboration-to-explore-autonomous-mobility-in-kazakhstan-built-on-nvidia-hyperion"><span style="font-weight: 400;">inDrive and Pony.ai</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are also collaborating to explore robotaxi services in Kazakhstan, with Astana as the first deployment city. The companies will assess the feasibility of bringing Pony.ai’s seventh-generation robotaxis to the inDrive app, supported by NVIDIA AI infrastructure, built on the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/solutions/autonomous-vehicles/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Hyperion</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> sensor and compute platform with the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-trust-center/halos/autonomous-vehicles/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Halos</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> safety system.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">More local capacity gives developers and companies room to train, fine-tune and serve increasingly capable models. Successful applications, in turn, create demand for more compute.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kazakhstan’s AI opportunity isn’t defined by GPU counts alone. Connecting that infrastructure with developers, universities, startups and industries can turn accelerated computing into useful applications.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Today’s announcements </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">represent different layers of the AI stack in Kazakhstan— infrastructure deployment, access to compute and AI-native product development — where the AI foundation is reaching new scale.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Launched today, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/networking/products/software/minerva/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Minerva</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is an AIOps platform built to help teams find problems earlier and keep AI factories productive. It brings together telemetry from infrastructure and AI workloads, then applies purpose-built AI models to turn that data into timely, actionable insight.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI factories are among the most complex operations in the world, with vast numbers of GPUs working across high-speed networks to train models and serve AI applications. At that scale, a single degraded component can slow an entire job. The warning signs may be easy to miss, such as a GPU running hotter than its peers or an NVMe drive losing performance under load.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The platform focuses on AI factory yield, a measure of the useful work infrastructure produces through GPU hours, goodput, cluster uptime and token production.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2><b>Delivering a Dynamic View of an AI Factory</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Traditional monitoring tools often divide the data center into separate views. Networking, compute, site reliability and machine learning teams each see a different part of the operation. Minerva connects those signals across NVIDIA GPUs, networking, storage, schedulers and workloads.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With these signals, Minerva builds a dynamic knowledge graph that logically maps the infrastructure — GPUs, SuperNICs, switches and cables — and the jobs running on it. This context helps operators trace a slow training job to the component affecting it, even across a factory with hundreds of thousands of GPUs.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It analyzes billions of data points per hour using supervised and unsupervised machine learning. It learns normal behavior, detects previously unseen anomalies, predicts emerging failures and correlates related events based on the factory’s topology. Instead of sending every symptom to an operator, it can group an event storm into a focused incident with a likely root cause.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Minerva comes with a native AI assistant and connects to external agentic AI tools. This adds a natural-language interface grounded in live telemetry. Operators can investigate cluster health and receive recommended remediation through conversation. Teams can keep a person in the loop to manually implement fixes or connect Minerva to automation through infrastructure application programming interfaces and external systems.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Results That Maximize AI Factory Yield</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The ability to correlate workload and infrastructure issues along with its agentic AI capabilities help simplify AI factory operations. Instead of dealing with a blizzard of alerts, Minerva aggregates related problems into a single actionable item. This allows AIOps teams to move from reactive to proactive maintenance. And all of this helps AI factories maximize GPU uptime.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Minerva delivers value for the key metrics AI factories focus on:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><b>Longer time between interruptions</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Minerva spots trouble before it happens, so jobs run longer without incident.</span></li>
<li><b>Faster time to repair</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: When an incident does happen, Minerva pinpoints the problem, leading to a significantly quicker resolution.</span></li>
<li><b>Greater insight</b><span style="font-weight: 400;">: Modern AI infrastructure generates a flood of data; Minerva filters it out, leaving only the handful of alerts that actually matter.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">IREN is among the NVIDIA partners using Minerva to optimize its AI factories. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“As we scale our AI factories, the key to providing the best service for our customers is maximizing the productivity of our GPUs,” said </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Denis Skrinnikoff, chief technology officer of IREN. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">“NVIDIA Minerva gives our operations team one view across the network, the GPUs and the jobs running on them, so we focus more on proactive maintenance rather than troubleshooting. 										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At GTC Berlin, banks, payment companies and financial data providers are showing what production-scale intelligence looks like — built on NVIDIA accelerated computing, powered by open weight models and running on their own infrastructure.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Revolut</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is rebuilding its technology stack on a single foundation model </span><a target="_blank" href="https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-financial-services-industry/revolut-builds-a-transaction-foundation-model-1185435"><span style="font-weight: 400;">PRAGMA</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, powered by NVIDIA accelerated computing. Using the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/topics/ai/data-science/cuda-x-data-science-libraries/cudf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuDF</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> toolkit and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/dynamo-triton"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dynamo-Triton software</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/blackwell-architecture/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Revolut is scaling PRAGMA in production, servicing an initial 80 million customers and engineered to scale 3x.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“Revolut processes billions of transactions — the opportunity was always there, but the question was whether foundation models could operate at that scale and across domains. PRAGMA answers that,” said Pavel Nesterov, head of AI at Revolut. “Built in collaboration with NVIDIA, it’s the clearest proof yet that the gap between a research breakthrough and a production banking system can be closed.”</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<h2><b>Training on Accelerated Infrastructure</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Starling Bank</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the first bank to train an agentic model on U.K. sovereign cloud infrastructure, has developed “Starling Intelligence” — the intelligence layer that underpins its AI capability — on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Civo’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> U.K. cloud using the bank’s own transaction data. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lloyds Banking Group</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> built </span><a target="_blank" href="https://medium.com/ai-at-lloyds-banking-group/introducing-atum-a-foundation-model-for-banking-4d5291577d50"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ATUM</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a transaction foundation model that converts billions of banking transactions into reusable intelligence — accelerated with NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs and improving efficiency across bank-wide use cases.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">SBS</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> launched a banking sovereign intelligence platform so banks can run generative and agentic models without their data leaving their own walls.</span></p>
<h2><b>Open Models at a Fraction of the Cost</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Open weight models give European financial institutions proprietary intelligence built on data they own, at costs they control. NVIDIA’s accelerated computing platform is the engine running beneath them.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Neobank </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">bunq’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> agentic assistant Finn now runs on open weight models. Powered by NVIDIA accelerated computing, deploying on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nebius</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> matches the quality of leading closed models at 60% of the cost per answer. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NatWest</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is building technology designed to recognize the full range of British accents, dialects and financial terminology, developed with NVIDIA’s speech models, including Nemotron 3.5 ASR. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Modulr</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is building specialized models for distinct parts of payment operations, based on the NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B open model.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Santander</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is an early adopter of the new NVIDIA Kumo family of open weight foundation models for structured financial data, using them to build specialized intelligence for core banking workloads.</span></p>
<h2><b>Accelerated Computing Transforms Financial Research</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">LSEG</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is working with NVIDIA to help financial institutions analyze large volumes of historical tick data up to 10x faster with the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.pola.rs/user-guide/lazy/gpu/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Polars GPU engine</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, accelerated by NVIDIA cuDF on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RavenPack</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> built a dataset generation pipeline on Bigdata.com that takes a plain-language research hypothesis and returns a labeled, backtest-ready dataset — running on a fine-tuned NVIDIA Nemotron model. 										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Companies around the world are building on the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DSX AI Factory Platform</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to plan, simulate and run the grid and the AI factory as one system, from the substation to the rack. Their adoption of the platform responds to an acute problem.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Power is now the biggest bottleneck in infrastructure. More than $300 billion in equipment is backlogged, and over 200 gigawatts of projects are waiting in U.S. interconnection queues. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">One root cause of the bottleneck is siloed systems. The grid, the facility and the compute inside have long been designed independently from one another, creating the conditions for delays, risk and stranded capacity.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To help address this, NVIDIA DSX brings these systems together:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>NVIDIA DSX Sim and SimReady assets</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from leading energy manufacturers let engineers simulate the grid, substation and AI factory together, validating generators, switchgear, power systems and cooling before breaking ground.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/flex/"><b>NVIDIA </b><b>DSX Flex</b></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> syncs AI factory demand with grid conditions and onsite generation, turning AI factories from static loads into flexible grid assets.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>NVIDIA DSX MaxLPS and DSX Exchange</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> squeeze more compute from every watt and securely share signals across compute, networking, energy, cooling and operations.</span></li>
</ul>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Weather forecasts shape more than daily plans; timely weather information is crucial for energy producers, insurers and transport operators to plan around changing conditions. A global forecast can show a storm taking shape, but decisions about solar farms, shipping routes or insured property depend on what happens locally.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/high-performance-computing/earth-2/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Earth-2</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> gives developers and researchers a family of open AI models and tools they can customize with their own data. Running on NVIDIA computing, these technologies support global weather prediction, detailed regional forecasts and environmental research.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<p><b>StormScope EU</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is the latest model to join Earth-2 — a regional version of the StormScope model for short-term forecasting, trained over Europe weather and climate data. It uses EUMETSAT satellite observations to predict evolving cloud conditions across 16 Meteosat channels at 10-minute intervals.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At GTC Berlin, NVIDIA is showcasing a live StormScope EU pipeline in an interactive weather demo built in partnership with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.earthmover.io/blog/building-a-low-latency-earth-2-nowcasting-pipeline-with-earthmover"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Earthmover</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. The demo uses Earth2Studio to run forecasts and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Earthmover’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> data platform to manage and serve the real-time results, which are updated as soon as new satellite observations come in, every 10 minutes. Earthmover serves these results as map tiles, letting visitors explore predictions across Europe without downloading entire datasets.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers can combine Earth-2 models in workflows tailored to regional needs, using StormScope for short-term forecasting and CorrDiff to add local detail to coarser weather data. Developers can also pick up these model architectures and train them for new environmental applications such as air-quality modeling. The open source </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/physicsnemo"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> framework provides training and customization tools so teams can build applications that incorporate proprietary data and domain expertise to meet regional needs.</span></p>

<p><b>AXA</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using NVIDIA Earth-2’s FourCastNet 3 to study regional cyclone risk beyond the historical record across more than 1,000 simulated seasons, generating insights that can inform risk assessments and help customers target prevention efforts.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Governments need modern solutions to serve the public while keeping sensitive information under their control.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Open models provide a path forward, letting governments evaluate them, adapt them to local languages and public services, run them on infrastructure they control, apply their own safeguards and keep sensitive data within controlled environments. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Some open models go further, with the datasets they were trained on published so governments can verify if training data was licensed, biased or compliant.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA partners and European governments are teaming to bring  advanced models to boost the efficiency of public administration while retaining control of data and infrastructure. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Several are also building editions of the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/nemotron-personas"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron-Personas collection</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the partner-grounded datasets behind </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/UserSim"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NeMo User Sim</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, NVIDIA’s open source library for population-scale user simulation that dynamically evaluates and trains models and agents in local languages.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2><b>European Commission: Scaling Governed AI Applications</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Berlin-based software company <a target="_blank" href="https://www.deepset.ai/news/deepset-tackles-ai-costs-with-agent-optimization-and-custom-models-for-government-defense-and-industry">deepset</a> is expanding its </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nam11.safelinks.protection.outlook.com/?url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.deepset.ai%2Fhaystack-platform&amp;data=05%7C02%7Cavikanova%40nvidia.com%7Cb1aac5c4554e4102010a08df2b03cf00%7C43083d15727340c1b7db39efd9ccc17a%7C0%7C0%7C639276967559950354%7CUnknown%7CTWFpbGZsb3d8eyJFbXB0eU1hcGkiOnRydWUsIlYiOiIwLjAuMDAwMCIsIlAiOiJXaW4zMiIsIkFOIjoiTWFpbCIsIldUIjoyfQ%3D%3D%7C0%7C%7C%7C&amp;sdata=2ee0ZpSWJYDNkZL0P1b5YrKYwTB4CKcKtgIATGoZgtc%3D&amp;reserved=0"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Haystack Enterprise Platform</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the foundation of AI@EC, the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.deepset.ai/case-studies/european-commission-deepset"><span style="font-weight: 400;">European Commission’s shared platform</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for building and running AI applications on commission-managed infrastructure.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The update connects agent development, execution, optimization and governance for all customers, adding capabilities to compare operating costs, evaluate answer quality and trace problems. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.nvidia.com/deeplearning/triton-inference-server/user-guide/docs/index.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Triton Inference Server</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> supports the shared services used to find and rank relevant information. The expansion builds on operating requirements demonstrated by deployments such as AI@EC, where reusable components, role-based access, audit trails and guardrails support applications including document summarization, knowledge management and multilingual policy analysis.</span></p>
<h2><b>Poland: Advanced Computing for Healthcare and Employment</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Deloitte is helping Poland build agentic systems for healthcare and employment services using open models. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Poland’s eHealth Centre is building its Intelligent Services Platform for medical imaging with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/dgx-basepod/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DGX BasePOD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and the MONAI framework. The tool will help physicians analyze X-rays, CT scans and MRI images, detect abnormalities and prioritize urgent cases, reducing the time patients wait for results.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With Deloitte’s support, Poland’s Ministry of Family, Labour and Social Policy is developing an agentic factory on NVIDIA accelerated computing infrastructure. Designed to support more than 400 labor offices, it will run models including PLLuM, GPT OSS and Inkling for document search, summarization and digital assistants, helping staff more efficiently assist job seekers.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA is also working with SpeakLeash in Poland and the University of Ljubljana in Slovenia on country-specific additions to its synthetic personas program, with localized datasets planned to follow.</span></p>
<h2><b>UK: Data for Locally Relevant Advanced Modeling</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">London-based </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.locailabs.com/blog/nvidia-nemotron-personas-uk-technical-report"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Locai Labs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> released </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-Personas-UK"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron-Personas-UK</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a dataset of 1 million synthetic personas reflecting the U.K.’s demographic and geographic diversity while reducing privacy risk. Potential uses include healthcare evaluation and bias testing across age groups, education levels and rural and urban populations. For developers building public-service models, it offers a way to test whether models work consistently across different communities.</span></p>
<h2><b>Germany: Digital Assistants and AI for Classified Work</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In Germany, <a target="_blank" href="http://google.com/url?q=https://www.pressebox.de/pressemitteilung/materna-information-communications-se/materna-stellt-sinfonie-vor-eine-on-premises-plattform-fr-digital-humans/boxid/1317329&amp;sa=D&amp;source=docs&amp;ust=1792606876214470&amp;usg=AOvVaw2rmPdVSxrnuUrumXURJ1jn">Materna</a> is introducing Sinfonie, an on-premises platform combining generative AI, voice interaction and 3D avatars, with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-open-sources-audio2face-animation-model/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Audio2Face</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> available for animation. Built on a project for a German state administration, it helps employees access institutional knowledge through spoken questions while keeping data on their organization’s infrastructure.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For organizations handling classified information, <a target="_blank" href="https://www.secunet.com/en/about-us/news-events/article/meilenstein-fuer-digitale-souveraenitaet-secunet-red-hat-und-deepset-stellen-ki-angebot-fuer-verschlusssachen-auf-basis-von-nvidia-technologien-vor">secunet</a>, NVIDIA, <a target="_blank" href="https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261021042665/en/Red-Hat-AI-Factory-with-NVIDIA-Accelerates-Agentic-AI-Security-Capabilities-Across-the-Open-Hybrid-Cloud">Red Hat</a> and <a target="_blank" href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/deepset-unveils-reference-architecture-for-self-improving-sovereign-ai-agents-on-nvidia-infrastructure-302914982.html">deepset</a> are going a step further. The four companies are developing a sovereign cloud offering built on secunet’s proven SINA Cloud foundation, which includes security components that have been authorized for use with classified information. The planned offering combines four components: NVIDIA accelerated computing and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> open models; Red Hat OpenShift AI for deploying and managing AI workloads; deepset’s Haystack AI agent orchestration; and infrastructure operated by secunet in German data centers. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To support local development, <a target="_blank" href="https://www.iis.fraunhofer.de/en/ff/amm/allinga/ai-model-factory/soofi.html">the Sovereign Open Source Foundation Models research initiative</a> (Soofi) is developing a family of AI models for German industry and the public sector. That includes </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/Nemotron-Personas-Germany"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron-Personas-Germany,</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> an open dataset of 1 million records, each with six synthetic personas, grounded in the 2022 census across all 16 federal states and 400 districts. Created with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.nvidia.com/nemo/datadesigner/getting-started/welcome"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA NeMo Data Designer</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> without real people’s private data, the dataset helps developers test whether public-service models work consistently across regions, age groups and communities. Like the U.K. edition, it can be used with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/UserSim/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NeMo User Sim</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> so developers can evaluate and train models against simulated users based on synthetic German personas.</span></p>
<h2><b>France: Advanced Open Models in the Public Sector Workspace</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">CANUT, France’s public purchasing body for digital services, selected </span><a target="_blank" href="https://linagora.com/sites/default/files/2026-10/PR_LINAGORA_CANUT_0.pdf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">LINAGORA</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and Docaposte to provide a sovereign collaborative workspace built on Twake.ai. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">More than 2,500 eligible organizations, including municipalities, schools and healthcare institutions, can access the framework without running their own tender. The workspace offers public employees email, document sharing and collaboration tools, with NVIDIA technology supporting speech transcription and infrastructure hosted in France. Région Occitanie is the preferred partner for scaling deployment.</span></p>
<h2><b>Greece: A Local Agentic Ecosystem</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Greek AI and energy company </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/from-energy-tokens-european-ai-ecosystem-showcased-nvidia-gtc-ystxe/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">KIEFER</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is developing an agentic factory built on NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD with DGX GB200 alongside Sophea open weight models and Greek-language tools for knowledge retrieval, speech and digital assistants. These capabilities stand to make institutional information easier to find and use for Greek-speaking public sector teams, while giving organizations control over where agents run and how data is handled.</span></p>
<h2><b>Codefest: Building the Next Applications</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In an effort to support public sector organizations in integrating agentic AI in Europe and beyond, NVIDIA Open Models Codefest brings together startups, software providers and labs across the region to turn open models into locally controlled solutions for public sector challenges, including medical reasoning, AI tutoring and model governance. The top five teams are </span><a target="_blank" href="https://pleias.ai/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Pleias</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="http://zehnlab.ai"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Zehnlab.ai</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.disseqt.ai/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Disseqt</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://algoritmica.ai/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Algoritmica GmbH</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://aba.technology/en/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ABA Technology</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. <a target="_blank" href="http://google.com/url?q=https://www.t-systems.com/de/en/insights/newsroom/news/deutsche-telekom-powers-open-models-ai-codefest-across-emea-with-nvidia-1221678&amp;sa=D&amp;source=docs&amp;ust=1792594206181923&amp;usg=AOvVaw0PljEq-38oAirZa6cqYiwu">Deutsche Telekom and T-Systems</a> have provided the compute infrastructure for teams to train and deploy large language models. </span></p>

<h2><b>Introducing Recursive Flint — A New Local Coding Model Optimized for RTX Spark</b><b></b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Today, frontier AI lab Recursive Superintelligence released <a target="_blank" href="https://recursive.com/updates/introducing-recursive-flint">Recursive Flint</a>, a local LLM built for fast, accurate coding. A 40-billion-parameter </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/mixture-of-experts/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">mixture-of-experts</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> model with 2.5 billion active parameters, Recursive Flint delivers fast inference </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">and performs well in agentic coding benchmarks like S</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">WE-Bench Pro v2, Terminal-Bench 2.1 and SWE-bench verified.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Recursive Flint was built using Recursive’s Eureka Machine, a system for large-scale automated AI research. It automates the full research loop: proposes ideas, implements them, runs experiments to validate ideas and uses the results to decide what to try next — creating specialized kernels that boosted Flint’s generation speed on RTX Spark by 55% without changing the model’s weights.</span><b></b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers can </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/RecursiveSI"><span style="font-weight: 400;">download the open weights </span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">on Hugging Face under the Apache 2.0 license and run the model on RTX Spark and DGX Spark systems, as well as GeForce RTX and RTX PRO GPUs. Developers can also post-train the model with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/Automodel/blob/main/docs/model-coverage/llm/flint/flint.mdx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NeMo Automodel</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for supervised fine-tuning and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">low-rank adaptation, or </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">LoRa, as well as </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-NeMo/RL/tree/flint-support"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NeMo RL</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for group relative policy optimization.  </span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">General availability of the instances is coming next month, with planned expansion of availability to Europe — bringing an integrated platform for training, inference and large-scale reinforcement learning to cloud customers.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A5X instances bring together NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72, NVIDIA ConnectX-9 SuperNICs, Google Cloud Virgo Network and Google Cloud’s fourth-generation advanced liquid-cooling technology for demanding agentic inference and reinforcement learning workloads at scale. Google Cloud plans to scale its Vera Rubin-based infrastructure to as many as 960,000 Rubin GPUs across multiple sites.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ineffable Intelligence</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a London-based technology company founded by AlphaGo creator David Silver, was the first customer to access A5X. Ineffable is using the platform to advance large-scale reinforcement learning and shape “superlearner” systems that learn continuously from experience.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Learning through interaction requires a continuous cycle of generating experience, evaluating outcomes and updating models, requiring infrastructure that can support the entire process at scale. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jSsyHFW3uOs"><span style="font-weight: 400;">A5X</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> brings Vera Rubin into Google Cloud’s AI Hypercomputer as an integrated system, combining compute, networking and storage to support experience-driven learning at scale.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“We’re building superlearners that continually improve through their own experience,” said David Silver, founder and CEO of Ineffable Intelligence. “That demands both substantial compute and a tight connection between generating experience and training on it, so each improvement in the agent can quickly shape its next experience. Vera Rubin’s combination of compute performance and high-bandwidth connectivity, between the Vera CPU and Rubin GPUs and between the Rubin GPUs within the rack, allow us to push the scale and ambition of our reinforcement learning research further.”</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">WPP</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a London-based marketing and communications company, has also demonstrated its creative agent reference architecture on NVIDIA Vera Rubin-based A5X instances using NVIDIA Nemotron models and NVIDIA OpenShell, part of the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/openshell-reference-architecture"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA OpenShell Reference Architecture</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for safe AI agents. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The agents turn conversational requests into multistep creative workflows, such as video generation, upscaling and restyling, with human approval before execution. OpenShell provides governed sandboxes for agent execution, helping control access to sensitive brand assets and creative tools. In </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">WPP’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> initial tests, workloads that typically required a multi-node cluster ran on a single A5X instance.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As organizations deploy agentic systems, they need to protect the proprietary models and sensitive data that power these systems — even when running on infrastructure they don’t own.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Google Cloud will be among the first cloud providers to offer </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/confidential-computing-model-builders-infrastructure"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Confidential Computing</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> at rack scale on bare metal instances such as A4X Max instances powered by NVIDIA GB300 NVL72. Additionally, Google Cloud is making Confidential Computing </span><a target="_blank" href="https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/ai-infrastructure/confidential-g4-gpus-are-ga"><span style="font-weight: 400;">generally available</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on G4 VMs powered by NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs, offering a flexible platform for confidential inference and fine-tuning. Customers including </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vertebrae</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Polaris Cloud </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Confidential Core AI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are using Confidential G4 for conversational privacy, verifiable agent execution and policy-governed enterprise intelligence.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To support energy-efficient deployment at scale, Google Cloud and NVIDIA are exploring </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/european-ecosystem-builds-worlds-most-efficient-ai-factories-with-nvidia-dsx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DSX MaxLPS</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> capabilities for Vera Rubin-based infrastructure to help optimize performance per watt and expand capacity within available data-center power budgets.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Demand from AI-natives is pulling the European AI cloud ecosystem forward faster than ever. Developers need compute closer to their data, open models they can customize and serve at scale, and full-stack infrastructure that is secure by design. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gpu-cloud-computing/partners/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Cloud Partners</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> across Europe, the Nordics, Africa, and Central Asia are expanding regional compute capacity for model training and inference, helping developers build agentic and physical AI applications, and establishing boundaries around what agents can access and do.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Together, they’re turning Europe’s energy, data, research and industrial expertise into productive intelligence — supporting new models, applications, organizations and jobs while giving people more control over where and how they develop and deploy AI.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>

<h2><strong>Sovereign: AI Clouds Building European AI Infrastructure</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Europe is home to world-class research, engineering talent, industrial expertise, data, the next generation of models and applications that requires large-scale computing close to where that knowledge resides. Regional AI clouds provide the infrastructure foundation on which model builders, enterprises and entire industries can grow.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As AI factories scale, power becomes a constraint which makes it critical for every watt to produce more intelligence. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nscale</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/maxlps/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DSX Platform</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to coordinate power allocation with workload requirements. In tests using NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 systems, Nscale demonstrated that NVIDIA DSX MaxLPS enabled 37% more GPUs and nearly 50% higher aggregate inference throughput within the same provisioned power budget. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nscale</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> also plans to deploy NVIDIA Vera Rubin infrastructure for frontier-model training and production workloads to support leading model and application builders using its full-stack, vertically integrated platform with expansion across five European markets.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Crusoe</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is developing Spark, its liquid-cooled modular AI factories designed for the NVIDIA Vera Rubin platform, to bring Crusoe Cloud capacity closer to users for low-latency AI inference. Crusoe Cloud serves leading model and application builders across EMEA, including </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Base44, Gradium, LTX and Wonderful.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Aleria,</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Cassava Technologies</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Firebird</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">G42</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Radiant, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">SWI Compute</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vultr</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are also developing and expanding NVIDIA-powered AI infrastructure across Europe, the Nordics, Africa and Central Asia. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Regional capacity from AI clouds combines the full-stack </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/european-ecosystem-builds-worlds-most-efficient-ai-factories-with-nvidia-dsx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DSX AI Factory platform</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, including NVIDIA Vera Rubin, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/networking/products/data-processing-unit/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA BlueField</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/networking/spectrumx/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Spectrum-X Ethernet</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> networking, giving customers greater choice and control over where workloads and data reside while advancing sovereign computing services and economic growth. </span></p>
<h2><strong>Open: Models and Libraries, From Training to Inference </strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Across the ecosystem, AI clouds are expanding NVIDIA-powered infrastructure and services for developers to build applications with open models and libraries. This new capacity supports model training, fine-tuning for specialized tasks and inference at scale, including applications deployed at the edge.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For production inference, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nebius</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> has adopted NVIDIA Groq 3 LPX, using Spectrum-X Ethernet networking to integrate it with Vera Rubin NVL72 on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nebius</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Token Factory. The combination will bring faster token generation to latency-sensitive applications and agentic systems through the platform’s managed serverless and dedicated endpoints and familiar application programming interfaces.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For robotics and physical AI, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nebius</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> offers a purpose-built AI cloud solution that includes its full-stack Physical AI Workbench. Robotics companies including </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rhoda AI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">RoboForce</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sereact</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are building on the platform. The company also launched three NVIDIA retail agent blueprints — covering inventory, store operations, and fine-tuning —- into early access on Nebius infrastructure.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nebius </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">is expanding its cloud services widely across the continent with its Physical AI Living Lab, and in Germany as a computer supplier for the Next Frontier Challenge of SPRIND, the Federal Agency for Breakthrough Innovation.  </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Baseten</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> has developed a specialized model for code-review routing with Nemotron 3.5 Lightning used by</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> CodeRabbit.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Through supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">CodeRabbit </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">improved routing agreement from 75.8% to 80.7% while reducing estimated inference costs by 50% for the evaluated workload. The results demonstrate how AI developers can customize NVIDIA open models for specialized tasks while improving accuracy and inference efficiency.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fireworks</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Era4</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are partnering to bring open models to enterprises across the U.K. and Europe. The collaboration combines </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fireworks</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">’ model training and inference platform with direct liquid-cooled capacity powered by NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra GPUs at </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Era4’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Chesterfield facility, supporting plans for enterprise AI solutions in financial services, insurance and life sciences.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Verda</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> plans to deploy NVIDIA Vera Rubin, continuing to promote open weight models with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Cosmos</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> already in production, and using the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/dynamo/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Dynamo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> open source framework for inference optimization.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Safe: Secure Boundaries for AI Agents, From Silicon to Software</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As developers connect models to tools and business systems, they also need controls over what agents can access and do.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Announced at GTC Berlin, the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/openshell-reference-architecture"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA OpenShell Reference Architecture</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with support from AI cloud ecosystem partners. Built on the foundation of </span><a target="_blank" href="https://build.nvidia.com/openshell"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA OpenShell</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> secure runtime software, the architecture is designed to give organizations full-stack control to deploy AI agents with clear boundaries around what agents can access and do.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 for model inference, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-cpu/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera CPUs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with OpenShell for agent harnesses and tool use, and NVIDIA BlueField-4 DPUs running NVIDIA Sentry for in-silicon enforcement. Each play a distinct role in delivering that control, working together so agents can achieve what they’re meant to do without moving beyond the boundaries people set for them.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Partners including </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Aleria</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Baseten</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cassava</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">CoreWeave</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Crusoe</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">DigitalOcean</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Firebird</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Fireworks, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Firmus</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">G42</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">GMI Cloud</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">IREN</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lambda</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lightning AI,</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Nebius</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nscale</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Radiant </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Verda</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Vultr</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are supporting the OpenShell Reference Architecture to enable secure agentic deployments for their customers.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Together, European AI clouds are connecting regional infrastructure investment with the work of building and deploying AI. Expanded capacity gives builders more places to run workloads. Model and inference services help them put that capacity to use. Agent controls address what applications are allowed to do as they move into production.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To increase productivity, accelerate innovation and create new opportunities for economic growth, Europe’s industrial leaders are tapping </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/technologies/cuda-x/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CUDA-X</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, as well as </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Cosmos</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> open models, across the engineering and manufacturing lifecycle. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bringing those capabilities together on the factory floor, the </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/factory-operations-fox-blueprint-ai-brain/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Factory Operations Blueprint (FOX)</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is now available in </span><a target="_blank" href="http://developer.nvidia.com/factory-operations-blueprint-early-access"><span style="font-weight: 400;">early access on GitHub</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, providing a path for developers to build and deploy always-on factory manager agents that coordinate work across machines, and specialist agents focused on use cases such as visual inspection, root cause analysis, report generation and fleet management.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These agents can connect to frontier models, including NVIDIA Cosmos and Nemotron, and foundational knowledge including computer-aided design, product lifecycle management, simulation software, and IT and operational technology systems. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building on the FOX blueprint enables the ecosystem to build industrial agents that monitor factory operations in real time, correlate visual findings and machine logs to investigate problems, and coordinate responses. With Cosmos in-context learning, these agents can adapt using examples and documentation, without fine-tuning.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-partners-nvidia-advance-ai-driven-autonomous-factories"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Siemens</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is integrating agents built using FOX into Intelligence Center X, its agentic enterprise system, to help identify root causes and coordinate factory operations, for estimated cost savings of 20%. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Siemens’</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Process Preparation X agents use <a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA/cosmos-data-factory">NVIDIA Cosmos Data Factory</a> tools and skills to generate defect images for circuit-board inspection. At its Erlangen electronics factory, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://news.siemens.com/en-us/siemens-nvidia-pcba-digital-twin-factory/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Siemens</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> used this approach to reduce calibration time of inspection processes by 40%.</span></p>

<h2><b>Accelerating Engineering From the First Design</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CUDA-X libraries accelerate simulation, giving engineers faster feedback to explore design alternatives before committing to physical prototypes. At GTC Berlin, </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cudss-generally-available-engineering-scientific-simulation"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA is releasing cuDSS for general availability</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, accelerating the demanding calculations behind the complex simulations of structural stress, fluid flow, electromagnetic fields and semiconductor device behavior.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mercedes-Benz </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">uses </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Siemens’</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Simcenter STAR-CCM+ running on NVIDIA GPUs to compare vehicle designs through faster aerodynamic simulations. In factories, the company applies NVIDIA Cosmos 3 and the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://build.nvidia.com/nvidia/video-search-and-summarization"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Metropolis VSS blueprint</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to build agents for vehicle inspection and windshield assembly verification, detecting deviations and ensuring consistently high quality. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://community.cadence.com/cadence_blogs_8/b/corporate-news/posts/cadence-and-toyota-advance-automotive-simulation-on-nvidia-ai-infrastructure"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cadence</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is benchmarking automotive fluid-dynamics workloads on NVIDIA GPUs to assess opportunities for faster design iteration, while </span><a target="_blank" href="https://adsknews.autodesk.com/en/news/autodesk-navpack-nvidia/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Autodesk</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> brings the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/physicsnemo"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA PhysicsNeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/warp-python"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Warp</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> frameworks into NavPack, integrated with Autodesk Alias, to help designers like those at </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bentley’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> predict vehicle performance and iterate faster using simulation-trained AI.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">imec</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Infineon</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are accelerating semiconductor simulation with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://ansys.synopsys.com/blog/synopsys-advances-physical-ai"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Synopsys</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> software and NVIDIA accelerated computing. Benchmarks of S-Litho on </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">imec’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> model show up to 40x speedup, while </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Infineon</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> uses Sentaurus Device with NVIDIA CUDA libraries to achieve a 12x speedup in 3D breakdown simulation for next-generation power devices.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Leonardo</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is working with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.neuralconcept.com/post/leonardo-neural-concept-engineering-ai-aerospace"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Neural Concept</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on AI-driven aircraft design, tapping NVIDIA Omniverse libraries to bring aircraft geometry and aerodynamic simulation results together.</span></p>
<figure id="attachment_99196" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-99196" style="width: 640px" class="wp-caption alignnone"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="wp-image-99196 size-full" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Leonardo-Neural-Concept-Aircraft-Design.gif" alt="Animated airflow simulation around a twin-propeller aircraft above mountainous terrain, with blue and orange streamlines tracing air movement around its wings and fuselage." width="640" height="360" /><figcaption id="caption-attachment-99196" class="wp-caption-text">Video courtesy of Neural Concept and Leonardo.</figcaption></figure>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Car manufacturers use </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><a target="_blank" href="https://blog.3ds.com/topics/company-news/long-running-agents">Dassault Systèmes</a>’</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> LEO &amp; AURA Virtual Companions, powered by NVIDIA Nemotron, and orchestrating CATIA AI Agents &amp; SIMULIA AI Agents with SIMULIA solvers, accelerated by NVIDIA CUDA-X libraries, to rapidly design and virtually test vehicle doors before building physical prototypes.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.universalagi.com/research/saab-warfighter"><span style="font-weight: 400;">UniversalAGI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is working with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Saab</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to protect sailors from life-threatending wave impacts at sea, using UniversalAGI’s Latent Interaction Field Transformer physics AI foundation models with accelerated processing by NVIDIA Warp to evaluate thousands of wave conditions and headings in real time instead of days. <p><a target="_blank" href="http://www.physicsx.ai/newsroom/physicsx-and-gb1-unveil-new-ai-powered-approach-to-americas-cup-yacht-design-at-nvidia-gtc-berlin"><span style="font-weight: 400;">PhysicsX</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is working with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">GB1, Britain’s America’s Cup team</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, on AI-powered yacht design, using NVIDIA Omniverse libraries to visualize hull geometry and pressure fields, while </span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><a target="_blank" href="https://fermioniq.com/research-post.html?slug=from-prompt-to-validated-solver-sofia">Fermioniq</a>’s</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Sofia agent helps </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Deltares</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dutch ministries</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> model river flow by translating natural-language requests into simulation solvers built on NVIDIA Warp.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Mistral</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> plans to use CUDA-X, Warp and Omniverse libraries to accelerate physics simulation and generate training data for engineering AI models.</span></p>

With </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft Azure NCv6 virtual machines powered by NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition GPUs,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> agents with spatial intelligence combine engineering data, operating information and 3D equipment geometry to see, simulate and reason over industrial facilities, supporting inspection and maintenance use cases across the process and energy industries.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuDSS, launched today in general availability, is an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-x-libraries"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CUDA-X library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> that shifts a critical part of engineering and scientific simulations onto GPUs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Complex engineering feats — from advanced chips to aircraft to industrial systems —– depend on simulation to predict how designs will perform before they’re built. As designs become more complex, simulations take longer to run, increasing computing demands and limiting the number of ideas teams can test.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA cuDSS accelerates solving large sparse systems — the math-heavy step that repeats throughout engineering and scientific simulations and often limits how many designs a team can test — by tapping into GPUs. This lets applications reduce time to results and increase simulation throughput, enabling end users to explore larger more realistic models, more accurate representations and a wider design space at a reduced cost.</span></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/gtc-berlin-2026-news/">Read All NVIDIA in Europe Announcements</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Industry leaders are already integrating NVIDIA cuDSS into their tools to transform workflows for their end customers:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Applied Materials:</b> <span style="font-weight: 400;">With cuDSS, Applied Materials accelerated sparse solves in Ginestra, its physics-based, defect-centric simulation platform, by up to 10x versus CPU-only approaches, speeding exploration of semiconductor materials and device reliability.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>COMSOL:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Using COMSOL Multiphysics with NVIDIA cuDSS on NVIDIA accelerated computing, ZEISS SMT achieved a measured 4x speedup in an EUV lithography plasma simulation benchmark, completing the full simulation in six days and 14 hours.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://community.cadence.com/cadence_blogs_8/b/corporate-news/posts/cadence-and-toyota-advance-automotive-simulation-on-nvidia-ai-infrastructure"><b>Cadence</b></a><b>:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Uses cuDSS in its Celsius software to accelerate simulations of heat, structural stress and warpage in advanced chips and 3D chip designs, helping customers including </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nokia</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> analyze complex designs faster.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Dassault Systèmes</b><b>: </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dassault Systèmes reports an 8-10x speedup using cuDSS within its Abaqus structural and computer simulation technology electromagnetic solvers for performance-critical simulations across industries.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Siemens</b><b>:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> Siemens uses cuDSS in Simcenter STAR-CCM+ to accelerate simulations of structures in fluid-structure interaction workflows.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://ansys.synopsys.com/blog/synopsys-advances-physical-ai"><b>Synopsys</b></a><b>: </b><span style="font-weight: 400;">Synopsys uses cuDSS in its Sentaurus Device simulator tool to accelerate simulations of advanced logic, memory and power chips, helping Infineon model increasingly complex devices faster.</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><b>Accelerated Simulation at Scale</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As simulation workloads grow in size and complexity, cuDSS can scale out of the box from individual workstations to large computing clusters.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Developers can integrate cuDSS into existing engineering, scientific and optimization software offerings, bringing GPU acceleration to tools already used by engineers and researchers. They can use cuDSS through C, C++,  Python and Fortran interfaces.</span></p>
