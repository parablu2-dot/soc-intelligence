										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The elves don’t have to be the only ones busy in the workshop this holiday season. With the lights twinkling and the gift wrap out, it’s the perfect time to give someone — or yourself — a spark to take a hobby to the next level.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For some, that means diving into a new game. For others, it’s video editing or even building a robot.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA technologies offer something for everyone on Santa’s list. Read on to unwrap the details.</span></p>
<h2><b>Make Time for One More Game</b></h2>
<p><iframe title="NBA 2K27 | NVIDIA DLSS 5 Launch Trailer" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/LHUzFnq70b8?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With the holiday break here, everyone can be online to score bragging rights or explore a world that’s only been seen in video game trailers. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For some players, an upgrade means a desktop that brings out the detail in every scene. For others, it’s a laptop they can take along when visiting family, or a way to play in the cloud on a device they already own. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The right setup depends on where and what to play:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/graphics-cards/50-series/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">GeForce RTX 50 Series</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> laptops and desktops power the action. Built on the NVIDIA Blackwell architecture, they feature fifth-generation Tensor Cores and fourth-generation RT Cores for AI-enhanced graphics, detailed ray-traced worlds and high frames-per-second gaming.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/rtx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA RTX Spark PCs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> combine RTX graphics with NVIDIA’s full-stack AI platform on a single superchip, bringing decades of NVIDIA innovation to slim, premium laptops and compact desktops on the familiar Windows platform. One PC can handle every workflow, from accelerated creation and immersive gaming to powerful local AI  — with a variety of designs and all-day battery life.</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/rtx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;"> </span></a></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">See games in a new light with NVIDIA DLSS 5 technology, which boosts performance and enhances image quality. The newest feature, DLSS 3D-Guided Neural Rendering, infuses scenes with lifelike lighting and materials in supported games. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LHUzFnq70b8"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Get a glimpse of </span><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">NBA 2K27</span></i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with DLSS 5</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Reflex and G-SYNC make gameplay more responsive and fluid. Reflex reduces system latency in supported games, while </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/products/g-sync-monitors/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">G-SYNC displays</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> use variable refresh rates to deliver smooth, tear-free gaming.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce-now/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">GeForce NOW</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> cloud gaming service enables playing top titles anywhere. Stream supported games on compatible laptops, TVs, handhelds and more. Connect to supported game stores to play owned PC games, with more than 4,500 titles available across the service.</span></li>
</ul>
<h2><b>Make Something Worth Sharing</b></h2>
<p><iframe title="Accelerate Your Creative Workflow with NVIDIA Studio" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/RIwHgDOGrHg?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For creators with a project that’s been on the backburner, the right gift this season can be the push to finally make it happen. Maybe it’s holiday clips becoming a short film, a snowy scene coming together in 3D or a livestream that brings friends across time zones to the same virtual table. Creators can use NVIDIA systems — such as GeForce RTX 50 Series and RTX Spark devices — to keep up with the workflows, along with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/studio/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Studio tools</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and technologies for AI acceleration. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Studio Drivers support video editing, 3D design and livestreaming. In supported applications, faster processing gives more room to experiment and refine. </span></p>
<h2><b>Build a Robot That Thinks</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Every robot starts somewhere. This season, it could be under the tree.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Building a robot is an experiment in teaching a device to see or sense its surroundings. A first project might be as simple as using a camera to identify a candy cane or a gingerbread man, then moving on to an app that probes what the camera sees. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Testing brings new questions. Can the robot recognize the candy cane under twinkling lights? Can it tell a frosted gingerbread man from an unfrosted one? Each experiment leaves more to improve and explore.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The NVIDIA Jetson edge AI platform provides the hardware and software to build projects like these. It combines compact computers with the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/embedded/jetpack"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA JetPack</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> software development kit to help makers build robots, autonomous machines and intelligent devices.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A </span><a target="_blank" href="https://marketplace.nvidia.com/robotics-edge/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Jetson Developer Kit</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is a gift to explore computer vision, generative AI and multimodal robot control, with the option to add cameras and sensors suited to various projects. AI runs directly on the device, processing information from its surroundings. When ready to take a prototype further, production systems powered by Jetson modules provide a path to deployment.</span></p>
<p><iframe title="Microduck Comes to Life With NVIDIA Jetson" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/u5MVlkn2ZU4?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">See how <a target="_blank" href="https://pollen-robotics.com/microduck/">Microduck</a> comes to life with</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-orin/nano-super-developer-kit/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, bringing together robot movement, visual understanding and natural-language interaction. With Hermes Agent and NVIDIA Cosmos, developers can explore how AI connects everyday requests with actions in the physical world.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="Bring an SO-101 arm to life with NVIDIA Jetson Orin" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/ve9P8AoVV7Q?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bring an SO-101 arm to life with NVIDIA Jetson Orin and explore agentic robotics. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Give OpenClaw a simple request, and watch it plan the steps, call NVIDIA tools and check the results. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/openshell/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenShell</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> runs the agent securely in a sandbox, controlling its access to models and robot tools. Based on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/gear/aspire/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ASPIRE</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> skills, the system reuses lessons from past attempts to guide future tasks.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="Reachy Mini the Robot Companion" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/UzYaY_hNFrM?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Build a robot companion that chats, moves and understands what it sees, with <a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-E4B-4GB/">NVIDIA </a></span><a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/nemotron/NVIDIA-Nemotron-3.5-Bolt-E4B-4GB/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron 3.5 Bolt and Cosmos</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> open models running locally on Jetson Orin Nano 8GB.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For more advanced projects, explore vision language action models and world action models to enable increasingly sophisticated robotic behaviors — beyond simple perception and predefined actions toward understanding natural language instructions, reasoning, planning multistep tasks and interacting with the physical world.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dive into setup guides and project tutorials in</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://www.jetson-ai-lab.com/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Jetson AI Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Whether it’s a gaming upgrade, a creative project or a first robot, the right tools can open up new possibilities well beyond the holidays. </span></p>

					<description><![CDATA[NVIDIA and Microsoft are extending their full-stack collaboration to help organizations build, customize and deploy intelligent applications — from next-generation infrastructure on Azure to industrial systems and Windows PCs. At Microsoft Ignite, the companies are expanding access to Azure virtual machines powered by NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems, as well as adding customer availability to [&#8230;]]]></description>
										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA and Microsoft are extending their full-stack collaboration to help organizations build, customize and deploy intelligent applications — from next-generation infrastructure on Azure to industrial systems and Windows PCs.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/events/microsoft-ignite/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft Ignite</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the companies are expanding access to Azure virtual machines powered by NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems, as well as adding customer availability to NVIDIA open models and customization capabilities through Microsoft Foundry, and new options for running AI at the edge, where businesses generate and use their data.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft is also using NVIDIA technologies to accelerate its own models and services, including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/dynamo/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Dynamo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> inference software for post-training and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/professional-desktop-gpus/rtx-pro-6000-family/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for Bing search models. </span></p>
<h2><b>NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 Running in Azure </b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 systems are running in Azure data centers</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, providing infrastructure designed for demanding reasoning, agentic applications, and large-scale model training and inference. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft and NVIDIA are also collaborating to extend confidential computing to bare-metal and rack-scale AI infrastructure. The work will help protect sensitive models and data while they are being processed, supporting organizations that need to work with proprietary or regulated information. </span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/solutions/confidential-computing/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Confidential Computing</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and attestation help customers verify the computing environment before releasing sensitive models, data or keys. As AI workloads span more interconnected accelerators, the companies are working to extend those protections across the AI factory infrastructure.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To help data centers get more performance from available power, NVIDIA and Microsoft are also collaborating on power efficiency using </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/products/dsx/maxlps/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA MaxLPS</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Azure’s Vera Rubin preview builds on NVIDIA and Microsoft’s codesign and performance optimization, underscored in the latest NVIDIA Blackwell-based MLPerf results. In </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/mlperf-training-endpoints-vera-rubin/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">MLPerf Training v6.1</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Azure scaled to 10,240 NVIDIA GPUs with GB200 NVL72 systems on the DeepSeek-V3 model, the largest scale in the round. In </span><a target="_blank" href="https://signal65.com/research/ai/microsoft-azure-leads-deepseek-r1-interactive-inference-in-mlperf-v6-1/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">MLPerf Inference v6.1</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, Azure’s NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 systems led cloud submissions on DeepSeek-R1 interactive throughput by over 10%, per Signal65.  </span></p>
<h2><b>Helping Enterprises Customize and Deploy Open Models</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Enterprise AI agents need to reason through complex tasks, find relevant company information and understand business documents. NVIDIA and Microsoft are expanding the models and services available through Microsoft Foundry to help developers bring these capabilities into business applications.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft’s serverless Programmed Training offering gives developers control over post-training workflows while Foundry manages the underlying infrastructure. Enterprises can focus on their business data, evaluating model responses and improving model behavior.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Foundry Managed Compute adds support for deploying custom model weights, helping connect model customization with deployment through Microsoft’s managed services.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.ey.com/en_us"><span style="font-weight: 400;">EY</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> used Programmed Training in Microsoft Foundry to adapt NVIDIA Nemotron open models for classifying corporate purchasing records, improving accuracy on its evaluation dataset by 55% and lowering cost by 15x compared with proprietary frontier models. The work demonstrates how enterprises can customize open models for specialized business tasks. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The expanded NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 lineup on Foundry Managed Compute supports different requirements across enterprise applications. Nemotron 3.5 Super is designed for long-running agents working across coding, research and business workflows, while Lightning supports applications that need fast responses and tool use.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Complementary models help agents find relevant information with Nemotron Embed and Rerank, extract structured information from documents with Nemotron Parse and detect harmful content with Nemotron Content Safety. Nemotron 3.5 Bolt — designed for high-throughput, low-latency reasoning — and Transcribe, for speech recognition, are coming soon.</span></p>
<h2><b>Bringing AI Closer to Enterprise Data </b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA and Microsoft are expanding deployment options beyond the cloud, helping organizations run models and agents closer to their data while maintaining control over their infrastructure and operations.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Expanded support for Nemotron 3.5 in Foundry Local brings capabilities for reasoning, coding and tool-using agents to Azure Local environments. Retrieval models help applications find evidence across text, document images, charts and tables.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Azure Local will also support NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs from hardware partners, giving organizations additional options for deploying AI on premises.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Azure Local is expanding to </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-thor/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Jetson Thor</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/autonomous-machines/embedded-systems/jetson-orin/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Jetson Orin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> edge AI platforms to support industrial applications such as machine vision, robotics and autonomous operations. NVIDIA physical AI models, including </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai/cosmos/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cosmos</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, are also being brought to Foundry Local.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">On Windows PCs powered by </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/rtx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA RTX Spark</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, NVIDIA and Microsoft will showcase local large language model experiences at Ignite, demonstrating how developers can run AI applications directly on devices.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For organizations using Cloud PCs, the companies are bringing Direct Stream capabilities to GPU-accelerated Windows 365 Cloud PCs. These capabilities support demanding graphics applications with up to two 4K streams at 60 frames per second and low latency, even over challenging networks. A limited preview is open for sign-ups, with public preview planned for early 2027.</span></p>
<h2><b>Connecting Scientific AI With Microsoft Discovery</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For scientific research, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-BioNeMo/bionemo-agent-toolkit"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA BioNemo Agent Toolkit</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA/nvalchemi-toolkit"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA ALCHEMI Toolkit</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> are</span><a target="_blank" href="https://aka.ms/Discovery-BioNeMo"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">expanding into the Microsoft Discovery catalog</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, bringing models for life sciences, drug discovery and materials and chemical science into Microsoft’s agentic research platform.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">BioNeMo Agent Toolkit helps scientists use natural language to run computational biology workflows in Microsoft Discovery, with agents coordinating the tools and model calls needed to carry out the work.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">An initial </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/healthcare-and-life-sciences-leaders-build-on-nvidia-to-advance-drug-discovery-and-expand-patient-care"><span style="font-weight: 400;">integration uses the RFD4-Proteina model</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, developed in collaboration with the University of Washington’s Institute for Protein Design, to generate candidate protein structures and amino acid sequences for further evaluation and laboratory validation. </span></p>
<h2><b>Accelerating Microsoft’s Models and Services</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alongside expanding customer access to NVIDIA technologies, Microsoft is using NVIDIA software and accelerated computing in its own AI development and services.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft AI uses NVIDIA Dynamo to support reinforcement learning rollout inference during post-training. This computationally intensive process generates the experiences used to improve models.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Dynamo coordinates inference across a large GPU fleet, optimizing performance, as well as helping manage requests, model updates and recovery from failures. The deployment builds on joint engineering between Microsoft AI and NVIDIA. </span></p>

<h2 id="autonomous-operations" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><strong>Physical AI Paves the Path to Autonomous Industrial Operations </strong><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#autonomous-operations"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>

<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI agents powered by frontier models pave the way to autonomous operations. They use industrial tools and engineering data to help engineers build </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/digital-twin/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">digital twins</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> that test plans and processes and evaluate operational decisions.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Falkor, a KONGSBERG company, shows how this works in practice. The company’s Falkor Surface platform lets customers turn their computer-aided design and engineering data into facility-scale digital twins using the GPT-6 Astra model. The platform uses NVIDIA Omniverse libraries and NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Server Edition GPUs on Microsoft Azure to enable advanced physics simulation.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI agents built on Falkor’s agentic engine understand dense technical documentation, spatial relationships and real-world physics. Engineers can create and test plans in simulation before traveling on site, reducing costly delays and mistakes. They can also inspect equipment and check clearances in a shared view.</span></p>

<h2 id="pick-and-place" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><strong>Build a Robot Pick-and-Place Simulation With SimReady Assets </strong><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#pick-and-place"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-100114" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/figure-01-intro.gif" alt="" width="960" height="540" /></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Getting a robot model into a scene is only the start. Developers also need to assemble its components, add controls and check whether it can complete a task.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA developers Dillon Bailey and Rishabh Chadha used 3D assets from the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/nvidia/simready-central-library"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SimReady Central</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> library to build a pick-and-place simulation of a robot moving a can using the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.isaacsim.omniverse.nvidia.com/latest/introduction/quickstart_index.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Sim</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> framework. They directed an agent equipped with GPT-6 Sol and Isaac Sim skills to assemble the scene and generate pick-and-place controls.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Testing the assembled scene shows how the assets work together, from joint movement and contact behavior to the gripper’s ability to hold, move and release the can. With that baseline in place, developers can adjust object positions and motion paths and repeat the tests to evaluate how each change affects the task.</span></p>

<h2 id="vr-simulation" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><strong>Build a Voice-Controlled VR Simulation With SimReady Assets </strong><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#vr-simulation"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="FMU Toaster in VR" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/m0HuPLuQD1I?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bringing 3D assets into virtual reality (VR) requires more than rendering a scene. Objects need to respond to controllers and behave as expected when grabbed, moved or released.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse community developer Michael Wagner took on this challenge. He directed GPT-6 Astra to use </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.blender.org/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Blender</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> software to model a study environment from two reference images, balancing visual quality with efficient performance in VR. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">He then used Claude Code to build an application that lets users find SimReady Central assets by voice and interact with them in an immersive VR scene. The Claude Code agent worked for about eight hours, and Wagner built the application over a weekend using an NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada workstation and a Meta Quest 3 VR headset. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The application ran on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">: </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovstage"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovstage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> shared the OpenUSD scene, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> rendered it, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovxr"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovxr </span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">enabled VR and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX/tree/main/ovphysx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovphysx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> ran physics. Wagner later built a second version with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovnewton"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovnewton</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, which integrates with ovstage, to compare the physics libraries.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The biggest challenge for Wagner was checking what happened inside the headset. Automated tests missed problems he could feel, including objects shaking when grabbed. Wagner reported the symptoms, guided the agent through changes and verified the interaction himself. Updating the grab control once per frame resolved the shaking. His takeaway: Give agents renders and logs to inspect, then check the experience directly. </span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore his</span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/perfectproducts/ovxr_simready_physx_demo"> <i><span style="font-weight: 400;">open source demo</span></i></a> <em>f</em><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">or build instructions, a starting prompt and reusable agent skills.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="send-microducks" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><strong>Send Microducks on Rounds in a Simulated Hospital </strong><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#send-microducks"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-2" width="1200" height="675" preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/microducks-icu-pathtraced-16x9-1920x1080-1.mp4?_=2" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/microducks-icu-pathtraced-16x9-1920x1080-1.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/microducks-icu-pathtraced-16x9-1920x1080-1.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before adapting a robot to a new environment, developers need to test whether its walking policy — the AI that controls how it moves — can keep it stable in that setting.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI developer Maziyar Panahi directed Claude Code, using Claude Opus 5.5, to assemble an intensive care unit from 13 </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/nvidia/simready-central"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA SimReady</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> hospital assets and test Microduck robots inside it. The assets came with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton physics</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> settings, so Panahi didn’t have to model the furniture or create collision geometry from scratch. The Newton physics engine also helped replicate the ward so hundreds of copies could run together on one NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 GPU.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When the cloud containers lacked graphics support, Panahi and the Claude Code agent separated simulation from rendering, recording robot poses in the cloud and rendering them locally. They also treated the furniture as static collision objects, which enabled larger runs within the solver’s limits. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The robots walked using AI they had already been trained on and followed preset points along a route. Panahi checked how many robots remained upright: In a 256-ward run, 766 of 768 Microducks were still standing after 14 simulated seconds. He said the agent’s work took about two hours.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/maziyarpanahi/ai-demos/tree/main/omniverse/simulate-robots-in-an-icu"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore the open source demo</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for a starter prompt, checks and instructions to begin with one ward on a CPU, then scale to hundreds on an NVIDIA GPU.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="editable-assets" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Create Editable 3D Assets With Houdini and AI Agents </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#editable-assets"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Creating animated 3D content requires connecting modeling, simulation, materials and rendering. Nick Scarcella, a senior technical artist on NVIDIA’s BondLab team, directed AI agents — primarily using Claude — to work with CUDA libraries and Isaac Sim to create animations. The BondLab team uses</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.sidefx.com/products/houdini/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">SideFX Houdini</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and the</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA ovrtx library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to develop materials, lighting and the final rendered appearance.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In one experiment, Scarcella guided Astra to recreate an OP-1 portable synthesizer using product images, manuals and technical direction. He exported the model into the</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/openusd/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenUSD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> framework, tested it in Houdini and Blender, then used Astra and Opus to refine details before adjusting materials and textures for ovrtx rendering.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Scarcella also used a publicly shared robot-hand checkpoint in Isaac Sim, directing the motion and developing materials and lighting in Houdini with Claude’s assistance. Working with team member Steven Dvornik, the team created an “Echos” visualization: translucent versions of the hand overlap and merge into a single hand animation, offering a visual interpretation of robot training.</span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Planning warehouse automation requires a model of the building itself, but shelving and machinery can obscure its structure in site scans. The goal is an editable, simulation-ready building model where developers can add or reposition shelving and machinery to evaluate warehouse layouts and automation plans before making physical changes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Wehr guided Astra to interpret panoramic photos, create building geometry and align it with point-cloud measurements. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/openusd/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenUSD</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> stores the models, while </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse’s ovrtx library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> renders results for inspection.</span></p>

<h2 id="gas-turbine" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Test Gas-Turbine Operating Scenarios With a Digital Twin </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#gas-turbine"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-6" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4?_=6" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Turbine-Lab-RTX-and-What-If-under-10MB-1.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before changing how industrial equipment operates, engineers need a way to explore the consequences. Pradeep Kaushik, a member of the NVIDIA Omniverse developer relations team, directed Astra to build a what-if analysis application around a simulated gas turbine and generator.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The application lets users adjust conditions such as load, ambient temperature and fuel composition, then compare power, efficiency, fuel consumption and equipment response. Six test procedures explore scenarios including load changes, a stuck fuel valve and an exhaust-sensor error.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The prototype gives engineers an interactive way to investigate how operating changes and faults could affect equipment behavior, supporting decisions about which scenarios warrant further testing.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Astra used tools like Cantera for thermochemistry and SciPy for equipment dynamics. The application maps solver results to rotor animation and color-coded temperature, pressure and flow-velocity indicators in a 3D turbine. NVIDIA Omniverse libraries update the scene (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovstage"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovstage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), render it with RTX (ovrtx) and stream the view to the browser (ovstream). Engineers can rotate and zoom the cutaway, switch between indicators, and pause or scrub through a simulated response alongside charts and virtual instruments.</span></p>

<h2 id="simready-assets" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Build Simulation Scenes With Open Source SimReady Assets </b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#simready-assets"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-7" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4?_=7" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/kitchen_final_blog_with_music.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With SimReady Central Library, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-central-assets-robotics-ai-factories"><span style="font-weight: 400;">announced at GTC Berlin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, developers can access more than 2,000 simulation-ready USD assets from equipment manufacturers and asset providers for robotics simulations and AI factory digital twins workflows.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Paul Kind, a member of NVIDIA’s Omniverse documentation team, pointed an AI agent to the library and guided scene assembly through conversation. His kitchen scene included 180 placements drawn from 63 unique library assets, alongside 18 custom geometry pieces. Kind reviewed renders produced with Isaac Sim and guided further adjustments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For robotics and factory workflows, developers can select SimReady assets with the simulation properties embedded to fit their required task then test in an assembled environment.</span></p>
<p><em><a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/spaces/nvidia/simready-central"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Find assets in SimReady Central Library</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">,</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and</span> <a target="_blank" href="https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/simready-central-assets-robotics-ai-factories#prepare-and-contribute-content"><span style="font-weight: 400;">learn how to contribute</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>

<h2 id="warehouse" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Stress-Test a Warehouse Simulation at Scale </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#warehouse"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-8" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4?_=8" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Infra-scale-demo-20260914_144614-Meeting-Recording.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">A warehouse simulation needs to keep running smoothly as more robot arms, conveyors and boxes fill the scene. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">When it slows down, developers need to identify what’s holding it back. </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Bhavik Sanganee, principal engineer at NVIDIA, used Astra with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse?size=n_12_n&amp;sort-field=featured&amp;sort-direction=desc"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to build a warehouse simulation, increase its complexity and investigate slowdowns.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Performance first fell below the target when the scene doubled in size. Running physics calculations, image rendering and video preparation concurrently restored performance and enabled further scaling.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With 80 conveyors, 40 arms and 800 boxes, the simulation sustained 30 frames per second at full HD resolution during a two-minute test. The arms completed 640 successful box placements.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Doubling the scene again reduced performance. Testing traced the slowdown to the physics and robot-control pipeline, giving Sanganee a specific area to improve.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start building with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse?size=n_12_n&amp;sort-field=featured&amp;sort-direction=desc"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>

<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-9" width="1200" height="406" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4?_=9" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/scurve_rl_vs_astra_side_by_side.mp4</a></video></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In electronics assembly, a robot may need to position a flexible cable around components before plugging it into a port. The cable bends as the robot moves it, and the connector must line up precisely to fit.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Oyindamola Omotuyi, robotics technical marketing manager at NVIDIA, directed Astra to build a robot simulation using </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> physics and Omniverse rendering with a</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K2ui16FdLZw"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Robotiq gripper</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> handling the cable.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Astra also developed a</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/reinforcement-learning/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">reinforcement learning</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> system that combined learned cable-routing movements with position-and-orientation feedback and corrective adjustments for USB-C insertion. The simulation gave Omotuyi a way to test cable handling and connector alignment before moving to physical-robot experiments.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The controller used information supplied directly by the simulation. Next steps include testing a friction-held grasp and using information a physical robot could obtain from cameras and sensors.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Get started with robot learning in </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/isaac/lab"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Isaac Lab</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></em></p>

<h2 id="cad" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Turn Robot Computer-Aided Design Into a SimReady Asset </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#cad"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before testing whether a robot can pick up and move a box, developers need a simulation model with joints that move correctly and with grippers that can hold the object. A computer-aided design (CAD) model provides the robot’s shape, but it still needs physics properties to perform the task.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Using an ABB Robotics YuMi robot, developers directed Astra with NVIDIA Omniverse content libraries to convert CAD files into OpenUSD, match the robot’s appearance to reference images, and configure joints and collision geometry. They checked the asset against SimReady Foundation requirements before testing it in NVIDIA Isaac Sim.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Both arms and grippers completed four cube pick-and-place cycles, using simulated finger contact and friction. These tests demonstrated behavior under the simulated conditions — estimated physical properties were not calibrated against the real robot.<br />
</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Follow the five-step </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/5-steps-to-create-simready-assets-for-robotics-with-frontier-ai-models/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SimReady tutorial</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></em></p>

<h2 id="humanoid-simulator" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Build a Humanoid Simulator for a Warehouse Environment </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#humanoid-simulator"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-10" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4?_=10" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Humanoid-Warehouse.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400;">Explore a gamified, physics-based control of a humanoid robot in first- and third-person view.</span></em><i></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before automating warehouse tasks, developers need an interactive simulation environment to explore task behavior and evaluate how the work gets done. Frank DeLise, Omniverse product manager at NVIDIA, used Astra to turn a </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/glossary/simready/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SimReady</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> warehouse and humanoid robot into an interactive simulator with first- and third-person views.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DeLise directed Astra to connect</span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for physics (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/PhysX/blob/main/ovphysx/README.md"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovphysx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), scene updates (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia-omniverse/ovstage"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovstage</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), rendering (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">) and the user interface (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-omniverse/ovui"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovui</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">). He also used Astra with SimReady (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/nvidia/simready-foundation"><span style="font-weight: 400;">simready-foundation</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">) to create the physical scene in simulation. Astra then generated animation and application code to bring those capabilities together.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Learn how to </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/5-steps-to-create-simready-assets-for-robotics-with-frontier-ai-models/"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">prepare and validate SimReady robot assets</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;"> with frontier AI models and NVIDIA Omniverse libraries.</span></i></p>

<h2 id="autonomous-driving-testing" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Connect an Autonomous-Driving Testing Workflow </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#autonomous-driving-testing"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-11" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4?_=11" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/zero-to-alpamayo-under-10MB.mp4</a></video></div>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Changing a scene, sensor or driving model can significantly affect autonomous-vehicle simulations. Doyub Kim, a manager on the simulation technology team at NVIDIA, asked Astra to build Zero to Alpamayo — a reusable simulation environment based on San Francisco’s Market Street.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kim directed Astra to map out the workflow, then connect asset creation, traffic, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/integrate-nvidia-omniverse-rtx-sensor-simulation-into-existing-apps/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse RTX sensor simulation</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/solutions/autonomous-vehicles/alpamayo/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alpamayo driving</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> in stages, checking each integration. The resulting prototype was a testing ground for comparing models and tracing how scene or sensor changes affect downstream driving behavior. A separate Cosmos3-Nano experiment varied weather and lighting in recorded simulation videos, allowing Kim to compare the driving model’s responses to the same scenario under different conditions.</span></p>

<h2 id="sensor-differences" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Use Sensor Differences to Create and Improve Digital Twins </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#sensor-differences"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<div style="width: 1200px;" class="wp-video"><video class="wp-video-shortcode" id="video-98677-12" width="1200" height="675" autoplay preload="metadata" controls="controls"><source type="video/mp4" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4?_=12" /><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4">https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/0_00-0_004-hubble_rivermark_Real_Sim_no_loop.mp4</a></video></div>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Comparing recorded and simulated camera and lidar outputs gives developers key performance indicator-based feedback for refining a scene.</span></i></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To test robots and autonomous vehicles, developers need to know how closely simulated sensors match real ones. Ashley Reid, who works on RTX sensor validation at NVIDIA, directed Astra and Claude Fable 5 agents to compare </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> camera and raw LiDAR outputs with recorded data. The agents created two digital twins from scratch and improved two existing ones.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Over about three days, Reid guided an iterative workflow in which agents measured differences, created or modified OpenUSD scenes, and checked the results. Changes addressed missing objects, geometry and materials, with acceptance depending on camera and LiDAR metrics. This gives developers a way to use measured discrepancies to guide scene creation and improvement.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Start by rendering an OpenUSD scene with the </span></i><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx/tree/main/examples/python/minimal"><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx minimal Python example</span></i></a><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">, then define a sensor measure to compare with recorded data.</span></i></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Teaching robots new movements means checking whether those actions work under physical constraints. <p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Under Kim’s direction, Astra built controllers and refined them through physics trials. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/newton-physics"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Newton Physics Engine</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> simulated behavior, the open source </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/warp-python"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Warp</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> framework accelerated calculations and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> rendered scenes and virtual-camera images. In one experiment, the robot cleared a single hurdle in 64 of 100 simulation trials. The trials gave Kim feedback for improving the robot’s timing and control.</span></p>

<h2 id="international-space-station" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Bring the International Space Station Into the Browser </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#international-space-station"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Turning 3D models into an application requires connecting assets, live data and an interface. Johns built the application with a single prompt, then used a follow-up prompt to shift the scene to Earth’s daytime side so the planet was visible.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The workflow used Blender for asset preparation and </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/omniverse"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Omniverse libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> for rendering (</span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/NVIDIA-Omniverse/ovrtx"><span style="font-weight: 400;">ovrtx</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">), scene runtime (ovstage) and streaming (ovstream). The application brings 3D models and operational data into the browser, with Johns guiding its development through prompts and corrections.</span></p>

<h2 id="testing-environments" class="wp-block-heading" style="font-size: 24px;"><b>Turn Captured Rooms Into Testing Environments </b><b></b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/developers-simulation-frontier-ai-agents/#testing-environments"><b><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></b></a></h2>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">New </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/rtx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA RTX Spark compact desktops</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> from ASUS, Dell and MSI are available today, bringing local AI agents, image and video generation, and model fine-tuning to a dedicated Windows PC.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With up to 128GB of unified memory and NVIDIA’s AI software stack, these systems provide room for larger local models alongside RTX-accelerated creative and development tools. A dedicated desktop can keep agents and scheduled tasks running while a laptop travels or handles other work.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alongside the new desktops, an expanding software ecosystem brings more creative and development workflows to RTX Spark laptops and compact desktops.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Native Windows on Arm applications from Adobe, the NVIDIA DLSS Ray Reconstruction feature in Blender and local generation workflows in ComfyUI expand what creators and developers can do on RTX Spark — all on one Windows PC.</span></p>
<h2><b>A Windows PC Built for Local AI</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As workflows expand to include local AI models and agents, more work can continue beyond an active editing session — from generating assets to processing long renders or scheduled tasks. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RTX Spark compact desktops give developers, creators and AI enthusiasts a dedicated Windows PC for local models and always-on agents. They bring together image and video generation, model fine-tuning, creative applications and development tools in one compact system.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Running supported models locally keeps locally processed code and files on the PC and avoids per-token cloud application programming interface (API) charges for those tasks.</span></p>
<figure id="attachment_100083" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-100083" style="width: 1280px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1.jpg"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-100083" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1.jpg" alt="" width="1280" height="680" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1-960x510.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11-18-Local-AI-Blog-copy-1-630x335.jpg 630w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1280px) 100vw, 1280px" /></a><figcaption id="caption-attachment-100083" class="wp-caption-text">RTX Spark compact desktops like the ASUS ProArt GR1X mini PC are built to run personal AI agents 24/7.</figcaption></figure>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">RTX Spark desktops are equipped with a 20-core NVIDIA Grace CPU, an NVIDIA Blackwell RTX GPU with 6,144 CUDA cores and up to 128GB of unified memory, delivering up to 1 petaflop of AI performance. Ten-gigabit Ethernet connectivity enables fast transfers of large datasets, model files and creative assets between RTX Spark compact desktops and networked storage.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This advanced hardware unlocks incredible capabilities for creators, developers and AI enthusiasts, including:</span></p>
<ul>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Running massive 120-billion-parameter large language models locally</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rendering 90GB+ 3D scenes in real time</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">Smoothly editing 4:2:2 video in real time</span></li>
</ul>
<figure id="attachment_100090" aria-describedby="caption-attachment-100090" style="width: 1280px" class="wp-caption aligncenter"><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series.png"><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="size-full wp-image-100090" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series.png" alt="" width="1280" height="328" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series-960x246.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/MSI-EdgeMesa-N-AI-Series-630x161.png 630w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1280px) 100vw, 1280px" /></a><figcaption id="caption-attachment-100090" class="wp-caption-text">The MSI EdgeMesa N AI+ Series RTX Spark compact desktop.</figcaption></figure>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">New compact desktops are available for purchase from ASUS, Dell and MSI, joining the RTX Spark family of laptops available today. Mini PCs from Acer, GIGABYTE and HP are coming soon, along with the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/surface/devices/surface-rtx-spark-dev-box"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Microsoft Surface RTX Spark Dev Box</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<h2><b>From Local Agents to AI Creation</b></h2>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA’s Ian Buck kicked off the company’s week at SC26 in Chicago on Monday with new tools for AI agents, software that can plan experiments, run code and check results on researchers’ own systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In NVIDIA’s special address, Buck announced</span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-introduces-nemotron-for-science-to-accelerate-discoveries-with-agents"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron for Science</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the Rubin HPC GPU and an</span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-expands-vera-cpu-platform-to-accelerate-scientific-discovery"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">expanded Vera CPU platform for high-performance computing</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. He also detailed a new collaboration in quantum computing with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-collaboration-with-ibm-and-fermi-national-accelerator-laboratory-to-launch-quantum-classical-codesign-platform-plus-error-correction-breakthrough-to-advance-practical-quantum-computing"><span style="font-weight: 400;">IBM and Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“The frontier of computing and the new scientific stack is going to be simulation, as always, but combined with AI and the future of quantum,” said Buck, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing at NVIDIA. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck opened the talk by looking back to his first public talk on CUDA, at an SC06 tutorial in Tampa, Florida, 20 years ago. NVIDIA now offers more than 1,000</span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/20-years-cuda/"> <span style="font-weight: 400;">CUDA-X libraries</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, he said.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="YouTube video player" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/uJG53ligJt4?si=Ai6y_W4HHpG3-AhX" width="560" height="315" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen="allowfullscreen"></iframe></p>
<p><b>Open Models for Science Agents</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“[Agents] have been just a game changer in how we interface with our supercomputers, with our devices, our instruments and how we can do the research,” Buck said.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Simulation remains the foundation of scientific computing, “which will always be on the top,” Buck said, with AI, quantum and now agents working alongside it.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron for Science combines the Nemotron 3.5 Super VL open model with a new open chemistry dataset and post-training recipes for life sciences and chemistry, Buck explained. Researchers can run it locally on a DGX Spark, a workstation or their own supercomputer.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At Argonne National Laboratory, Buck said, an agent built with the toolkit took a single prompt, planned and tested hypotheses, chose which simulations to run and identified two new binding sites.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“You own your data. You own the model,” Buck said.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck highlighted other agents already in use:</span></p>
<ul>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>GROMACS:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> An agent from KTH Royal Institute of Technology repairs broken GROMACS setups on its own. In one overnight run on a single DGX Spark, it fixed 81% of test cases.</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>SETI Institute:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> At the Allen Telescope Array, an agent took a one-line prompt to plot a methanol line. Buck explained that it then retuned for Doppler shift on its own [NGC 869 vs. 896, check slide].</span></li>
<li style="font-weight: 400;" aria-level="1"><b>Nsight AI Porting Agent:</b><span style="font-weight: 400;"> NVIDIA’s new agent ported a routine from the Weather Research and Forecasting (WRF) model to GPUs, Buck said, speeding it up more than 5x using about 150 million tokens.</span></li>
</ul>
<p><b>CPUs for the Agent Loop</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Agents lean on CPUs to run tool calls between model steps, Buck explained.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“That’s exactly what agents need,” he said. “They need a CPU that can run, evaluate, run the tool, perform the operations, gather the results.” </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">DKRZ, the German Climate Computing Center, has selected Vera CPUs for its HLRE-5 supercomputer, expected in early 2028. Buck said it joins Vera systems already announced at Texas Advanced Computing Center. </span></p>
<p><b>A New GPU for Science</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rubin HPC is a new GPU in the Vera Rubin platform, built for science, engineering, analytics and AI, Buck said. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It supports precisions from FP64 to FP4, adds sparse-data acceleration and connects over seventh-generation NVIDIA NVLink. With more than 50,000 CUDA cores, it has about three times as many as Hopper, Buck said. It is expected in 2028.</span></p>
<p><b>Quantum Error Correction</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck said a simulation on Jülich’s JUPITER supercomputer showed that a technique called magic state cultivation can cut the operations needed for one logical step tenfold. The work is being added to CUDA-Q.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA, IBM and Fermilab announced plans today for a quantum computing co-design platform to support error-correction and applications research. NVIDIA and IBM are pairing NVIDIA DGX B200 systems with IBM quantum computers, including those powered by IBM Quantum Nighthawk. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck also introduced a new breakthrough for quantum decoding. The goal, Buck explained, is to cut decoding latency from about 300 microseconds to 10. A new CUDA-Q decoding algorithm, TCBP-JOBS, cuts error-correction research computation from 12 weeks to 45 minutes, he said.</span></p>
<p><b>Networking</b></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Buck also showed one of the first deployments of </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/lambda-photonics/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Photonics co-packaged optics switches, at Lambda</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. He said they are 15x more reliable on bit errors and link flaps. </span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At SC26, an annual supercomputing conference taking place this week in Chicago, NVIDIA is introducing the DGX Spark HPC Developer Kit for researchers, academics and developers.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The kit is a four-node cluster of </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/dgx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DGX Spark systems</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, each connected directly to its two neighbors in a ring, with an NVIDIA HPC SDK installation playbook coming soon. Researchers get a place at their desk to run multi-node scientific workloads, AI models and agents, instead of waiting for time on a shared GPU cluster. The kit puts the GB10 Grace Blackwell Superchip on researchers’ desks. </span></p>
<h2><b>A Supercomputing Lab on Any Desk</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The DGX Spark Developer Kit changes how researchers work.</span></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">It scales from one system, or node, to four — linked directly to one another over NVIDIA ConnectX-7 networking, with no network switch to buy or set up. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At four nodes, it provides 512GB of combined memory and 16TB of NVMe storage that provides researchers more capacity to distribute models and scientific workloads across the cluster. With NVFP4, researchers can deploy models with up to 700B parameters locally across the four DGX Spark clusters, such as DeepSeek V4.1.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With that much power at the desk, researchers with a fixed compute and server allocation no longer have to wait in a queue for every job. Teams can develop and debug on their own, saving server hours for production runs and keeping unpublished results, and proprietary datasets on premises.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The playbook for NVIDIA HPC SDK is coming soon, bringing GPU-accelerated C, C++ and Fortran compilers, libraries and message passing interface tools to the DGX Spark cluster — the same development tools used on NVIDIA-powered supercomputers. Teams can build and test scientific applications across four nodes, check how computation and data are distributed, and debug communication issues before scaling to larger systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The kit brings scientific simulation and AI models and agents to the desktop, with demonstrated four-system workloads including LAMMPS with Kokkos for materials simulation and NAMD for molecular dynamics. The broader DGX Spark community has validated GROMACS for molecular dynamics, OpenFOAM for fluid dynamics, WRF for weather modeling and NVIDIA NIM microservices such as OpenFold3 and Boltz-2 for predicting biomolecular structures.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Running on DGX Spark, new open dataset and post-training recipes for </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-introduces-nemotron-for-science-to-accelerate-discoveries-with-agents"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nemotron 3.5 Super VL</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> help researchers adapt the model for life sciences and chemistry, enabling secure scientific workflows on infrastructure organizations control.</span></p>
<h2><b>What Researchers Can Do With It</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The kit opens up new possibilities for researchers. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Researchers can explore agent-assisted workflows that prepare simulation inputs, launch runs and summarize results, while the scientists validate the setup and conclusions.  </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Researchers can also train AI models that approximate slow simulations, then use them to refine wing designs toward minimum drag. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Before committing supercomputer time, a team can narrow a 10-million-cell simulation to the 50-500 dimensions that matter.</span></p>
<ul>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://www.kvantify.com/blog/nvidia-dgx-spark"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Kvantify</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using NVIDIA cuQuantum, and reports running tensor-network simulations of quantum-chemistry circuits with up to 70 qubits and 975 gates on a single DGX Spark, benchmarking circuits generated for penicillin ground-state energy calculations.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/case-studies/cyran-iit-delhi/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">CYRAN AI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, an IIT Delhi spin-out, uses DGX Spark and its PixelFlux library to accelerate JPEG 2000 decoding for large satellite images, supporting local geospatial analysis.</span></li>
<li><a target="_blank" href="https://openfold.io/blog-finetuning-openfold3.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">OpenFold</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> shows how researchers can fine-tune OpenFold3 on NVIDIA DGX Spark to improve protein-ligand structure predictions for drug discovery.</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.therubinlab.org/research"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rubin Lab at IGI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> at University of California, Berkeley is using a DGX Spark and NVIDIA Evo 2 model to predict bacterial traits directly from genome sequences by comparing embeddings from genomic and protein language models against the classical baselines. With no 11-hour cluster queue times, results arrived 15x sooner on DGX Spark.</span></li>
<li><span style="font-weight: 400;">The </span><a target="_blank" href="https://hartwig.cchem.berkeley.edu/publications/?topic=10"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Hartwig Group at UC Berkeley</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is using a DGX Spark to perform LoRA fine-tuning on the Nemotron 3 Super 120B-A12B model for chemistry machine learning, training a prediction head on experimental values. By running locally on the Spark using NVFP4 quantization, the lab successfully fit a 120B-class model on a single device and bypassed multi-day cluster queue times, allowing them to run continuous eight- to 24-hour training fits back-to-back.</span></li>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p>Deb Shoquist leads operations at NVIDIA, overseeing the supply chain and manufacturing behind its AI platforms. That description sums up the job, but it doesn’t begin to encompass what Shoquist has made possible over decades.</p>
<p>Shoquist, her team and their global network of manufacturing partners enable NVIDIA to introduce world-leading technologies year-round.</p>
<p>Much of the team’s success comes down to her deep relationships with some of the world’s most accomplished manufacturers, including TSMC, Foxconn, Wistron, SPIL, King Yuan Electronics (KYEC), Unimicron, Lumentum, Coherent and Amkor. TSMC has partnered with NVIDIA since 1998. SPIL and KYEC were assembling and testing its chips more than two decades ago.</p>
<p>For Shoquist, those relationships grow stronger through each challenge solved together on the factory floor, as every generation of technology demands more from the people and tools behind it.</p>
<p>Ask her about the global network she relies on, and she starts with the people. There’s the supplier in Taiwan so eager to get production moving that he calls her at 3 a.m. with a fix to the latest problem. There are the factory workers in Vietnam whose only ask of her are more tools to get more work done. She remembers the partners that supported NVIDIA when it was small, and the work they’ve done to help each other grow.</p>
<p>Shoquist meets with key suppliers quarterly and spends six weeks each year on the road visiting partners in Taiwan, China, Korea, Vietnam and the U.S., where conversations move from meeting rooms to factory floors.</p>
<p>Recently, she paused her travels to accept the <a target="_blank" href="https://designthesolution.org/lisa-su-woi-award/">2026 Dr. Lisa Su Woman of Innovation Award</a> — and to share how she finds the right people and companies to imagine and produce at massive scale technology that has never been built before.</p>

<p><strong>Q: How would you describe your job to someone outside NVIDIA?</strong></p>
<p>A: At the most basic level, our job is to convert designs into the physical world. Our design team creates a blueprint, and our job is to transition it into a reliable, high-performance supply chain.</p>
<p>A matchmaker is probably the best analogy. You have to find the partner that matches your ramp style and has the technology that can meet the needs of very precise designs. The supplier has to be financially strong enough to invest in our ramps. They have to be highly technical. And they have to be willing to do something that has never been done before.</p>
<p>If you don’t have all three, you can’t create products at the volume and speed that we need. You need those three legs, and in some cases, if you only have two, you have to invest in the company to make them a partner.</p>
<p><strong>Q: What’s an example of that in practice ?</strong></p>
<p>A: Amphenol’s cable cartridge is the most complex interconnect assembly they have ever built. When I recently visited the Vietnam factory, now in full production, I was struck by the level of precision required to build a product of this complexity.</p>
<p>In this large cable cartridge, there are 1,500 small cable legs. Each one requires a very precise assembly to yield a quality level of less than 1 DPPM, or defect part per million. Amphenol built over a hundred automated lines to produce this product at the speed and scale we require. That takes know-how and technology, but it also takes heart, willingness and dedication.</p>
<p>Creating anything of that complexity and precision with a supplier forges the partnership. The partners NVIDIA maintains are the ones who supported us when we were small, and yet are willing to scale with us today.</p>
<p><strong>Q: What changed as NVIDIA went from selling chips to building systems and platforms?</strong></p>
<p>A: When we were primarily a chip manufacturer, we were small, and the semiconductor industry was at scale. In that environment, it was about picking the best technology match for the products we envisioned.</p>
<p>As we became a data center systems company, we needed more partners. But we’ve also now entered into new technology areas where the scale of the supply base is still developing.</p>
<p>For example, our optical component partners have the technology and the willingness, but they don’t yet have the scale or capital to build fabs for the volume we need. To grow, we needed to support and invest, like we did with <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-and-coherent-announce-strategic-partnership-to-develop-optics-technology-to-scale-next-generation-data-center-architecture">Coherent</a> and <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-strategic-partnership-with-lumentum-to-develop-state-of-the-art-optics-technology">Lumentum</a>.</p>
<p>Beyond that, with products like CPO (co-packaged optics), we’re creating entirely new supply chains with scale partners that are new to the optics world. Leading the integration of talent across the supply chain is the key to driving this incredible technology to high volume quickly.</p>

<p><strong>Q: You clearly enjoy spending time with supply partners, and in particular on the factory floor. What have you learned there over time that can’t be learned anywhere else?</strong></p>
<p>A: Over 20 years, you meet many individuals key to the success of your supply. You meet not only the CEOs, but also the people that are running the production floor. These are the people that really know the problems of manufacturing.</p>
<p>If you don&#8217;t have a tight working relationship with the people deeper in the organization, then it’s hard to have an open dialogue about any challenges. But when you have tight relationships, they want to help you, and they feel comfortable enough telling you, “I need better technical support on the floor. I need more quality engineers. We need a better design. We need to automate this. We need more buffers of material.”</p>
<p>That’s what 20 years of working with our suppliers has taught us. It’s year after year of learning from people, getting to know people deeper in the organization and creating the trust where they can tell us what we need to do to be successful with them.</p>
<p><strong>Q: Demand for NVIDIA technology is already massive and growing. Your job is to make sure NVIDIA’s volume can keep up. How do you make it happen?</strong></p>
<p>A: It goes back to relationships. When you decide that you&#8217;re going to push a line hard to increase volume, you have to have the relationships. You have to know their processes in detail. You have to know their people in detail, and they have to trust you. Only then are you in a position to ask them for special favors, because you can do it surgically, and your ask is credible and reasonable.</p>
<p>And when you’re successful together, they&#8217;re happy, you&#8217;re happy, and the appreciation for the partnership deepens. We all appreciate those that make us successful.</p>

<p><strong>Q: How do you prioritize resources across the supply chain?</strong></p>
<p>A: You need to first understand what the most critical components are, and protect and prioritize production around them. We need wafers like we need air. And the critical component is the TSMC chip. As it comes out of assembly every week, we decide what factory gets what chip.</p>
<p>Every week, we look at factory performance. If a particular factory yield is low, if their time of ownership is not at the level we want, they don’t get more chips that week.</p>
<p>We keep track of every chip, from the identification number on the die all the way to the customer invoice. If you want to be our supplier, you have to support that traceability process. With that process, we took the time it takes from chip out at TSMC to system out at Foxconn and Wistron from five to two weeks when supply and demand requires it.</p>
<p>Think of the supply chain as a large circulatory system with pathways everywhere — and when you need to accelerate any branch of it, you have the power to do that. We have the capability to do that. It’s empowering and exciting.</p>

<p><strong>Q: What’s an example of a time things didn’t go as planned? How did you, the team and your partners find a way forward?</strong></p>
<p>A: We were getting ready to go to volume production with Foxconn and Wistron for the compute board of <a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/dgx-gb200/">NVIDIA</a><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/dgx-gb200/"> GB200</a>. The last step in the process was a test managed by an automated handler — a completely new approach for us, developed to meet the demands of high-volume throughput for these complicated products. During the initial ramp, the throughput was significantly below plan.</p>
<p>The operations director of Taiwan manufacturing made the call that to protect the product and shipments, he needed to disable the entire process and return to manual test loading. Our manufacturers backed him without question and brought in the necessary manpower to meet the immediate needs, literally hundreds of operators. That kept production moving, but would not solve the throughput requirement of full production.</p>
<p>As with any operations problem, the root cause had many elements to it: test tray assembly, test tray design, automation hardware design, automation control software, product yield. From all viewpoints, it was a difficult problem to resolve quickly.</p>
<p>That’s when we executed the NVIDIA swarm. Teams from NVIDIA design engineering, operations industrial, and product and test engineering, as well as from Foxconn automation engineering, flooded the line with talent to redesign and reactivate the line before the scale ramp hit.</p>
<p>This is not an unusual occurrence when there is a problem in operations. All teams at NVIDIA are street fighters — action-oriented, and know no organizational boundaries. We say that “the mission is the boss,” but we also live “the mission is the boss.”</p>
<p><strong>Q: What makes this work so exciting for you?</strong></p>
<p>A: Operations is a very empowering place to be at NVIDIA. Our team is designing and implementing changes to the supply chain constantly to meet the strong demand of our new products. The challenges are understandably high, but we’re always given the resources we need to be successful. The only thing between our goals and our success is our own creativity. That is exhilarating.</p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To train an AI model, thousands of GPUs have to work together, requiring a network that’s fast, efficient and resilient.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"><br />
</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lambda</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> is tackling the network piece of the puzzle with NVIDIA Photonics co-packaged optics (CPO) switches. The cloud provider is using the switches to connect </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/gb300-nvl72/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA GB300 NVL72</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> racks with more than 10,000 GPUs, delivering lower power, higher resiliency and longer mean time between interruptions (MTBI) to an AI factory at scale.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“Networking has to keep pace with the demands of increasingly large AI clusters,” </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">said Dave Ward, president of cloud services at Lambda.</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> “Bringing co-packaged optics into a deployment of this scale is an important step toward building the infrastructure needed to support the next generation of AI.” </span></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href=" https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In the company’s tests using the switches, network latency — the time needed for moving data — dropped by about 4% at rack scale and by up to about 10% at pod scale. Across rack, scalable unit and pod configurations, the network maintains the same throughput as conventional pluggable optics.</span></p>

<h2><strong>Delivering Power Efficiency and Resiliency at Scale</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Improving power efficiency and resiliency matters because the network sits at the center of AI factory productivity. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In a large training job, one interrupted link can stall thousands of GPUs. With agentic inference, one network disruption can introduce cascading latency effects on many agents. Every interruption costs time, and every watt spent on networking is a watt not available for compute. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Photonics addresses both power efficiency and resiliency. By replacing pluggable transceivers with co-packaged optical engines on the switch package, it delivers up to 5x better power efficiency and up to 15x higher reliability than conventional pluggable optics. It also uses 4x fewer lasers, which means fewer components that can fail.</span></p>
<h2><strong>Cooler Optics, Replaceable Light Sources Increase Resiliency</strong></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lambda’s deployment uses NVIDIA Photonics switches, each with 144 ports running at 800Gb/s. Liquid cooling carries heat away from the switch chip and optical engines. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The company reports a median optics temperature of 36°C, compared with 51°C for conventional pluggable optics, and its tests find more stable signal integrity.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Photonics also brings the electrical-to-optical conversion closer to the switch chip. Each switch has 18 removable external light sources, which supply light to the optical engines. Optical engines sit alongside the chip on the same package and convert electrical signals into light that carries data over fiber between parts of the cluster. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Technicians can replace the light-source modules in the field, unlike traditional pluggable optics, which require replacing the entire module if a laser fails.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Co-packaging shortens the electrical path from inches to millimeters, which reduces signal loss. It also removes the need for retimer chips, cutting both power draw and the number of parts in the signal path.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Quantum processor deployments at supercomputing centers have grown nearly sevenfold over the past five years, with nearly 30 supercomputing centers around the world now in the process of deploying over 50 quantum processors. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Many of these centers use </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/software/slurm/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Slurm</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to run scientific supercomputing workloads, so expanding its support for quantum processors builds on familiar workflows and tools, enabling computational chemists, nuclear physicists and materials scientists to bring quantum computing into their research domains without friction.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In a new release, Slurm builds on previous work with IBM, Pasqal and the wider quantum computing community to allow QPU builders to create a single interface to their hardware for every supercomputing center, through a simple file. This makes it easier than ever for HPC centers and QPU builders to integrate and serve their hardware. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The expansion is already being adopted by </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Alice &amp; Bob</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">IBM</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">IQM</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Pasqal</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Quantinuum</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">MQSC</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and supports the widely used</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">QRMI</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">QDMI</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">interfaces.</span></p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href=" https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<h2><b>Beating the Bottleneck</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Until now, integrating a QPU into a supercomputing center meant a new software challenge for every new QPU or supercomputing center. By building on existing quantum-HPC interfaces such as QRMI and QDMI, the Slurm expansion allows QPU builders to reuse their integrations across all their deployments. It also means that supercomputing centers can add new quantum processors to their existing scheduling and resource-management workflows without developing bespoke QPU-specific solutions.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">For quantum computing to advance science, it needs to be used by scientists. Now scientists can run quantum-classical applications developed with platforms such as NVIDIA CUDA-Q with Slurm coordinating access to all the processing they need.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Slurm 26.11 will be available later this month. </span></p>
<h2><b>Orchestrating Resources in an Open Quantum-GPU Platform</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The <a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/integrating-qpus-into-supercomputing-centers-using-slurm-hres-plugins/">Slurm expansion</a> adds a community-developed orchestration layer to NVIDIA’s open platform for quantum-GPU supercomputing, which centers around the CUDA-Q open platform for hybrid quantum-classical computing. At SC26, new releases expand all aspects of the platform:</span></p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q QEC</strong> allows researchers developing quantum error correction to rapidly prototype and test new ideas, including with a new <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/blogs/blog/2026/11/16/cudaq-qec-decoding-server/">decoder server</a>. A new TCBP-JOBS decoder is 13,000x faster than the industry standard.</p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q</strong> gets an expanded layer of fault-tolerant primitives that application builders can use to realistically compile their ideas. Fermilab has already used CUDA-Q to perform studies in three weeks that would normally have taken five months.</p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q Algorithms </strong>provides reusable components for builders of quantum applications, including new capabilities in helping design the loading data into quantum circuits and tools specifically for chemistry and materials applications</p>
<p><strong>CUDA-Q Pulse</strong> gives quantum hardware builders a layer for <a target="_blank" href="https://nvidia.github.io/cuda-quantum/blogs/blog/2026/11/16/introducing-cudaq-pulse/">shaping the ‘pulses’</a> needed to control qubits on a QPU, including tools to GPU accelerate their generation.</p>
<p>The quantum ecosystem around the world is continuing to build with this platform. Quantum Motion integrated its QUICHE chemistry framework with CUDA-Q. Altera and Oxford Quantum Circuits designed an FPGA-based controller connecting qubit control and readout to GPUs through NVQLink.</p>
<p>At supercomputing scale, QunaSys used cuQuantum and 1,024 NVIDIA Hopper GPUs on ABCI-Q to simulate more than 300 million quantum operations, reaching chemical accuracy for a four-hydrogen-atom model. <span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA researchers used </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">JUPITER</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> to simulate 104 qubits under realistic noise, testing fault-tolerant designs before they’re built.</span></p>

<h2><b>NVIDIA Sweeps MLPerf Training v6.1</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">AI agents are now everywhere, writing code, running business workflows, answering customer questions and, as this week’s </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">SC26 conference highlights</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, advancing scientific research. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Faster training means smarter agents can reach users sooner, drug discovery models can keep pace with new lab data and fraud-detection models can adapt to new threats faster.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">MLPerf Training measures how fast a system can train a range of AI models to a target level of accuracy. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The NVIDIA platform was the only one with results on every benchmark this round, spanning large language models, image generation, recommender systems and reinforcement learning post-training. In its debut, Vera Rubin NVL72 delivered up to 2x more performance per GPU than the highly optimized NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 system — early results that show the pace of generation-over-generation innovation. </span></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA also demonstrated performance at scale. On the DeepSeek-V3 benchmark, Vera Rubin NVL72 scaled from 256 to 1,024 GPUs with about 97% efficiency: quadrupling the GPUs cut time to train from 16 minutes to about four. </span></p>

<h2><b>NVIDIA Vera Rubin Leads Agentic Inference on MLPerf Endpoints</b></h2>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Each of those steps generates tokens. Because AI data centers are limited by power, the more tokens a system can generate each second for every watt of power, the more agents it can serve and the more value it creates. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">MLPerf Endpoints tests systems on real agentic coding and enterprise workflows, and checks that the agents still complete their tasks correctly. NVIDIA Vera Rubin leads across all of its agentic benchmarks.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With expanded </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/technologies/cuda-x/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CUDA-X</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> libraries, including cuPhoton, cuPotential and cuSpa, researchers can sift through vast amounts of data to accelerate research, from astrophysics and materials science to single-cell biology.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The speedups are helping scientists analyze larger datasets and model more complex systems.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The new libraries give AI agents the tools to help researchers make discoveries faster than ever.</span></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href=" https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>

<h2><b>Bringing Black Holes Into Focus</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Seeing a black hole takes a telescope the size of Earth. The Event Horizon Telescope links radio dishes worldwide, generating petabytes of data that can take months to process.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">With NVIDIA cuPhoton.xBeam, a module within the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/high-performance-computing/ai-for-science/cuphoton/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">cuPhoton Toolkit</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, black hole observations from the Event Horizon Telescope can now be correlated orders of magnitude faster than ever. Analysis that once took over a month now takes eight minutes.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">By combining global radio dishes into Earth-sized aperture, cuPhoton delivers unprecedented astronomical imaging resolution. cuPhoton successfully correlated observations of powerful black hole jets J1924 and 3C279 in the night sky — making it a reality to reconstruct a Messier 87* black hole galaxy in real time. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The entire pipeline, from data loading and processing to visualization, runs on distributed multi-GPU systems or a compact </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/products/workstations/dgx-spark/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA DGX Spark</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> system, helping scientists study how gas spirals inward under a black hole’s powerful gravitational pull.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="NVIDIA cuPhoton Brings Real-Time Black Hole Science to GPUs" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/E3Hy-WRJbXE?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<h2><b>Steering Fusion Simulations</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Sustaining plasma at millions of degrees is essential to achieving stable, continuous fusion energy. But simulating its behavior is costly: on petascale systems, a single time step can take days, with simulations running for weeks. Experiments also generate data faster than traditional pipelines can process it, often leaving scientists waiting until experiments end to analyze results.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Pacific Fusion researchers used NVIDIA cuPhoton.xLens module to extract insights while plasma simulations run, helping them visualize what’s happening as results emerge. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The NVIDIA cuPhoton.xLens module extracts insights while plasma simulations run, helping researchers understand what’s happening as results emerge. Researchers can couple the entire pipeline with an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">-powered agent running locally on the same system, enabling interactive analysis that can help steer simulations in real time to inform fusion reactor target and chamber design.</span></p>
<h2><b>Exploring Potential Medicines</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Molecular simulations help scientists evaluate drug designs before costly laboratory experiments. NVIDIA cuPotential, built on work by researchers at </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Simons Foundation’s Flatiron Institute</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">D. E. Shaw Research</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, accelerates existing simulation software by up to 3x while improving calculation stability and accuracy. It expedites synthetic data creation for generative AI models, finding previously unknown molecular binding sites and calculating the strength of molecular interactions.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA is working with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Schrödinger</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and the </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">AMBER</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">NAMD</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">GROMACS</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">LAMMPS</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Open Molecular Software Foundation</span> <span style="font-weight: 400;">communities to bring cuPotential acceleration to molecular dynamics engines and workflows.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Cadence</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Fathom Therapeutics</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Topos Bio</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Genesis Molecular AI</span><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Terray Therapeutics </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">are adopting it for drug discovery.</span></p>
<h2><b>Accelerating Materials Research</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Daviator</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, a collection of batched GPU kernels for the widely used Vienna Ab initio Simulation Package, is coming to </span><a target="_blank" href="http://developer.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-x-libraries/alchemi"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA ALCHEMI</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> — delivering quantum-mechanical calculations up to 22x faster and at up to 75% lower cost. With Daviator, researchers building simulation models can now use GPU acceleration across atomistic systems of all sizes to generate the millions of calculations of atomic interactions needed for model training and inference, including for small systems that previously ran faster on CPUs.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the NVIDIA DAQIRI networking library connects a high-speed electron microscopy detector to GPU processing, moving analysis into the experiment itself. Researchers can identify useful signals, discover previously unknown regions of interest and decide what to measure next while measurements are being taken. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The workflow supports atomic-resolution materials studies that can distinguish elements, laying the groundwork for future automated experiments.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="Real-Time Discovery Starts With NVIDIA DAQIRI" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/uGZO6I-CJtM?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<h2><b>Revealing Biology at Scale</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The newly open source cuSpa accelerates cell polygon calculations, enabling developers to generate massive spatial graphs for next-generation patient and cancer models at </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, with a 900x speedup compared with using CPUs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Codeveloped with </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">scverse</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, the rapids-singlecell framework accelerates single-cell analysis — and is now available in a single, agent-ready container. Using the tool, </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/therna-biosciences-releases-chronos-the-data-engine-behind-its-ai-rna-biologist-302878554.html?tc=eml_cleartime"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Therna Biosciences</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> processed data 200x faster for therapeutic design. </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/10x-genomics-announces-sentira-a-new-computational-platform-to-turn-complex-biological-data-into-experimental-conclusions-302892240.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">10x Genomics</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> integrated rapids-singlecell in its new Sentira platform, helping academic researchers, biopharma teams and foundation model builders accelerate the path from large-scale spatial data to biological insights. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And the NVIDIA GPU-accelerated HMMER library, also open source, accelerates the annotation of predicted protein sequences, supporting data preparation for biological foundation models. At </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Basecamp Research</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, benchmark searches ran up to 84x faster than with standard HMMER, reducing the time required to annotate the large biological datasets used to train the company’s therapeutic design models.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">This week at SC26, NVIDIA is marking two decades of </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.nvidia.com/cuda"><span style="font-weight: 400;">CUDA</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> by honoring the researchers, developers and engineers who helped make GPU computing a foundation for modern science and AI. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">CUDA’s 20th anniversary presents an occasion to reflect across computing eras, from the people who wrote the very first CUDA programs to the AI developers building on their work today. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At the platform’s core is this developer community whose experiments, tools and persistence expanded what accelerated computing could do.</span></p>
<p><iframe loading="lazy" title="20 Years of CUDA: Honoring the Architects of Progress" width="1200" height="675" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/v2I4vqz87fk?feature=oembed" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe></p>
<h2><b>From SC06 to SC26</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA </span><a target="_blank" href="https://web.archive.org/web/20220706095459/https://www.clustermonkey.net/Conference-Reports/sc06-jeffs-blog-day-1-sunday/Print.html"><span style="font-weight: 400;">announced CUDA in November 2006</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and brought it to the scientific community at SC06, giving scientists and developers a familiar, high-level programming model for using GPUs. It fundamentally extended accelerated computing beyond graphics use cases to accelerate demanding applications for science, simulation and more.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In the two decades since, developers have used CUDA to advance </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.kva.se/en/news/the-nobel-prize-in-chemistry-2024/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nobel Prize-winning AI research</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, accelerate </span><a target="_blank" href="https://docs.nersc.gov/performance/readiness/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">scientific applications on supercomputers</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> and support </span><a target="_blank" href="https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-quadro-powers-all-five-academy-award-nominees-for-best-visual-effects"><span style="font-weight: 400;">visual effects in Oscar-winning films</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Today, </span><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/gtc-2026-news/#cuda"><span style="font-weight: 400;">more than 11 million developers</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> build on CUDA across the computing stack. </span></p>

<h2><b>Honoring the Architects of Progress</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Now, at SC26, NVIDIA is looking back at some of the CUDA luminaries who’ve spearheaded breakthroughs in science and AI:</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">At the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, biophysicist Klaus Schulten’s team helped connect GPU computing with molecular biology through </span><a target="_blank" href="https://developer.download.nvidia.com/compute/academia/whitepapers/Achievement2014_UIUC.pdf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Nanoscale Molecular Dynamics and Visual Molecular Dynamics</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, tools for molecular simulation and visualization. In 2013, researchers at Illinois, the University of Pittsburgh and Vanderbilt University used CUDA-accelerated tools to help resolve the HIV capsid’s atomic structure. They combined experiments with simulations on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.ks.uiuc.edu/Highlights/?highlight=2013-06&amp;section=2013"><span style="font-weight: 400;">Blue Waters</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, one of the world’s most powerful supercomputers at the time.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">In a critical breakthrough in AI, University of Toronto researchers Alex Krizhevsky, Ilya Sutskever and Geoffrey Hinton used CUDA to </span><a target="_blank" href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c399862d3b9d6b76c8436e924a68c45b-Paper.pdf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">train AlexNet</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> on just two NVIDIA GPUs. Their image-recognition system </span><a target="_blank" href="https://papers.nips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/file/c399862d3b9d6b76c8436e924a68c45b-Paper.pdf"><span style="font-weight: 400;">won the 2012 ImageNet competition</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">, helping demonstrate the potential of GPU computing for deep learning. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That connection between computing and scientific discovery is seen with AlphaFold, which predicts protein structures. Demis Hassabis and John Jumper shared the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for protein structure prediction. Their team’s </span><a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold"><span style="font-weight: 400;">publicly released AlphaFold 2 software</span></a> <a target="_blank" href="https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphafold/blob/main/docker/Dockerfile"><span style="font-weight: 400;">uses CUDA for GPU execution</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;">.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These are just a few of many examples that show how one generation’s work creates opportunities for the next. With 1,000+ NVIDIA CUDA-X libraries, developers are now reshaping industries and giving machines the power to see, learn and reason — from discovering new medicines and designing next-generation factories to advancing autonomous vehicles, modeling financial markets and unlocking quantum computing — and more.</span></p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<div id="bsf_rt_marker"></div><p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Announced today at SC26, NVIDIA Rubin HPC is a new GPU in the </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/technologies/rubin/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA Vera Rubin</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> platform designed to accelerate simulation and data processing for science, engineering and analytics workflows. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">As agents help scientists and engineers explore more candidate materials, molecules and designs, they rely on simulations to test those ideas and guide their next steps. Rubin HPC will accelerate those simulations, shortening the path from exploration to scientific discovery and industrial design.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">The Rubin HPC GPU features a record number of CUDA cores — more than 50,000 on a single GPU — as well as a sparse data acceleration capability that keeps those cores fed with data, and accelerates GPU-to-GPU connectivity with seventh-generation </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/nvlink/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA NVLink</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> interconnect technology.</span></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<div style="margin: 0 !important; padding: 0 !important; text-align: center; line-height: 1;"><a style="display: inline-flex; align-items: center; justify-content: center; margin: 0 !important; padding: 10px !important; background-color: #76b900; color: #ffffff !important; font-family: 'NVIDIA Sans', Arial, sans-serif; font-size: 16px; font-weight: bold; line-height: 1 !important; text-align: center; text-decoration: none !important; white-space: nowrap; vertical-align: top;" href=" https://blogs.nvidia.com/sc26-news/">Read All NVIDIA Announcements From SC26</a></div>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Scientists and engineers rely on simulation to understand complex systems before committing to experiments or physical prototypes. Working with agents, these teams can explore more candidate materials, molecules and designs. This increases demand for the GPU-accelerated simulations needed to evaluate those ideas and guide next steps.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">“For agents tackling science and engineering, faster simulation means faster discovery,” said </span><span style="font-weight: 400;">Ian Buck</span><span style="font-weight: 400;">, vice president of hyperscale and high-performance computing at NVIDIA. “Rubin HPC accelerates the simulations agents use to test ideas and guide their next steps, helping scientists and engineers explore more possibilities and bring breakthroughs closer.” </span></p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-99923" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807.jpg" alt="" width="1600" height="900" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807.jpg 1600w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807-960x540.jpg 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807-1280x720.jpg 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807-1536x864.jpg 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807-1290x725.jpg 1290w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807-630x354.jpg 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807-300x169.jpg 300w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/hpc-press-hpc-nvl4-tray-rack-1600x900-5802807-400x225.jpg 400w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1600px) 100vw, 1600px" /></p>
<h2><b>Turning Faster Computation Into Faster Discovery</b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">Across climate models, engineering simulations, genomic analysis and financial risk, scientific computing workloads take a similar shape: an application reaches into an immense dataset, gathers a handful of scattered values, calculates a result and repeats this on an enormous scale. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">To help address the challenge, Rubin HPC will offer a balanced design, scaling data movement and compute together rather than increasing compute alone. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">That balance is what optimizes Rubin HPC for scientific computing. It keeps the GPU’s record number of CUDA cores supplied with data, so they spend their time computing rather than waiting. This accelerates recurring patterns of every simulation workload — from sparse matrix operations to stencil computations to sorting and reductions. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">These patterns are prevalent across data analytics, science and engineering workloads, which is why a purpose-built GPU delivering the broadest acceleration can help unlock cross-industry breakthroughs.</span></p>
<p><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/technologies/cuda-x/"><span style="font-weight: 400;">NVIDIA CUDA-X</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400;"> libraries offer a growing set of optimized implementations of complex algorithms for agents to use, including those most relevant to simulation and analytics. Used together with Rubin HPC, the libraries can give agents the fastest glide path to reasoning outcomes. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400;">And faster agent-invoked simulations mean faster feedback — shortening the loop of hypothesis, simulation and refinement, so researchers and engineers can choose next steps sooner and point lab work at the most promising candidates.</span></p>
<p><i><span style="font-weight: 400;">Rubin HPC is expected to be available in 2028.</span></i></p>

<p><span style="font-weight: 400">As AI advances healthcare, surgery is a high-stakes, highly regulated setting for its use — and one with significant opportunity for efficiency gains. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Only about 10% of time spent in the operating room is clinical. The rest is devoted to scheduling, turnover and documentation. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">With the help of NVIDIA technology, Proximie is introducing AI into operating room (OR) logistics so that clinicians can focus on taking care of patients.</span></p>
<h3><b>A New Vantage Point</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Proximie is using </span><a target="_blank" href="https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos3/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA Cosmos 3</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, the frontier open world foundation model family for physical AI, to power a platform used in more than 700 ORs today. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Trained on a decade of data, it captures real-time audio, video and device signals from a close-up view of the surgery as well as a wide view of the whole room. The comprehensive view offers context held by no single person in the room, whether the surgeon, scrub nurse or coordinator.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Agentic notifications and voice AI tools can call the next patient when a room is ready, document alerts and capture practitioner notes. To protect privacy, Proximie’s models blur identifying details, such as faces, tattoos and onscreen text before footage leaves the OR. Surgeons control the blurring to ensure that recordings used for training still show what trainees need to see.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Across health systems where Proximie’s technology has been applied, the platform has enabled clinical teams to handle 8% more cases, turn over the OR 45% faster and start procedures on time 26% more often. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Next, the company hopes to turn its technology into an OR copilot that assists with staging equipment ahead of time, estimating time remaining and running implant safety checks. Proximie will also use NVIDIA Cosmos 3 to generate realistic synthetic scenes grounded in real ORs, so models can learn from the rarest, most critical scenarios. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">The goal is to create a room that runs itself, so practitioners can spend more time with patients and dedicate less time to documentation and preparation. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Proximie is working with UC San Diego Health to build an AI model that learns from many kinds of surgical data at once using NVIDIA open models. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Before being trained on Proximie’s data, Cosmos 3 could already identify 80% of key equipment in the OR with high accuracy. Training it further on Proximie’s footage made the model more accurate and smaller, so several models can run at once on a single computer in the OR alongside tools to protect patient privacy.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Proximie is also among the first participants in</span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.open-h.org/"> <span style="font-weight: 400">Open-H-Hospital</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, an NVIDIA-led, community-driven open initiative that brings real hospital operation data together with simulation data to train the next generation of hospital AI and robotics. </span></p>

<p><em>Monday, Nov. 16, 8:00 a.m. PT <b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/how-ai-is-helping-care-teams/#sword-health"><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></a></b></em></p>
<h2 id="sword-health" class="wp-block-heading"><b>Sword Brings Dependable AI Guidance to Nearly a Million Members</b><b></b></h2>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="alignnone size-medium wp-image-100080" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-960x444.png" alt="" width="960" height="444" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-960x444.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-1680x777.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-1280x592.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-1536x710.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-scaled.png 2048w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/sword-image-nvidia-page_-1-630x291.png 630w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 960px) 100vw, 960px" /></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Many patients have faced the challenge of deciding whether a new symptom warrants a clinician visit or can be safely managed with the right adjustments at home. Sword, a leading AI health company, built its Phoenix AI health partner to help make that call for nearly a million people and over 2,500 organizations. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">After Sword trained an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/foundation-models/nemotron/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA Nemotron</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400"> open model to its clinical standards for musculoskeletal (MSK) care, Phoenix’s performance improved significantly — making the right call in 10 test runs nearly 80% of the time, up from under 50%. Sword estimates that using an open model costs 5-6x less than frontier models, saving hospitals and patients valuable resources. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“Clinicians don’t trust an AI teammate because it’s right once,” said Megan Hill, head of clinical specialists at Sword. “We trust it because it makes the same call on Tuesday at 1 a.m. that it made on Saturday at 7 p.m.” </span></p>
<h3><b>An AI Teammate That Knows Its Role</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Phoenix works alongside each person’s dedicated clinician. For every message, it chooses to answer or bring the clinician in — and decides whether to speak or stay quiet to respect the clinician and patient’s conversation.</span></p>
<p><img loading="lazy" decoding="async" class="aligncenter size-large wp-image-100034" src="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1680x1371.png" alt="" width="1200" height="979" srcset="https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1680x1371.png 1680w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-960x783.png 960w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1280x1044.png 1280w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-1536x1253.png 1536w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2-630x514.png 630w, https://blogs.nvidia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Sword2.png 1999w" sizes="auto, (max-width: 1200px) 100vw, 1200px" /></p>
<p>&nbsp;</p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Using an </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/hgx/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA HGX B300 </span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">system</span><span style="font-weight: 400">, Sword trained Phoenix with reinforcement learning — a technique where the model practices, gets graded and repeats what earns high marks — on de-identified care conversations. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">An AI judge scores every conversation on seven criteria defined by physical therapists, nurses and dietitians on Sword’s team, including tone, clinical boundaries and respect for the clinician’s role. If Phoenix crosses a clinical boundary, the safety score automatically drops to the minimum. </span></p>
<h3><b>Consistent, Repeatable Results</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">To test the model, Sword runs Phoenix on situations where the right call is predetermined, running each situation 10 times. With NVIDIA Nemotron, first-try accuracy for MSK scenarios rose from 78% to 93%, while in women’s health, the model’s accuracy rose from 79% to 87% correct on the first try and from 50% to 72% correct on all 10 runs. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Sword can retrain Phoenix whenever it identifies a problem, and member data is kept private. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">This work builds on </span><a target="_blank" href="https://sword.com/newsroom/dawn-1-intelligence-for-mental-health?_storyblok=228222201085072&amp;_storyblok_c=articleNewsroom&amp;_storyblok_version=&amp;_storyblok_lang=default&amp;_storyblok_release=0&amp;_storyblok_rl=1791501236075&amp;_storyblok_tk%5Bspace_id%5D=325797&amp;_storyblok_tk%5Btimestamp%5D=1791501235&amp;_storyblok_tk%5Btoken%5D=57dc446845064c27023fd59adf34f6b03a2edc7a"><span style="font-weight: 400">Dawn 1</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">, Sword’s previous model focused on mental health. By pairing open models with rigorous clinical oversight, Sword is proving that AI can expand access to care, extend clinician time for the patients who need it most, and support safe, reliable care at global scale.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400">Learn more about </span><a target="_blank" href="https://sword.com/research/reliable-care-with-nvidia-open-models"><span style="font-weight: 400">Sword</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400">.</span></em></p>
<hr />
<p><em>Friday, Nov. 13, 5:00 a.m. PT <b><a href="https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/how-ai-is-helping-care-teams/#dyania-health"><img src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/72x72/1f517.png" alt="🔗" class="wp-smiley" style="height: 1em; max-height: 1em;" /></a></b></em></p>
<h2 id="dyania-health" class="wp-block-heading"><b>Dyania Health Uses AI to Turn Decades of Patient Records Into Better Care </b></h2>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Hospitals hold decades of patient history, most of it written in free text,</span><span style="font-weight: 400"> as physicians&#8217; notes, operative reports, imaging and pathology reads and discharge summaries. To understand one patient’s full story, a clinician would need to read through years of charts</span><span style="font-weight: 400">. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Dyania Health</span><span style="font-weight: 400">, a New Jersey-based clinical AI company, built Synapsis AI to do just that. Synapsis AI is a clinical reasoning engine: It works across every note, report and lab result in a patient&#8217;s record, connecting findings that may be years apart to answer clinical questions the way a specialist reviewing the chart would. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">The output is a set of clinical signals, each traceable to its source in the record, that tells care teams which patients need attention and why. Clinicians review the evidence and make every decision. </span></p>
<h3><b>Keeping Patient Data Inside the Hospital Walls</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Synapsis AI runs on NVIDIA-accelerated computing inside a health system’s own firewall. The entire health system’s patient records can be processed onsite, so patient data is never shared and never leaves the hospital. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Processing records this way takes chart review off the desks of clinical teams and lets them spend that time with patients. </span><span style="font-weight: 400">But Synapsis AI still requires clinician input — it’s an assistive tool that surfaces evidence from medical records that a clinician must still confirm and act on. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Dyania builds and trains its clinical models with </span><a target="_blank" href="https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/ai-data-science/products/nemo/"><span style="font-weight: 400">NVIDIA NeMo</span></a><span style="font-weight: 400"> open libraries, then uses NVIDIA TensorRT and TensorRT-LLM to optimize them to run faster and more efficiently on the hospital’s own GPU servers. </span></p>
<h3><b>Synapsis AI in the Real World</b></h3>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Cleveland Clinic</span><span style="font-weight: 400"> used Synapsis AI to support a study of more than 7,800 aortic valve replacement patients between 2005 and 2021. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">Synapsis AI reasoned across each patient’s record, including every echocardiogram added in the years after surgery, and used this information to build a predictive model of valve deterioration and reintervention risk at the three-, five- and ten-year mark after a patient undergoes an aortic valve replacement procedure. The AI platform completed chart review across the full cohort that would have taken clinicians years to complete by hand. </span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">“Using technology to bring together and analyze information that can help us predict the duration of implanted heart valves is a promising step towards enhanced outcomes and more personalized care for patients,” said </span><span style="font-weight: 400">Dr. Lars Svensson</span><span style="font-weight: 400">, chief of the Heart, Vascular &amp; Thoracic Institute at Cleveland Clinic.</span></p>
<p><span style="font-weight: 400">The same approach can be applied to any healthcare challenge where the answer sits inside the medical record, such as identifying patients eligible for clinical trials, closing care gaps in cardiology and oncology, and populating clinical registries.</span></p>
<p><em><span style="font-weight: 400">Learn more about <a target="_blank" href="https://dyaniahealth.com/">Dyania Health</a>.</span></em></p>
